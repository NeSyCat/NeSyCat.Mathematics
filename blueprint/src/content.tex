\section{NeSyCat}

This blueprint is \textbf{version 0} of the NeSyCat library reference
document: the itemized mathematical content of the NeSy26 paper
[NeSy26], restated here as a standing library organized by
mathematical structure rather than by paper section. The Lean
formalization lives in the flat \texttt{NeSyCat.\textit{name}}
namespace. Items accumulate \texttt{\textbackslash lean} and
\texttt{\textbackslash leanok} marks as formalization proceeds; a
\texttt{\textbackslash leanok} mark is only ever added once the named
declaration(s) exist and are fully proved (no \texttt{sorry}), and
\texttt{scripts/blueprint.sh}'s decl-check gate --- which
\texttt{\#check}s every \texttt{\textbackslash lean}-cited name against
the built library --- keeps the \texttt{\textbackslash lean} marks
honest. An item's absence of either mark means exactly what it always
meant: no Lean proof exists for it yet.

Two editorial principles govern every item:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Natural generality.} Each item is stated at its natural
  mathematical level (for example, the weight-monad construction
  $\MS{S}$ for any semiring $S$, not assumed commutative), with the
  paper's concrete cases (Boolean, mass, log semirings; the $[0,1]$
  readout) presented as instances. This document is the canonical
  informal reference of the NeSyCat library in its own right, not a
  restatement graded against the source text: items are stated at
  their natural generality in the library's own voice, and a cited
  source's narrower case then appears in-item as an instance or
  corollary, noted as such rather than as a divergence declared
  relative to the source.
\item \textbf{Provenance.} Every item ends with a bracketed pointer
  into the source, e.g.\ \texttt{[NeSy26, App.~A]}; the pointer credits
  where the mathematical content originates, and is checked for
  accuracy against what the source actually contains, not for whether
  the source's own statement matches this document's verbatim.
\end{itemize}

Notation is simplified from the source where this costs nothing
mathematically: plain function and vector notation replaces the
source's Dirac bra-ket notation throughout, and composition of maps is
written in the usual right-to-left order $g \circ f$ (``first $f$,
then $g$'') rather than the source's left-to-right sequencing notation
for the same composite. Neither change is a content divergence, so
neither is separately declared below.

The library has six chapters: semiring weight monads; truth spaces and
lifted connectives; the three-layers trichotomy; syntax and monadic
semantics; batching; and the bridges/normalization theory connecting
the probability and tensor readings.

\paragraph{Sources and provenance.} This blueprint is the canonical
informal reference document of the NeSyCat library: it is not a
restatement of some other standing target document, and library items
are faithful to it directly. Bracketed tags such as
\texttt{[NeSy26, App.~A]} cite the NeSy26 paper (``NeSyCat Torch'', a
NeSy 2026 submission) as the mathematical source for the corresponding
item. The text this version~0 was authored and verified against is
preserved at git ref \texttt{779b0be} of this repository
(sha256~\texttt{845babfad280736117b84f10c66f698654a09d75cc3b4772de035a151ad62462}
of \texttt{target/nesy26-paper.tex} as it stood at that commit); source
documents are not vendored into this repository as standing copies ---
where a later authoring or verification pass needs a pinned text, it is
pinned by git ref at that moment and the ref is recorded here and in
the campaign ledger.

\begin{remark}[Connective conventions]
\label{rem:connective-conventions}
Table 3 of the source distinguishes two families of binary connective,
kept visually and notationally apart throughout this document:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lattice connectives} $\vee,\wedge$, with bounds
  $\bot,\top$ (Definition~\ref{def:lattice-semiring},
  Definition~\ref{def:order-family}).
\item \textbf{Monoid connectives on truth values}, at the
  \emph{logical/truth-value} level only: $\parr$ (or-like, unit
  $\dzero$) and $\AndC$ (and-like, unit $\done$). These name the
  \emph{lifted} family of Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} once
  it is applied to truth spaces (Chapter~\ref{sec:truth-spaces}
  onward): the lifted-family prose, the three-layers linear/lifted
  layer, and the De~Morgan/negation laws.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Scope rule.} $\parr,\AndC$ are reserved for the
logical/truth-value level. The \emph{semiring/categorical} level ---
the weight-semiring operations inside $\MS{S}$ itself, the $\bind$
formula of Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}, and every Chapter~1
item built directly on it (Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad},
Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws},
Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-commutative},
Lemma~\ref{lem:log-iso}) --- keeps $\oplus,\otimes$ throughout. This
separation is deliberate: it is exactly what keeps the logical
connectives from clashing, on the page, with the categorical ones they
are built out of.

Implications and negations are always written indexed by the
connective they belong to: $\impc{\vee},\impc{\wedge},\impc{\parr},
\impc{\AndC}$ and $\negc{\vee},\negc{\wedge},\negc{\parr},\negc{\AndC}$;
the optional primitive involution (when one is assumed rather than
derived) is plain $\neg$. Linear-logic notation such as $\multimap$,
$\setminus$, or $\ominus$ for these connectives is never used here.

\emph{One documented deviation from linear logic}: our $\AndC$ is the
\emph{multiplicative} conjunction (linear logic's $\&$ is the
\emph{additive} conjunction, which here is $\wedge$). This is
deliberate and is recorded once, here, rather than at each site the
symbol is used.

These symbols are LaTeX \texttt{\textbackslash newcommand}s collected
in the symbol registry of \texttt{blueprint/src/macros/common.tex}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The four constants]
\label{rem:four-constants}
Four constants recur at the logical/truth-value level, and are never
conflated even where they happen to coincide: the lattice bounds
$\bot,\top$ (Definition~\ref{def:order-family}) and the monoid units
$\dzero,\done$ of $\parr,\AndC$
(Remark~\ref{rem:connective-conventions}). A coincidence between a
bound and a unit (e.g.\ on a normalized, mass-one row) is recorded as
a \emph{named axiom} in the UnitBounds style when it is used --- never
folded into the notation itself, so that $\bot=\dzero$ or
$\top=\done$ always reads as a substantive fact about a particular
instance, not a definitional identification.
\end{remark}

\section{Semiring weight monads}
\label{sec:semiring-monads}

\begin{definition}[Lattice-semiring]
\label{def:lattice-semiring}
\lean{NeSyCat.LatSRng, NeSyCat.LatCSRng, NeSyCat.BLatSRng, NeSyCat.BLatCSRng, NeSyCat.BoolW.instBLatCSRng, NeSyCat.instLatCSRngNNReal, NeSyCat.instLatCSRngLogS}
\leanok
A \textbf{lattice-semiring} ($\mathrm{LatSRng}$) is a semiring
$(S, \oplus, \otimes, 0, 1)$ equipped
with a lattice order $\le$ on the carrier $S$: a meet $\wedge$ and join
$\vee$ exist for every pair of elements, and $\oplus$, $\otimes$ are
each monotone in \emph{both} arguments with respect to $\le$. A
\textbf{commutative lattice-semiring} ($\mathrm{LatCSRng}$) is a
lattice-semiring whose $\otimes$ is commutative; a \textbf{bounded
lattice-semiring} ($\mathrm{BLatSRng}$, resp.\ its commutative
refinement $\mathrm{BLatCSRng}$) additionally carries an in-carrier
$\bot$ and $\top$.

The library's three running instances are all commutative
($\mathrm{LatCSRng}$); only the Boolean one is bounded
($\mathrm{BLatCSRng}$), since mass and log have no in-carrier $\top$
without adjoining $\infty$ externally:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Boolean} (bounded): carrier $\BoolS = \{0,1\}$, $\bot{=}0$,
  $\top{=}1$, $\vee{=}\mathtt{or}$, $\wedge{=}\mathtt{and}$, $0{=}0$,
  $1{=}1$, $\oplus{=}\mathtt{or}$, $\otimes{=}\mathtt{and}$;
\item \emph{mass} (unbounded above): carrier $\MassS$, $\bot{=}0$,
  $\top{=}\infty$ only after adjoining $\infty$ externally,
  $\vee{=}\max$, $\wedge{=}\min$, $0{=}0$, $1{=}1$, $\oplus{=}{+}$,
  $\otimes{=}\cdot$;
\item \emph{log} (unbounded): carrier $\LogS$, $\bot{=}{-\infty}$,
  $\top{=}\infty$ only after adjoining $\infty$ externally,
  $\vee{=}\max$, $\wedge{=}\min$, $0{=}{-\infty}$,
  $1{=}0$, $\oplus{=}\lse$, $\otimes{=}{+}$.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Not} an instance: the probability row $(\ProbS, p{+}q{-}pq,
\cdot)$ carried by $\max,\min$ as its lattice --- see
Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring}.
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why the noncommutative generality]
\label{rem:lattice-semiring-generality}
Plain (possibly noncommutative, unbounded) semirings need no separate
name of their own in Definition~\ref{def:lattice-semiring} --- they are
just semirings. On the general (possibly noncommutative) case:
$\oplus$'s two directions of monotonicity are never independent data
--- a semiring's addition is unconditionally commutative, so
right-monotonicity always follows from left-monotonicity --- but
$\otimes$'s two directions genuinely are, absent commutativity of
$\otimes$; they collapse to one only in the commutative refinement
$\mathrm{LatCSRng}$. This library states the lattice-semiring notion at
this natural level of generality, splitting it the way Mathlib splits
\texttt{Semiring}/\texttt{CommSemiring}; [NeSy26, App.~A] states the
commutative case throughout, matching the library's three running
instances.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Probability is not a semiring]
\label{lem:prob-not-semiring}
$(\ProbS, {+}{-}\cdot, \cdot, 0, 1)$, with $p \oplus q := p+q-pq$ and
$p \otimes q := pq$, is \emph{not} a semiring: $\otimes$ does not
distribute over $\oplus$ in general. Concretely, at
$p=q=r=\tfrac12$,
\[
  p \otimes (q \oplus r) = \tfrac12\bigl(\tfrac12+\tfrac12-\tfrac14\bigr)
    = \tfrac38,
  \qquad
  (p\otimes q) \oplus (p \otimes r)
    = \tfrac14 \oplus \tfrac14
    = \tfrac14+\tfrac14-\tfrac1{16} = \tfrac{7}{16},
\]
and $\tfrac38 \ne \tfrac{7}{16}$. Consequently $\ProbS$ with this
$(\oplus,\otimes)$ is not a row of Definition~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}:
it is the mass-one slice of the \emph{lifted} Boolean De~Morgan family
of Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} (a pair of commutative
monoids, not a semiring), read out as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-prob-readout}, not a semiring in its own right.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring}
\lean{NeSyCat.prob_not_semiring}
\leanok
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The displayed numerical instance witnesses the failure
of distributivity directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Semiring monad $\MS{S}$]
\label{def:semiring-monad}
\lean{NeSyCat.MS, NeSyCat.ret, NeSyCat.bind, NeSyCat.bind_apply}
\leanok
Let $S = (S,\oplus,\otimes,0,1)$ be a semiring. The \textbf{semiring
monad} $\MS{S}$ sends a set $X$ to the set $\MS{S}X$ of finitely
supported functions $f \colon X \to S$, with
\[
  \Ret(x) := \delta_x, \quad \delta_x(x')=1 \text{ if } x'=x,\ 0
  \text{ otherwise},
  \qquad
  (f \bind k)(y) := \bigoplus_{x \in X} f(x) \otimes k(x)(y)
\]
for $k \colon X \to \MS{S}Y$.
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Reading $\MS{S}$ and its bind]
\label{rem:semiring-monad-reading}
$\MS{S}X$ reads as the set of finitely-supported $S$-weighted formal
combinations of elements of $X$; the sum defining $f \bind k$ in
Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} is finite since $f$ is finitely
supported, and is taken in the left-to-right order $f(x)\otimes
k(x)(y)$ throughout --- no step needs $\otimes$ to commute.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Bind is matrix multiplication]
\label{cor:bind-matrix-mult}
$\bind$ of Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} is matrix multiplication
over $S$: $f \colon X \to S$ and $k \colon X \to \MS{S}Y$ correspond to
a row vector and an $X \times Y$ matrix over $S$, and $(f \bind k)(y) =
\bigoplus_x f(x) \otimes k(x)(y)$ is exactly the $y$-th entry of their
product.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from unfolding the $\bind$ formula of
Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} against the matrix-product formula
$(fK)(y) = \bigoplus_x f(x)\otimes K(x,y)$ at $K(x,y) := k(x)(y)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Semiring monad laws]
\label{thm:semiring-monad-laws}
\lean{NeSyCat.ret_bind, NeSyCat.bind_ret, NeSyCat.bind_assoc}
\leanok
For every semiring $S$, $(\MS{S}, \Ret, \bind)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} is a monad on $\mathrm{Set}$:
commutativity and associativity of $\oplus$ give associativity and
commutativity of independent binds, associativity of $\otimes$ gives
associativity of chained binds, and distributivity of $\otimes$ over
$\oplus$ gives associativity of $\bind$ itself.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Unfolding $\bind$ from Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}, the
left and right unit laws are the two distributivity-free identities
$\delta_x \bind k = k(x)$ and $f \bind \Ret = f$ (each uses only a unit
law of $S$), and associativity of $\bind$ expands, on both sides, to a
double sum over $S$ that is rearranged into the other order by
associativity and commutativity of $\oplus$ together with
associativity of $\otimes$ and distributivity of $\otimes$ over
$\oplus$ --- commutativity of $\otimes$ is never invoked.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Semiring monad laws, informally]
\label{rem:semiring-monad-laws-gloss}
Informally, in Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws}: commutativity and
associativity of $\oplus$ is ``addition marginalises'' (independent
binds commute and associate), and associativity of $\otimes$ is
``multiplication chains'' (chained binds associate); none of this needs
$\otimes$ to commute.
\uses{thm:semiring-monad-laws}
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Semiring monad commutativity]
\label{thm:semiring-monad-commutative}
\lean{NeSyCat.dstL, NeSyCat.dstR, NeSyCat.dstL_apply, NeSyCat.dstR_apply, NeSyCat.dst_comm_iff, NeSyCat.dst_comm}
\leanok
For a semiring $S$, define the two double-strength (interchange) maps
$\dstL, \dstR \colon \MS{S}X \times \MS{S}Y \to \MS{S}(X\times Y)$,
binding the two arguments in either order,
\[
  \dstL(f,g) := f \bind \lambda x.\, g \bind \lambda y.\, \Ret(x,y),
  \qquad
  \dstR(f,g) := g \bind \lambda y.\, f \bind \lambda x.\, \Ret(x,y),
\]
which unfold pointwise to $\dstL(f,g)(x,y) = f(x)\otimes g(y)$ and
$\dstR(f,g)(x,y) = g(y)\otimes f(x)$. Then $\MS{S}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws}) is a \emph{commutative} monad
--- $\dstL(f,g) = \dstR(f,g)$ for every $X,Y,f,g$ --- \emph{if and only
if} $S$ itself is commutative.
\uses{def:semiring-monad, thm:semiring-monad-laws}
[NeSy26, App.~A; Coumans--Jacobs, Lem.~23]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Forward: $\otimes$ commuting makes the two pointwise
formulas $f(x)\otimes g(y)$ and $g(y)\otimes f(x)$ agree directly.
Converse: testing $\dstL=\dstR$ at $X=Y=\{*\}$ (a one-point set) against
the two point masses $\delta_a,\delta_b$ ($a,b\in S$) evaluates both
sides at $(*,*)$ to $a\otimes b$, resp.\ $b\otimes a$, recovering
$a\otimes b = b\otimes a$ for arbitrary $a,b$ --- so no weaker
hypothesis than full commutativity of $\otimes$ suffices; even the
single instance of $\dstL=\dstR$ at this one-point set already forces
it.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where commutativity is first needed]
\label{rem:monad-commutativity-role}
Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-commutative} is exactly where
$\otimes$-commutativity is first needed in this chapter: not for the
monad laws of Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws} (which hold for a
general, possibly noncommutative semiring), but for the monad's own
commutativity in the technical, monad-theoretic sense above.
Downstream, it is this monad-level commutativity --- not the bare
monad laws --- that the lifted connectives of
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} (built by binding several
arguments and pushing forward along a Boolean operation, order
immaterial only once the binds commute) and the order-irrelevance of
independent binds in the $\Do$-notation semantics of
Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation} both rely on.
\uses{thm:semiring-monad-commutative, def:lifted-connective, def:kleisli-interpretation}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Mass-one closure]
\label{lem:dist-closure}
\lean{NeSyCat.ret_mass_one, NeSyCat.bind_mass_one}
\leanok
For the mass semiring monad $\Tmon = \MS{\MassS}$
(Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}), $\Ret$ has mass one, and
$\bind$ of mass-one families has mass one: if $\sum_x \rho(x) = 1$ and
$\sum_y k(x)(y) = 1$ for every $x$, then $\sum_y (\rho \bind k)(y) = 1$.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$\Ret(x)=\delta_x$ has mass
$\sum_{x'}\delta_x(x')=1$, and if $\sum_x\rho(x)=1$ and
$\sum_y k(x)(y)=1$ for every $x$ then
\[
  \sum_y (\rho \bind k)(y) = \sum_y\sum_x \rho(x)\,k(x)(y)
  = \sum_x \rho(x) \sum_y k(x)(y) = \sum_x \rho(x)\cdot 1 = 1.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Distribution monad]
\label{def:dist-monad}
\lean{NeSyCat.Dist, NeSyCat.Dist.pure, NeSyCat.Dist.bind}
\leanok
The \textbf{distribution monad} is the mass-one submonad of the mass
semiring monad,
\[
  \Dmon X := \{\, \rho \in \MS{\MassS} X \mid \textstyle\sum_x \rho(x) = 1 \,\}
  \;\subset\; \MS{\MassS} X \;=\; \Tmon X,
\]
well-defined by Lemma~\ref{lem:dist-closure}.
\uses{lem:dist-closure, def:semiring-monad}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Not $\MS{\ProbS}$]
\label{rem:dist-not-prob-semiring}
$\Dmon$ is not $\MS{\ProbS}$: $\ProbS$ is not a semiring
(Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring}), so $\MS{\ProbS}$ is not even
defined by Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}.
\uses{def:dist-monad, lem:prob-not-semiring}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Log isomorphism]
\label{lem:log-iso}
\lean{NeSyCat.LogS, NeSyCat.logEquiv, NeSyCat.logRingEquiv, NeSyCat.logTensEquiv, NeSyCat.toLogTens_ret, NeSyCat.toLogTens_bind}
\leanok
$\log \colon (\MassS, +, \cdot, 0, 1) \to (\LogS, \lse, +, -\infty, 0)$
is a semiring isomorphism ($e^{\lse(u,v)} = e^u+e^v$), and it
transports the monad $\MS{\MassS} = \Tmon$ pointwise (applying $\log$
to every weight) to an isomorphism of monads
$\Tmon \cong \LTmon = \MS{\LogS}$.
\uses{def:semiring-monad, thm:semiring-monad-laws}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$\log$ is a bijection $\MassS \to \LogS$ carrying $+
\mapsto \lse$ (definition of $\lse$) and $\cdot \mapsto +$ (log of a
product), hence a semiring isomorphism by
Definition~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}; applying it coordinatewise to a
finitely supported weight function commutes with $\Ret$ (both are the
indicator/point mass) and with $\bind$ (a monad-law computation as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws}, transported along the semiring
isomorphism), so it is a monad isomorphism $\MS{\MassS} \cong
\MS{\LogS}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Truth-value structures}
\label{sec:truth-structures}

The lifted connective family of a truth space (Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}, Chapter~\ref{sec:truth-spaces} onward) is \emph{not} a semiring (Lemma~\ref{lem:copying-fails}, Theorem~\ref{thm:three-layers}): it is a bounded lattice carrying two monoids. This section isolates that shape abstractly, under names from the library's own naming scheme (\textbf{BLat2Mon} and its refinements), develops the linearity and De~Morgan laws such a structure can carry, and locates each running row of [NeSy26, App.~A] --- Boolean, probability, mass, log --- inside the resulting hierarchy. The connective and constant conventions of Remark~\ref{rem:connective-conventions} and Remark~\ref{rem:four-constants} are assumed throughout, not re-explained.

\begin{definition}[Two-monoid bounded lattice]
\label{def:blat2mon}
A \textbf{BLat2Mon} is a set carrying a bounded lattice
$(\wedge,\vee,\bot,\top)$ together with two monoid structures
$(\parr,\dzero)$ and $(\AndC,\done)$; unlike a lattice-semiring
(Definition~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}), no distributivity of any kind
is assumed between the three structures. A \textbf{BLat2CMon} is a
BLat2Mon whose two monoids $(\parr,\dzero)$ and $(\AndC,\done)$ are
both commutative.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring}
Distilled from [NeSy26, App.~A] (the lifted-connective family);
vocabulary from linear logic [Girard 1987].
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Linear two-monoid lattice]
\label{def:lin-blat2mon}
A \textbf{LinBLat2Mon} is a BLat2Mon (Definition~\ref{def:blat2mon}) in
which $\AndC$ preserves finite joins in each argument and $\parr$
preserves finite meets in each argument:
\begin{itemize}
\item binary: $p \AndC (q \vee r) = (p \AndC q) \vee (p \AndC r)$ and
  $(q \vee r) \AndC p = (q \AndC p) \vee (r \AndC p)$; dually
  $p \parr (q \wedge r) = (p \parr q) \wedge (p \parr r)$ and
  $(q \wedge r) \parr p = (q \parr p) \wedge (r \parr p)$;
\item nullary: $p \AndC \bot = \bot = \bot \AndC p$ (annihilation) and
  $p \parr \top = \top = \top \parr p$ (absorption).
\end{itemize}
A \textbf{LinBLat2CMon} is a commutative one (a BLat2CMon satisfying
these laws), in which case the one-sided version of each law suffices.
\uses{def:blat2mon}
These are exactly the four linear distributions of linear logic
($\otimes$ over $\oplus$, $\parr$ over $\&$, together with their
units), transported along the dictionary of
Remark~\ref{rem:ll-dictionary}; [Girard 1987].
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Linearity gives monotonicity]
\label{lem:lin-monotone}
In a LinBLat2Mon (Definition~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon}), $\AndC$ and
$\parr$ are monotone in each argument.
\uses{def:lin-blat2mon}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $p \le q$ then $q = p \vee q$, so
$q \AndC r = (p \vee q) \AndC r = (p \AndC r) \vee (q \AndC r) \ge
p \AndC r$; the other argument, and $\parr$ (via $p = p \wedge q$ and
meet-preservation), are dual.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lax dual preservations]
\label{lem:lin-lax-duals}
In a LinBLat2Mon, the dual preservations hold laxly:
$p \AndC (q \wedge r) \le (p \AndC q) \wedge (p \AndC r)$ and
$(p \parr q) \vee (p \parr r) \le p \parr (q \vee r)$, and likewise in
the other argument.
\uses{lem:lin-monotone}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$q \wedge r \le q$ and $q \wedge r \le r$, so monotonicity
(Lemma~\ref{lem:lin-monotone}) gives $p \AndC (q \wedge r) \le p \AndC q$
and $\le p \AndC r$, hence $\le$ their meet; dually for $\parr$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[De Morgan structure]
\label{def:dm-structure}
A \textbf{DM structure} on a BLat2Mon (Definition~\ref{def:blat2mon})
is a map $\neg$ satisfying (i) $\neg\neg p = p$; (ii)
$p \le q \Rightarrow \neg q \le \neg p$; (iii)
$\neg(p \AndC q) = \neg q \parr \neg p$ (in the commutative case, (iii)
reads $\neg p \parr \neg q$).
\uses{def:blat2mon}
Linear negation [Girard 1987], axiomatized minimally; the
presentations are compared in Proposition~\ref{prop:dm-presentations}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Negation is an order anti-automorphism]
\label{lem:dm-lattice-laws}
For a DM structure (Definition~\ref{def:dm-structure}),
$\neg(p \wedge q) = \neg p \vee \neg q$,
$\neg(p \vee q) = \neg p \wedge \neg q$, $\neg\bot = \top$,
$\neg\top = \bot$.
\uses{def:dm-structure}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By (i) and (ii), $\neg$ is an order-reversing bijection, and an
order-reversing bijection carries meets to joins, joins to meets, and
each bound to the other.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The dual De Morgan law]
\label{lem:dm-dual-law}
For a DM structure, $\neg(p \parr q) = \neg q \AndC \neg p$.
\uses{def:dm-structure}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Applying (iii) at $\neg q, \neg p$ and using (i):
$\neg(\neg q \AndC \neg p) = \neg\neg p \parr \neg\neg q = p \parr q$;
applying $\neg$ to both sides and using (i) again gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Negation swaps the monoid units]
\label{lem:dm-unit-swap}
For a DM structure, $\neg\done = \dzero$ and $\neg\dzero = \done$.
\uses{def:dm-structure}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every $p$, by (iii) and (i):
$\neg\done \parr p = \neg\done \parr \neg\neg p = \neg(\neg p \AndC \done)
= \neg\neg p = p$, and symmetrically
$p \parr \neg\done = \neg(\done \AndC \neg p) = p$; so $\neg\done$ is a
two-sided $\parr$-unit, and monoid units are unique, giving
$\neg\done = \dzero$. Applying (i): $\neg\dzero = \neg\neg\done =
\done$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Presentations of De Morgan structure]
\label{prop:dm-presentations}
On a BLat2Mon, let $\neg$ satisfy (i) $\neg\neg p = p$ and (iii)
$\neg(p \AndC q) = \neg q \parr \neg p$. Then the following are
equivalent, and each yields the full De~Morgan calculus of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:dm-lattice-laws}, \ref{lem:dm-dual-law} and
\ref{lem:dm-unit-swap}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\neg$ is antitone;
\item[(b)] $\neg(p \wedge q) = \neg p \vee \neg q$ for all $p, q$;
\item[(c)] the full list: both lattice De~Morgan pairs, both monoid
  De~Morgan laws, bound swap, unit swap, and antitonicity.
\end{enumerate}
\uses{lem:dm-lattice-laws, lem:dm-dual-law, lem:dm-unit-swap}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a)$\Rightarrow$(c) is Lemmas~\ref{lem:dm-lattice-laws},
\ref{lem:dm-dual-law} and \ref{lem:dm-unit-swap}. (c)$\Rightarrow$(b)
is immediate. (b)$\Rightarrow$(a): if $p \le q$ then $p = p \wedge q$,
so $\neg p = \neg(p \wedge q) = \neg p \vee \neg q \ge \neg q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Negation maps invertibles to invertibles]
\label{lem:dm-maps-units}
For a DM structure, if $p$ is $\AndC$-invertible then $\neg p$ is
$\parr$-invertible.
\uses{def:dm-structure, lem:dm-unit-swap}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $p \AndC q = \done = q \AndC p$. Applying $\neg$ and (iii):
$\neg q \parr \neg p = \neg\done = \dzero$ and, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:dm-unit-swap}, $\neg p \parr \neg q = \dzero$, so
$\neg q$ is a two-sided $\parr$-inverse of $\neg p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Unit-bound mixins]
\label{def:unit-bounds}
For a BLat2Mon (Definition~\ref{def:blat2mon}): \textbf{ZeroBot}
demands $\dzero = \bot$; \textbf{OneTop} demands $\done = \top$;
\textbf{UnitBounds} demands both, collapsing the four constants of
Remark~\ref{rem:four-constants} to two.
\uses{def:blat2mon}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Unit bounds via mixed absorption]
\label{lem:dualabsorb-decomposition}
In a LinBLat2Mon (Definition~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\done = \top$ iff $p \AndC q \le p \wedge q$ for all
  $p,q$ iff the mixed absorption $p \vee (p \AndC q) = p$ holds;
\item[(ii)] $\dzero = \bot$ iff $p \vee q \le p \parr q$ for all $p,q$
  iff the mixed absorption $p \wedge (p \parr q) = p$ holds.
\end{enumerate}
\uses{lem:lin-monotone, def:unit-bounds}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) If $\done = \top$: monotonicity gives
$p \AndC q \le p \AndC \top = p \AndC \done = p$ and likewise
$p \AndC q \le \top \AndC q = q$, hence $\le p \wedge q$. Conversely,
taking $p = \done$: $q = \done \AndC q \le \done \wedge q \le \done$
for all $q$, so $\top \le \done$. The absorption form is the standard
rewriting of $p \AndC q \le p$. (ii) is dual: if $\dzero = \bot$ then
$p = p \parr \dzero = p \parr \bot \le p \parr q$ and symmetrically,
hence $p \vee q \le p \parr q$; conversely $q = \dzero$ gives
$p \vee \dzero \le p$, so $\dzero \le p$ for all $p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The four-connective chain (MIX)]
\label{lem:mix}
In a LinBLat2Mon with UnitBounds (Definition~\ref{def:unit-bounds}),
the four binary connectives form a chain:
$p \AndC q \le p \wedge q \le p \vee q \le p \parr q$. In particular
MIX holds: $p \AndC q \le p \parr q$.
\uses{lem:dualabsorb-decomposition}
The MIX rule [Girard 1987]; here it factors through the lattice.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The outer inequalities are Lemma~\ref{lem:dualabsorb-decomposition}(i)
and (ii); the middle one is the lattice.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Chains are linear]
\label{thm:chain-lin}
Let $L$ be a lattice, not necessarily bounded, carrying two monoid
structures $(\parr,\dzero)$ and $(\AndC,\done)$ (a Lat2Mon), whose
order is a chain (total order), and suppose $\AndC$ and $\parr$ are
monotone in each argument. Then: (i) the four binary linear laws of
Definition~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon} hold; (ii) if $L$ is bounded and
UnitBounds (Definition~\ref{def:unit-bounds}) holds, the nullary laws
hold too, so the structure is a LinBLat2Mon.
\uses{def:lin-blat2mon, def:unit-bounds}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) On a chain, $q \vee r \in \{q, r\}$; say $q \le r$, so
$q \vee r = r$. Then $p \AndC (q \vee r) = p \AndC r$, while
monotonicity gives $p \AndC q \le p \AndC r$, so
$(p \AndC q) \vee (p \AndC r) = p \AndC r$ as well; the other argument
and the $\parr/\wedge$ laws are dual. (ii) Annihilation from OneTop:
$p \AndC \bot \le \top \AndC \bot = \done \AndC \bot = \bot$;
absorption from ZeroBot: $\top = \dzero \parr \top \le p \parr \top$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The Boolean row]
\label{lem:bool-truth-structure}
$\BoolS$ with its lattice $(\wedge,\vee,0,1)$, monoids
$(\parr,\dzero) = (\vee,0)$ and $(\AndC,\done) = (\wedge,1)$, and
$\neg$ is a LinBLat2CMon (Definition~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon}) with DM
structure (Definition~\ref{def:dm-structure}) and UnitBounds
(Definition~\ref{def:unit-bounds}); the connective pairs collapse,
$\AndC = \wedge$ and $\parr = \vee$ --- the idempotent case.
\uses{def:lin-blat2mon, def:dm-structure, def:unit-bounds,
thm:chain-lin}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A finite check (or: classical Boolean algebra); linearity via
Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-lin} on the two-element chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The probability row]
\label{lem:unit-interval-truth-structure}
$\ProbS = [0,1]$ with lattice $(\min,\max,0,1)$, monoids
$(\parr,\dzero) = (p+q-pq,\ 0)$ and $(\AndC,\done) = (pq,\ 1)$, and
$\neg p = 1-p$ is a LinBLat2CMon with DM structure and UnitBounds.
This is precisely the structure the non-semiring row of
Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring} does carry.
\uses{def:lin-blat2mon, def:dm-structure, def:unit-bounds,
thm:chain-lin, lem:prob-not-semiring}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Closure: $p+q-pq = 1-(1-p)(1-q) \in [0,1]$. Monoid laws: $\AndC$ is
real multiplication; $\parr$ is its conjugate under the involution
$x \mapsto 1-x$, hence associative, commutative, with unit
$1-1=0$. Both are monotone (partial derivatives $\ge 0$ on the square,
or directly: $q \le r$ gives $pq \le pr$ and
$p+q-pq = q(1-p)+p \le r(1-p)+p$), so Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-lin}
applies: Lin holds. DM: $1-x$ is involutive and antitone, and
$\neg(p \AndC q) = 1-pq = (1-p)+(1-q)-(1-p)(1-q) = \neg p \parr \neg q$.
UnitBounds: $\dzero = 0 = \bot$, $\done = 1 = \top$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No De Morgan structure on the mass row]
\label{thm:no-dm-mass}
On $\MassS = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ with its usual order there is no
involutive antitone map; hence no two-monoid structure over the mass
row carries a DM structure.
\uses{def:dm-structure}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\neg$ is involutive and antitone. For every $p$,
$\neg p \ge 0$, so applying $\neg$: $p = \neg\neg p \le \neg 0$. Thus
$\neg 0$ is a top element, but $\MassS$ has none
($\neg 0 + 1 > \neg 0$).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No De Morgan structure on the log row]
\label{thm:no-dm-log}
The same holds for $\LogS = \mathbb{R} \cup \{-\infty\}$: bottom
$-\infty$, no top, hence no involutive antitone map.
\uses{thm:no-dm-mass, lem:log-iso}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
As in Theorem~\ref{thm:no-dm-mass}, $\neg(-\infty)$ would be a top
element.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Outlook: completions]
\label{rem:dm-completions-outlook}
On the completed carriers $[0,\infty]$ and $[-\infty,\infty]$ the order
obstruction of Theorems~\ref{thm:no-dm-mass} and \ref{thm:no-dm-log}
vanishes, since the order becomes self-dual; whether the completed
rows carry DM structures is taken up together with the completion
instances in a later chapter.
\uses{thm:no-dm-mass, thm:no-dm-log}
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[The mass square is not linear]
\label{thm:square-not-lin}
On $\MassS^2$ with the order family (slot $0$ reversed,
Definition~\ref{def:order-family}) and the lifted operations of
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}, the slot swap
$\neg(w_0,w_1) = (w_1,w_0)$ is a DM structure
(Definition~\ref{def:dm-structure}) and both monoids are commutative,
but $\AndC$ is not monotone; hence, by Lemma~\ref{lem:lin-monotone},
the linear laws fail --- the square rows are BLat2CMon-with-DM
territory, not Lin.
\uses{def:order-family, lem:lifted-mass, def:dm-structure,
lem:lin-monotone}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
DM: the swap is involutive; it is antitone for the slot-0-reversed
order; and $\neg(w \AndC v) = (w_1v_1,\ w_0v_0 + w_0v_1 + w_1v_0) =
\neg w \parr \neg v$ by the formulas of Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}.
Non-monotonicity: take $p = (1,0)$, $r = (5,0)$, $q = (0,10)$. Then
$r \le q$ (slot 1: $0 \le 10$; slot 0 reversed: $5 \ge 0$), but
$p \AndC r = (5, 0)$ and $p \AndC q = (10, 0)$, and
$(5,0) \le (10,0)$ fails in the reversed slot ($5 \ge 10$ is false).
The mechanism is the cross term $w_0v_1$ in slot 0 of $\AndC$: raising
the \emph{for}-weight $v_1$ raises the \emph{against}-slot of the
output. Neither operation is monotone: $\parr$ fails dually, via the
cross term $w_1v_0$ in its slot 1 --- as it must, since an antitone
involution exchanging the two operations
(Lemma~\ref{lem:dm-dual-law}) makes $\parr$ monotone if and only if
$\AndC$ is. The counterexample persists unchanged in the bounded
completion $[0,\infty]^2$, so no bounded extension of this order
family is Lin either.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The linear-logic dictionary]
\label{rem:ll-dictionary}
Our $\vee$ is linear logic's additive disjunction $\oplus$; our
$\wedge$ is linear logic's additive conjunction (linear logic writes
it $\&$); our $\AndC$ is linear logic's \emph{multiplicative}
conjunction $\otimes$; our $\parr$ is linear logic's $\parr$. Units:
$\dzero$ is linear logic's $\bot$ (the $\parr$-unit), $\done$ is
linear logic's $1$ (the $\otimes$-unit), our $\bot$ is linear logic's
$0$, our $\top$ is linear logic's $\top$. This library's $\AndC$ is
deliberately a \emph{multiplicative} connective despite linear logic using
the same ampersand glyph for its additive one: the library keeps
[NeSy26]'s notation throughout, per
Remark~\ref{rem:connective-conventions}. Definition~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon}
is exactly linear logic's four linear distributions;
Lemma~\ref{lem:mix} is the MIX rule; linear implication and
$*$-autonomy sit at the S/R-implication boundary, deferred to the
residuation chapter. [Girard 1987]
\uses{def:lin-blat2mon, lem:mix}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Dioid pairings]
\label{rem:dioid-viterbi}
The mixed pairs are semirings where the pure pair is not:
$(\vee, \AndC)$ is an idempotent semiring (dioid) --- on $[0,1]$ the
Viterbi semiring --- and $(\wedge, \parr)$ is its dual; the pair
$(\parr, \AndC)$ fails distributivity in general (linear logic's
refusal to distribute $\otimes$ over $\parr$: only the four laws of
Definition~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon} survive), of which
Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring} is the concrete probability-row
instance.
\uses{def:lin-blat2mon, lem:prob-not-semiring}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Exponentials]
\label{rem:exponentials-log-iso}
Linear logic's exponentials $!/?$ are mirrored here by the exp/log
bridge between the mass and log rows; Lemma~\ref{lem:log-iso} is the
first ingredient, and the matter is taken up together with the
completion instances.
\uses{lem:log-iso}
\end{remark}

\section{Truth spaces and lifted connectives}
\label{sec:truth-spaces}

\begin{definition}[Truth space]
\label{def:truth-space}
For a semiring monad $\MS{S}$ (Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}) or
$\Idmon$, the \textbf{truth space} is $\MS{S}\BoolS$ (resp.\
$\Idmon\,\BoolS = \BoolS$): the monad applied to the Boolean carrier,
with Boolean value $0$ read as \emph{False} and $1$ as \emph{True}.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Generality of the truth-space definition]
\label{rem:truth-space-generality}
Definition~\ref{def:truth-space} is stated for a general semiring monad
$\MS{S}$; [NeSy26, App.~A] only tabulates the four instances $\Idmon,
\Dmon, \Tmon, \LTmon$ used in the paper.
\uses{def:truth-space}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Truth-space instances]
\label{lem:truth-space-instances}
The four truth spaces of the paper and their readouts:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Idmon\,\BoolS = \BoolS$, readout $=\BoolS$ itself;
\item $\Dmon\,\BoolS \cong \ProbS$ via $p \mapsto p(1)$, forgetting the
  redundant coefficient $p(0) = 1 - p(1)$ (the constraint
  $p(0)+p(1)=1$ of Definition~\ref{def:dist-monad});
\item $\Tmon\,\BoolS \cong \MassS^2$ via $w \mapsto (w(0), w(1))$,
  nothing forgotten (no mass-one constraint);
\item $\LTmon\,\BoolS \cong \LogS^2$ via $a \mapsto (a(0), a(1))$, the
  image of the $\Tmon$ readout under Lemma~\ref{lem:log-iso}.
\end{itemize}
\uses{def:truth-space, def:dist-monad, lem:log-iso}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\Idmon$ and $\Tmon,\LTmon$ readouts are immediate from
finite support; the $\Dmon$ readout uses the mass-one constraint of
Definition~\ref{def:dist-monad} to eliminate $p(0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Lifted connective]
\label{def:lifted-connective}
Let $* \colon \BoolS^n \to \BoolS$ be an $n$-ary Boolean operation and
$\MS{S}$ a semiring monad. The \textbf{lifted connective}
$*_{\MS{S}} \colon (\MS{S}\BoolS)^n \to \MS{S}\BoolS$ is
\[
  *_{\MS{S}}(w_1,\ldots,w_n)
  := w_1 \bind \lambda u_1.\, \cdots\, w_n \bind \lambda u_n.\,
     \Ret(*(u_1,\ldots,u_n)).
\]
\uses{def:truth-space}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Lifted connective via strength]
\label{cor:lifted-connective-strength}
$*_{\MS{S}}(w_1,\ldots,w_n)$ of Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}
equals $\mathrm{dst}(w_1,\ldots,w_n) \bind (\Ret \circ *)$, where
$\mathrm{dst} \colon (\MS{S}\BoolS)^n \to \MS{S}(\BoolS^n)$ is the
$n$-ary tensorial strength pairing the $n$ arguments into one element
of $\MS{S}(\BoolS^n)$.
\uses{def:lifted-connective}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Both sides bind each of $w_1,\ldots,w_n$ in turn against $\Ret \circ *$;
this is exactly the definition of the $n$-ary strength $\mathrm{dst}$
as the iterated bind pairing the arguments.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Generality of the lifted connective]
\label{rem:lifted-connective-generality}
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} is stated for a general $n$-ary
Boolean operation via the $n$-ary strength; [NeSy26, App.~A] spells out
only the binary cases $\wedge,\vee,\parr,\AndC$ and the unary $\neg$,
via the equivalent ``outer product then regroup along the operation''
description.
\uses{def:lifted-connective}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Lifted connectives, mass coordinates]
\label{lem:lifted-mass}
On $\Tmon\,\BoolS \cong \MassS^2$ (coordinates $(w_0,w_1)$, slot $0$
the weight against, slot $1$ the weight for), the lifted family of
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} is
\[
  w \AndC v = \bigl(w_0v_0+w_0v_1+w_1v_0,\ w_1v_1\bigr), \qquad
  w \parr v = \bigl(w_0v_0,\ w_0v_1+w_1v_0+w_1v_1\bigr), \qquad
  \neg w = (w_1, w_0),
\]
with units $\Ret(0) = (1,0)$ (the $\parr$-identity, i.e.\ certain
False) and $\Ret(1) = (0,1)$ (the $\AndC$-identity, i.e.\ certain
True). The order family
(Definition~\ref{def:order-family}) gives
$w \wedge v = (\max(w_0,v_0), \min(w_1,v_1))$,
$w \vee v = (\min(w_0,v_0), \max(w_1,v_1))$, with bounds
$\bot=(\infty,0)$, $\top=(0,\infty)$.
\uses{def:lifted-connective, def:order-family}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct expansion of Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}
at $*\in\{\wedge,\vee,\neg\}$ using $\bind$ of
Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} for $S=\MassS$: the entry with
$*(1,1)=1$ collects the product $w_1v_1$ into slot $1$ and the three
entries with $*=0$ sum into slot $0$ (dually for $\parr$); the unit
values are the (unique) identities of $\parr,\AndC$ found by solving
$e\parr w = w$, resp.\ $e\AndC w = w$, for all $w$.
\end{proof}

\uses{lem:lifted-mass}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Lifted connectives, log coordinates]
\label{lem:lifted-log}
On $\LTmon\,\BoolS \cong \LogS^2$ (coordinates $(a_0,a_1)$, the
$\log$-image of Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass} under
Lemma~\ref{lem:log-iso}, products becoming sums and sums becoming
$\lse$):
\[
  a \AndC b = \bigl(\lse(a_0{+}b_0,\,a_0{+}b_1,\,a_1{+}b_0),\ a_1{+}b_1\bigr),
  \qquad
  a \parr b = \bigl(a_0{+}b_0,\ \lse(a_0{+}b_1,\,a_1{+}b_0,\,a_1{+}b_1)\bigr),
\]
\[
  \neg a = (a_1,a_0), \qquad
  \Ret(0)=(0,-\infty), \qquad \Ret(1)=(-\infty,0),
\]
\[
  a \wedge b = (\max(a_0,b_0), \min(a_1,b_1)), \qquad
  a \vee b = (\min(a_0,b_0), \max(a_1,b_1)), \qquad
  \bot=(\infty,-\infty),\ \top=(-\infty,\infty).
\]
\uses{lem:lifted-mass, lem:log-iso}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:log-iso} coordinatewise to
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}: $\log$ of a product is a sum, $\log$ of a
sum is $\lse$ of the logs, and $\log$ is order-preserving so
$\max,\min$ pass through unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lifted connectives, the {$[0,1]$} readout is derived]
\label{lem:lifted-prob-readout}
Under the $\Dmon$ readout $p := w_1$ (so $w_0 = 1-p$, the mass-one
slice $Z(w) = w_0+w_1=1$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}), the lifted
family restricts to
\[
  p \AndC q = pq, \qquad p \parr q = p+q-pq, \qquad \neg p = 1-p,
\]
i.e.\ exactly the operations of the (non-semiring)
$\ProbS$ row of Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring} --- \emph{derived}
from binding two independent truth values and pushing forward along
the Boolean operation, not stipulated.
\uses{lem:lifted-mass, def:dist-monad}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute $w_0=1-p,\ w_1=p,\ v_0=1-q,\ v_1=q$ into
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}: $w_1v_1=pq$; the $\parr$-slot
$w_0v_1+w_1v_0+w_1v_1 = (1-p)q+p(1-q)+pq = p+q-pq$; and
$\neg w = (w_1,w_0)=(p,1-p)$ read out at slot $1$ of the swapped pair
is $1-p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Order family]
\label{def:order-family}
For a lattice-semiring $S$ (Definition~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}), the
\textbf{order family} on $\MS{S}\BoolS \cong S^2$ (coordinates
$(w_0,w_1)$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:truth-space-instances}) is the order
\[
  w \le v \quad :\iff \quad w_1 \le_S v_1 \ \text{and}\ w_0 \ge_S v_0
\]
(componentwise in $S$'s own order, slot $0$ reversed), with meet
$\wedge$ and join $\vee$ its meet and join, and bounds
$\bot = (\top_S, \bot_S)$, $\top = (\bot_S, \top_S)$ where they exist
in $S$ (after adjoining $\infty$ to $S$ if needed).
\uses{def:lattice-semiring, def:truth-space}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Order family: provenance and generality]
\label{rem:order-family-generality}
The order family of Definition~\ref{def:order-family} is induced by
the order of $S$, not by the monad structure of $\MS{S}$. It is stated
for a general lattice-semiring $S$; [NeSy26, App.~A] works the order
out concretely only for $S \in \{\MassS, \LogS\}$
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}, \ref{lem:lifted-log}).
\uses{def:order-family}
\end{remark}

\section{Three layers}
\label{sec:three-layers}

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise]
\label{lem:pointwise}
For a lattice-semiring $S$, $\MS{S}(\BoolS) = S^{\BoolS} \cong S\times
S$ carries all scalar operations ($\oplus,\otimes,0,1$) and the order
($\wedge,\vee,\bot,\top,\le$) componentwise, and a (finite) power of a
lattice-semiring is again a lattice-semiring.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring, def:semiring-monad}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$S^{\BoolS}$ is the product of two copies of $S$ in the
variety of lattice-semirings (a variety defined by the semiring
equations plus the monotonicity axioms of
Definition~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}, all of which are preserved by
products); all structure is inherited componentwise.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Linear laws lift]
\label{lem:linear-lift}
For a commutative semiring $S$, the lifted family
$(\MS{S}\BoolS, \parr, \Ret(0))$ and $(\MS{S}\BoolS, \AndC, \Ret(1))$ of
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} are commutative monoids, and
$\neg$ is a De~Morgan involution between them: $\neg\neg w = w$,
$\neg \Ret(0) = \Ret(1)$, and
$\neg(w \parr v) = \neg w \AndC \neg v$,
$\neg(w \AndC v) = \neg v \parr \neg w$.
\uses{def:lifted-connective, lem:pointwise, thm:semiring-monad-commutative}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Directly from Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}
(equivalently~\ref{lem:lifted-log}): associativity, commutativity and
the unit laws of $\parr,\AndC$ each involve every variable exactly
once per side of the defining equation, hence expand, via
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}'s bind chain, to associativity,
commutativity and the unit laws of $S$ itself
(Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}); commutativity of $\parr,\AndC$
specifically needs the two independent binds building the lifted
connective from its two arguments to agree in either order, i.e.\
$\dstL = \dstR$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-commutative}), which
is exactly where commutativity of $S$ enters; the De~Morgan identities
are a direct check on the explicit formulas of Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Instances, not a schema]
\label{rem:linear-lift-schema}
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-lift} is stated as these concrete instances
rather than as the source's meta-theorem over general equation schemas
in which each variable occurs exactly once per side, per the
commutative-monad/symmetric-monoidal argument cited there; this
library records the theorem only at the instances it uses, not as a
schema-level meta-theorem.
\uses{lem:linear-lift}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Copying laws do not lift]
\label{lem:copying-fails}
Equations that duplicate a variable --- idempotence, absorption,
lattice distributivity --- fail for the lifted family in general.
Witness: in $\Dmon\,\BoolS \cong \ProbS$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-prob-readout}), $p \AndC p = p^2 \ne p$ for
$p \in (0,1)$, so $\AndC$ is not idempotent.
\uses{lem:lifted-prob-readout}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The displayed computation; $p^2=p$ iff $p\in\{0,1\}$.
\end{proof}

% Statement-minimality law (M5 Part B.8, predates this sweep): the
% "This failure is structural..." commentary is discourse about the
% lemma, not part of what it states or proves, so it moves to its own
% remark rather than living inside the lemma body.
\begin{remark}[Why copying fails]
\label{rem:copying-fails-structural}
This failure is structural, not accidental: a lifted operation reads
its two arguments as independent, and non-idempotence is exactly what
makes truth graded rather than classical.
\uses{lem:copying-fails}
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Three layers of lattice semirings]
\label{thm:three-layers}
Let $S$ be a lattice-semiring and apply $\MS{S}$ to the lattice-semiring
$\BoolS$. Then $\MS{S}\BoolS$ decomposes into three independent
contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{pointwise}: $S^{\BoolS}$ is again a lattice-semiring
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise}), so the construction closes and
  iterates --- $\MS{S}(\BoolS)$ can serve as the weight semiring of a
  next layer for $S \in \{\BoolS, \MassS, \LogS\}$ (not $\ProbS$, which
  is excluded by Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring});
\item[(ii)] \emph{linear/lifted}: the family $\AndC,\parr,\neg$ with
  units $\Ret(0),\Ret(1)$ satisfies every linear Boolean law and no
  copying law (Lemmas~\ref{lem:linear-lift}, \ref{lem:copying-fails}):
  two commutative monoids with a De~Morgan involution, not a semiring;
\item[(iii)] \emph{order}: the family $\wedge,\vee,\bot,\top$ of
  Definition~\ref{def:order-family} is induced by the order of $S$,
  componentwise with the $0$-slot reversed, not by the monad.
\end{enumerate}
The copying axioms are exactly the order-generating ones ($u \le v$
iff $u \wedge v = u$), so (ii) and (iii) are disjoint contributions:
both families are logic, differing only in origin. They coincide
exactly where the carrier is idempotent, on $\BoolS$ itself. The units
$\Ret(0),\Ret(1)$ agree with the bounds $\bot,\top$ only in the
normalized (mass-one) rows.
\uses{lem:pointwise, lem:linear-lift, lem:copying-fails, def:order-family, lem:prob-not-semiring}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) is Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise}; (ii) is
Lemmas~\ref{lem:linear-lift} and \ref{lem:copying-fails}; (iii) is the
construction of Definition~\ref{def:order-family}.
\end{proof}

\section{Syntax and monadic semantics}
\label{sec:syntax-semantics}

This chapter replaces its previous itemization --- the NeSy26 paper's
Tarski/Kleisli/Nesy trichotomy, its do-notation-primitive term grammar,
and its $(D \to M\Omega) \to M\Omega$ quantifier semantics, all now
\textbf{superseded} and retained only in this repository's git history
--- with the current NeSyCat theory, authored directly against the
working text of \emph{NeSyCat Theory: Semantics in Kleisli Categories
for Neuro-Symbolic AI in HaskTorch} (\textbf{NeSyCat Theory v2}), lines
1--396 of \texttt{new.tex} in the \texttt{NeSyCat.Logics} repository
(sha256 prefix \texttt{9a84f22551f1e9ec} of that working text, itself
layered over \texttt{NeSyCat.Logics} git ref \texttt{1ade210}); no
notation from the superseded §3+ of the NeSy26 paper is reused below.
The theory is a stack of four signature/interpretation layers ---
categorical, logical, domain, grammatical --- the last of which fixes a
context-free term/formula grammar with an explicit substitution rule
and interprets it in the Kleisli category of a strong monad.

\paragraph{Categorical layer.}

\begin{definition}[Categorical signature]
\label{def:categorical-signature}
A \textbf{categorical signature} $\Sigma_\alpha$ consists of a
\textbf{category symbol} $\mathbf C$, an \textbf{actor symbol}
$\mathbf A$, and a \textbf{monad symbol} $\bigcirc$. The identity monad
symbol $\mathrm{Id}$ is left implicit, but can always be added
explicitly to a set of monad symbols
$\mathrm{Mon} := \{\mathrm{Id}, \bigcirc\}$.
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[CD category]
\label{def:cd-category}
A \textbf{CD (copy--discard) category}
$(\mathcal C, \otimes_{\mathcal C}, \mathrm I_{\mathcal C}, \mathsf{copy}, \mathsf{del})$
is a symmetric monoidal category equipped with a commutative comonoid
$\mathsf{copy}_X \colon X \to X \otimes_{\mathcal C} X$,
$\mathsf{del}_X \colon X \to \mathrm I_{\mathcal C}$ on every object
$X$, compatible with $\otimes_{\mathcal C}$.
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Categorical interpretation]
\label{def:categorical-interpretation}
A \textbf{categorical interpretation} $\mathcal I_\alpha$ of a
categorical signature $\Sigma_\alpha$ (Definition~\ref{def:categorical-signature})
is given by a CD category
$(\mathcal C, \otimes_{\mathcal C}, \mathrm I_{\mathcal C}, \mathsf{copy}, \mathsf{del}) := \mathcal I(\mathbf C)$
(Definition~\ref{def:cd-category}) for the category symbol $\mathbf C$,
a monoidal category
$(\mathcal A, \otimes_{\mathcal A}, \mathrm I_{\mathcal A}) := \mathcal I(\mathbf A)$
for the actor symbol $\mathbf A$, acting on $\mathcal C$ as an
\textbf{actegory} with action
$\actop \colon \mathcal A \times \mathcal C \to \mathcal C$, and a
monad $\mathcal M := \mathcal I(\bigcirc)$ on $\mathcal C$ for the
monad symbol $\bigcirc$. The identity monad symbol $\mathrm{Id}$ is
always interpreted by the identity functor on $\mathcal C$.
\uses{def:categorical-signature, def:cd-category}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Running categorical interpretations]
\label{rem:categorical-instances}
Three categorical interpretations recur as running examples of
Definition~\ref{def:categorical-interpretation}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Set/Tens/Identity}: the set-theoretic category
  $\mathcal C := \mathbf{Set}$ for $\mathbf C$, the monoidal category
  $\mathcal A := \mathbf{Tens}$ for $\mathbf A$, and the identity monad
  $\mathcal M := \mathrm{Identity}$ for $\bigcirc$.
\item \textbf{Set/Tens/Dist}: the measure-theoretic category
  $\mathcal C := \mathbf{Set}$ for $\mathbf C$, $\mathcal A :=
  \mathbf{Tens}$ for $\mathbf A$, and the distribution monad
  $\mathcal M := \mathrm{Dist}$ for $\bigcirc$.
\item \textbf{Tens/Tens/LogVec}: the geometric category
  $\mathcal C := \mathbf{Tens}$ for $\mathbf C$, $\mathcal A :=
  \mathbf{Tens}$ for $\mathbf A$, and the monad
  $\mathcal M := \mathrm{LogVec}$ for $\bigcirc$.
\end{itemize}
\uses{def:categorical-interpretation}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Logical layer.}

\begin{definition}[Logical signature]
\label{def:logical-signature}
A \textbf{logical signature} $\Sigma_\beta$ consists of a \textbf{truth
symbol} $\tau$, a set of \textbf{connective symbols} $\mathrm{Conn}$
each with an arity $n \in \mathbb N_0$ and a monad symbol
$M \in \mathrm{Mon}$, and a set of \textbf{(finite) quantifier symbols}
$\Quan$ each with a monad symbol $M \in \mathrm{Mon}$. A connective
symbol $*$ with arity $n$ and monad symbol $M$ is written
$* \colon M\tau^n \to M\tau$, a comparison symbol $\prec$ with arity
$n$ and monad symbol $M$ is written
$\prec \colon M\tau^n \to M\mathrm{Bool}$, and a quantifier symbol $Q$
with monad symbol $M$ is written $Q \colon M\tau$.
\uses{def:categorical-signature}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Logical interpretation]
\label{def:logical-interpretation}
A \textbf{logical interpretation} $\mathcal I_\beta$ of a logical
signature $\Sigma_\beta$ (Definition~\ref{def:logical-signature}) in a
CD category $(\mathcal C, \otimes, \mathrm I, \mathsf{copy}, \mathsf{del})$
(Definition~\ref{def:cd-category}) is given by an object
$\Omega := \mathcal I(\tau)$, a morphism
$\mathcal I(*) \colon (\mathcal I(M)\Omega)^{\otimes n} \to \mathcal I(M)\Omega$
for each connective symbol $* \colon M\tau^n \to M\tau$ of arity $n$,
and a family of morphisms $\{\mathcal I(Q)_n\}_{n \in \mathbb N_0}$,
\[
  \mathcal I(Q)_n \colon (\mathcal I(M)\Omega)^{\otimes n} \to \mathcal I(M)\Omega,
\]
for each quantifier symbol $Q$ with monad symbol interpreted as
$\mathcal I(M)$.
\uses{def:logical-signature, def:categorical-interpretation}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Domain layer.}

\begin{definition}[Domain signature]
\label{def:domain-signature}
A \textbf{domain signature} $\Sigma_\gamma$ is given by:
\begin{itemize}
\item a set of \textbf{domain symbols} $\mathrm{Dom}$ and a set of
  \textbf{(parameter) space symbols} $\mathrm{Spc}$;
\item a set of \textbf{function symbols} $\Fun$ with functions
  $\mathrm{dom}, \mathrm{cod} \colon \Fun \to \mathcal L(\mathrm{Mon}
  \times \mathrm{Dom})$, mapping each function symbol to a list of pairs of
  monad and domain symbols --- one marker per argument, so a function
  may consume and produce a mix of plain and monadic arguments --- for
  its domain and codomain respectively, and a function
  $\mathrm{par} \colon \Fun \to \mathcal L(\mathrm{Spc})$, mapping each
  function symbol to its list of parameter spaces;
\item a set of \textbf{relation symbols} $\Rel$ with a function
  $\mathrm{ari} \colon \Rel \to \mathcal L(\mathrm{Mon} \times \mathrm{Dom})$,
  mapping each relation symbol to its list of pairs of monad and
  domain symbols, and a function
  $\mathrm{par} \colon \Rel \to \mathcal L(\mathrm{Spc})$, mapping each
  relation symbol to its list of parameter spaces;
\item a set of \textbf{variable symbols} $\Var$ with a function
  $\mathrm{ovr} \colon \Var \to \mathrm{Dom}$, mapping each variable symbol to
  its domain symbol, and a set of \textbf{parameter symbols}
  $\mathrm{Par}$ with a function
  $\mathrm{ovr} \colon \mathrm{Par} \to \mathrm{Spc}$, mapping each
  parameter symbol to its parameter space.
\end{itemize}
\uses{def:categorical-signature}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Writing convention for typed symbols]
\label{rem:domain-signature-notation}
For a function symbol $f \in \Fun$ with domain
$(M_1,S_1),\ldots,(M_n,S_n)$, codomain
$(N_1,T_1),\ldots,(N_m,T_m)$, and parameter spaces
$\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k$, this library follows the source's
writing-layer convention and displays $f$ as
\[
  f_{\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k} \colon M_1 S_1, \ldots, M_n S_n \to N_1 T_1, \ldots, N_m T_m,
\]
subscripting the parameters, and likewise
$R_{\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k} \colon M_1 S_1, \ldots, M_n S_n \to \tau$
for a relation symbol $R \in \Rel$. In both cases the identity monad
symbol $\mathrm{Id}$ is omitted from the display. A variable symbol
$x \in \Var$ over domain symbol $S$ is displayed $x \colon S$, and a
parameter symbol $\theta \in \mathrm{Par}$ over parameter space
$\Theta$ is displayed $\theta \colon \Theta$. This is purely a
writing-layer convention, governed by the writing-layer discipline of
this library --- it is \emph{not} an abbreviation in the
Blueprint-to-Lean-correspondence sense, and it introduces no
\texttt{\textbackslash lean}-markable content of its own.
\uses{def:domain-signature}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Domain interpretation]
\label{def:domain-interpretation}
A \textbf{domain interpretation} $\mathcal I_\gamma$ of a domain
signature $\Sigma_\gamma$ (Definition~\ref{def:domain-signature}) into
the CD category $\mathcal C$ with its actegory $(\mathcal A, \actop)$
(Definition~\ref{def:categorical-interpretation}) sets
$\mathcal I(M_1 S_1,\ldots,M_n S_n) := \mathcal I(M_1 S_1)
\otimes_{\mathcal C} \cdots \otimes_{\mathcal C} \mathcal I(M_n S_n)$
with $\mathcal I(MS) := \mathcal I_\alpha(M)\mathcal I(S)$, and
$\mathcal I(\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k) := \mathcal I(\Theta_1)
\otimes_{\mathcal A} \cdots \otimes_{\mathcal A} \mathcal I(\Theta_k)$,
and assigns:
\begin{itemize}
\item an object $\mathcal I(S) \in \mathrm{Ob}_{\mathcal C}$ for each
  domain symbol $S \in \mathrm{Dom}$, and an object
  $\mathcal I(\Theta) \in \mathrm{Ob}_{\mathcal A}$ for each space
  symbol $\Theta \in \mathrm{Spc}$;
\item for each function symbol
  $f_{\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k} \colon M_1 S_1,\ldots,M_n S_n \to N_1
  T_1,\ldots,N_m T_m$ (Remark~\ref{rem:domain-signature-notation}) a
  morphism
  \[
    \mathcal I(f) \colon \mathcal I(\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k) \actop
    \mathcal I(M_1 S_1,\ldots,M_n S_n) \to \mathcal I(N_1 T_1,\ldots,N_m T_m)
  \]
  in $\mathcal C$;
\item for each relation symbol
  $R_{\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k} \colon M_1 S_1,\ldots,M_n S_n \to \tau$
  a morphism
  \[
    \mathcal I(R) \colon \mathcal I(\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k) \actop
    \mathcal I(M_1 S_1,\ldots,M_n S_n) \to \Omega
  \]
  in $\mathcal C$, with $\Omega$ as in
  Definition~\ref{def:logical-interpretation};
\item $\mathcal I(x) := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I(S)}$ for each variable
  symbol $x \colon S$, and $\mathcal I(\theta) := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal
  I(\Theta)}$ for each parameter symbol $\theta \colon \Theta$.
\end{itemize}
\uses{def:domain-signature, def:categorical-interpretation, def:logical-interpretation}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Grammatical layer.}

\begin{definition}[Context]
\label{def:context}
A \textbf{context} $\vec x$ is a finite list $[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ of
\emph{distinct} variable symbols from $\Var$
(Definition~\ref{def:domain-signature}), including the empty context
$[\,]$.
\uses{def:domain-signature}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Contexts as Elephant-style lists]
\label{rem:context-elephant-style}
Definition~\ref{def:context} is the Elephant-style presentation of
contexts (Johnstone); contexts are equivalently linearly ordered
subsets of $\Var$, so the usual set operations (union, intersection,
difference) apply to them.
\uses{def:context}
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Grammatical signature]
\label{def:grammatical-signature}
A \textbf{grammatical signature} $\Sigma_\delta$ is given by a set of
\textbf{terms} $\Xi$ with functions
$\inn, \out \colon \Xi \to \mathcal L(\Var)$, mapping each term to a
context (Definition~\ref{def:context}) of free variables (its input)
and a context of typed outputs (its output), a set of
\textbf{formulas} $\Phi$ with a function
$\mathrm{on} \colon \Phi \to \mathcal L(\Var)$, mapping each formula to
the context of its free variables, and the subset of \textbf{variable
terms} $\Xi_{\mathrm{var}} \subseteq \Xi$: each variable term
$\xi \in \Xi_{\mathrm{var}}$ is a variable symbol $x \in \Var$, with
$\inn(\xi) = \out(\xi)$. For $\phi \in \Phi$ write
$[\phi] := \mathrm{on}(\phi)$ for its canonical context. Terms and
formulas are generated over a domain signature $\Sigma_\gamma$
(Definition~\ref{def:domain-signature}) and a logical signature
$\Sigma_\beta$ (Definition~\ref{def:logical-signature}) by the
following context-free grammar:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\hline
\textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Symbols} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Metatype} \\
\hline
$\xi ::= x$ & $x \in \Var$ & variable term & $\xi$ \\
$\xi ::= f(\vec\xi)$ & $f \in \Fun$ & functional term & $\vec\xi \to \xi$ \\
$\phi ::= R(\vec\xi)$ & $R \in \Rel$ & atomic formula & $\vec\xi \to \phi$ \\
$\phi ::= *(\vec\phi)$ & $* \in \Conn$ & compound formula & $\vec\phi \to \phi$ \\
$\phi ::= Q\vec x(\phi)$ & $Q \in \Quan$ & quantified formula & $\phi \to \phi$ \\
$\phi ::= \phi[x := \xi]$ & $x \in [\phi]$ & substituted formula & $\xi,\phi \to \phi$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
the last rule --- \textbf{substitution} $\phi[x := \xi]$, for $x$ a
free variable of $\phi$ and $\xi$ any term --- generates formulas
explicitly, rather than only being a meta-level operation on already
generated formulas.
\uses{def:domain-signature, def:logical-signature, def:context}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Wire adapters and bind set]
\label{def:wire-adapters}
Consider composable typed symbols (Remark~\ref{rem:domain-signature-notation})
$f \colon M^f_1 S_1,\ldots,M^f_n S_n \to N^f_1 T_1,\ldots,N^f_m T_m$
and
$g \colon M^g_1 T_1,\ldots,M^g_m T_m \to N^g_1 U_1,\ldots,N^g_k U_k$,
whose marked interfaces on the shared domain symbols $T_1,\ldots,T_m$
need not agree: the output marker $N^f_j$ of $f$ and the input marker
$M^g_j$ of $g$ on $T_j$ are independent. For each wire
$j \in \{1,\ldots,m\}$ the \textbf{adapter} $\alpha_j$ is given by:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cclc}
\hline
$N^f_j$ & $M^g_j$ & \textbf{Adapter} & \textbf{Nature} \\
\hline
$\mathrm{Id}$ & $\mathrm{Id}$ &
  $\alpha_j := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I(T_j)}$ & pure \\
$\mathrm{Id}$ & $\bigcirc$ &
  $\alpha_j := \eta_{\mathcal I(T_j)}$ & pure (Dirac passed as data) \\
$\bigcirc$ & $\bigcirc$ &
  $\alpha_j := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal M\mathcal I(T_j)}$ & pure (computation passed as data) \\
$\bigcirc$ & $\mathrm{Id}$ &
  $\alpha_j := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal M\mathcal I(T_j)}$, $j \in J$ & effectful (bound) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
On the first three rows $\alpha_j$ lands exactly in the expected input
object $C_j := \mathcal I(M^g_j T_j)$; on the last row no such morphism
exists ($g$ expects a value while $f$ delivers a computation
$\mathcal M\mathcal I(T_j)$). These wires form the \textbf{bind set}
$J \subseteq \{1,\ldots,m\}$.
\uses{def:domain-signature, def:categorical-interpretation, def:domain-interpretation}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Feed-forward composite]
\label{def:feed-forward}
With notation as in Definition~\ref{def:wire-adapters}, let
$J = \{j_1 < \cdots < j_r\}$ be the bind set,
$\vec\alpha := \alpha_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes \alpha_m$, and
$(b_1,\ldots,b_m) := \vec\alpha\bigl(\mathcal I(f)(x)\bigr)$ the
adapted wires. The \textbf{feed-forward composite}
$\mathcal I(f) \seqop \mathcal I(g) \colon \mathcal I(\mathrm{dom}(f))
\to \mathcal M\,\mathcal I(\mathrm{cod}(g))$ binds the effectful wires
in list order,
\[
  \bigl(\mathcal I(f) \seqop \mathcal I(g)\bigr)(x) :=
  \Do\{\, c_{j_1} \leftarrow b_{j_1};\ \ldots;\ c_{j_r} \leftarrow b_{j_r};\
  \Ret\ \mathcal I(g)(c_1,\ldots,c_m) \,\},
\]
where $c_j := b_j$ for $j \notin J$. Equivalently, point-free,
\[
  \mathcal I(f) \seqop \mathcal I(g) := \mathcal I(f) \seq \vec\alpha
  \seq \eta \seq \bind\,\sigma^{(j_1)} \seq \cdots \seq
  \bind\,\sigma^{(j_r)} \seq \bind\,\bigl(\mathcal I(g) \seq \eta\bigr),
\]
where
$\sigma^{(j)} \colon X_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal M X_j \otimes
\cdots \otimes X_m \to \mathcal M(X_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes X_m)$ is
$\mathcal M$'s strength at the $j$-th factor and
$\bind\,k := \mathcal M k \seq \mu$ abbreviates binding a continuation
$k$ against $\mathcal M$'s multiplication $\mu$.
\uses{def:wire-adapters}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Kleisli interpretation]
\label{def:kleisli-interpretation}
Fix a categorical interpretation with CD category $\mathcal C$ and
strong monad $\mathcal M$ (Definition~\ref{def:categorical-interpretation}),
a domain interpretation (Definition~\ref{def:domain-interpretation}),
and a logical interpretation (Definition~\ref{def:logical-interpretation}).
The \textbf{Kleisli interpretation} $\sem{\cdot}$ of the grammatical
signature $\Sigma_\delta$ (Definition~\ref{def:grammatical-signature})
assigns
\[
  \sem\xi \colon \mathcal I(\inn(\xi)) \to \mathcal M\,\mathcal I(\out(\xi))
  \quad (\xi \in \Xi), \qquad
  \sem\phi \colon \mathcal I([\phi]) \to \mathcal M\,\Omega \quad (\phi \in \Phi),
\]
by induction on the grammar of Definition~\ref{def:grammatical-signature}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
\textbf{Syntax} & \textbf{Semantics} & \textbf{Type} \\
\hline
$\sem x$ & $\eta_{\mathcal I([x])}$ & $\mathcal I([x]) \to \mathcal M\mathcal I([x])$ \\
$\sem{f(\vec\xi)}$ & $\sem{\vec\xi} \seqop \mathcal I(f)$ & $\mathcal I(\inn(\vec\xi)) \to \mathcal M\,\mathcal I(\mathrm{cod}(f))$ \\
$\sem{R(\vec\xi)}$ & $\sem{\vec\xi} \seqop \mathcal I(R)$ & $\mathcal I(\inn(\vec\xi)) \to \mathcal M\Omega$ \\
$\sem{*(\vec\phi)}$ & $\sem{\vec\phi} \seqop \mathcal I(*)$ & $\mathcal I([\vec\phi]) \to \mathcal M\Omega$ \\
$\sem{Q\vec x(\phi)}$ & $\bigotimes_i \sem\phi_{[\vec x\mapsto\mathcal I([\vec x])_i]} \seq \mathcal I(Q)_{|\mathcal I([\vec x])|}$ & $\mathcal I([\phi]\setminus[\vec x]) \to \mathcal M\Omega$ \\
$\sem{\phi[x:=\xi]}$ & $\sem\phi_{[x\mapsto\sem\xi]}$ & $\mathcal I([\phi]_{[x]\mapsto\inn(\xi)}) \to \mathcal M\Omega$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The semantics of a \emph{list} of formulas is the tensor of
the components over their shared context,
\[
  \sem{\vec\phi} := \mathsf{copy} \seq (\sem{\phi_1} \otimes \cdots
  \otimes \sem{\phi_n}), \qquad
  \mathsf{copy} \colon \mathcal I([\vec\phi]) \to \mathcal I([\phi_1])
  \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal I([\phi_n]),
\]
where $\mathsf{copy}$ routes each shared variable to every component
using it, built from $\mathsf{copy}$ and the symmetry of the CD
category $\mathcal C$ (Definition~\ref{def:cd-category}), and is the
identity on disjoint contexts. For a (finite-sorted) quantified formula
$Q\vec x(\phi)$ with $\vec x = [x_{p_1},\ldots,x_{p_l}] \subseteq [\phi]$
a sublist of the canonical context occupying positions
$p_1 < \cdots < p_l$, the \textbf{product state space}
$\mathcal I([\vec x]) := \mathcal I([x_{p_1}]) \otimes \cdots \otimes
\mathcal I([x_{p_l}])$ is again finite, enumerated lexicographically by
$\mathcal I([\vec x])_i \colon \mathrm I \to \mathcal I([\vec x])$,
$i = 1,\ldots,|\mathcal I([\vec x])| = \prod_{j=1}^l|\mathcal
I([x_{p_j}])|$, each $i$ decomposing lexicographically into components
$(i_1,\ldots,i_l)$ indexing the states of
$\mathcal I([x_{p_1}]),\ldots,\mathcal I([x_{p_l}])$. Splitting $[\phi]$
at the positions of $\vec x$ into the (possibly empty) gaps
$[\phi] = [z_0] + [x_{p_1}] + [z_1] + \cdots + [x_{p_l}] + [z_l]$, so
that $[\phi]\setminus[\vec x] = [z_0]+\cdots+[z_l]$, the
\emph{insertion} of the $i$-th product state at positions
$\vec p = (p_1,\ldots,p_l)$ is
\[
  \mathcal I([\vec x])_i^{(\vec p)} := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I([z_0])}
  \otimes \mathcal I([x_{p_1}])_{i_1} \otimes \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal
  I([z_1])} \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal I([x_{p_l}])_{i_l} \otimes
  \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I([z_l])} \colon \mathcal I([\phi]\setminus[\vec
  x]) \to \mathcal I([\phi]),
\]
via the unitors identifying each empty gap with the tensor unit
$\mathrm I$, so every $\vec p$ is covered; then
$\sem\phi_{[\vec x \mapsto \mathcal I([\vec x])_i]} := \mathcal
I([\vec x])_i^{(\vec p)} \seq \sem\phi$ and
\[
  \sem{Q\vec x(\phi)} = \bigotimes_i \sem\phi_{[\vec x \mapsto \mathcal
  I([\vec x])_i]} \seq \mathcal I(Q)_{|\mathcal I([\vec x])|} \colon
  \mathcal I([\phi]\setminus[\vec x]) \to (\mathcal M\Omega)^{\otimes
  |\mathcal I([\vec x])|} \to \mathcal M\Omega,
\]
the single-variable case $l=1$ ---with $\vec x = [x]$ and gaps
$[z_0]=[y_1,\ldots,y_{p-1}]$, $[z_1]=[y_{p+1},\ldots,y_m]$ for $x$ at
position $p$--- recovering the source's original clause $\sem{Qx(\phi)}$.
The substituted formula $\phi[x:=\xi]$ uses the analogous
single-position insertion notation for the \emph{term}'s denotation: with
$\sem\xi^{(p)} := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I([y_1,\ldots,y_{p-1}])} \otimes
\sem\xi \otimes \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I([y_{p+1},\ldots,y_m])}$, define
$\sem\phi_{[x \mapsto \sem\xi]} := \sem\xi^{(p)} \seqop \sem\phi$, so
that
\[
  \sem{\phi[x:=\xi]} = \sem\phi_{[x \mapsto \sem\xi]} \colon \mathcal
  I([\phi]_{[x]\mapsto\inn(\xi)}) \to \mathcal M\Omega,
\]
where $[\phi]_{[x]\mapsto\inn(\xi)}$ is $[\phi]$ with $x$ replaced by
$\inn(\xi)$ via list concatenation,
$[y_1,\ldots,y_{p-1}] + \inn(\xi) + [y_{p+1},\ldots,y_m]$: the
substituted formula binds \emph{one} computation and copies its
\emph{value} into every occurrence of $x$, in contrast with repeating
the term itself (Example~\ref{ex:dice-scoping}).
\uses{def:grammatical-signature, def:feed-forward, def:domain-interpretation, def:logical-interpretation, def:cd-category}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Quantifier nestability]
\label{lem:quantifier-nestable}
Call a family $\mathcal I(Q)_n$ ($n = 1,2,\ldots$) \textbf{nestable}
when $\mathcal I(Q)_{mn} = \bigl(\mathcal I(Q)_n\bigr)^{\otimes m} \seq
\mathcal I(Q)_m$ (grouping $mn$ inputs into $m$ blocks of $n$), and
\textbf{symmetric} when $\mathcal I(Q)_n$ is invariant under
permutations of its $n$ inputs. For a symmetric, nestable quantifier
family, the simultaneous interpretation
$\sem{Q\vec x(\phi)}$ of Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}
equals the iterated interpretation
$\sem{Qx_1(Qx_2(\cdots(\phi)\cdots))}$, for any ordering of $\vec x$.
\uses{def:kleisli-interpretation, def:logical-interpretation}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex)]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induction on the length $l$ of $\vec x$. Base case $l=1$: the
simultaneous and iterated interpretations coincide definitionally, both
being $\sem\phi_{[\vec x\mapsto \mathcal I([\vec x])_i]} \seq \mathcal
I(Q)_1$ at the single variable. Inductive step: enumerating the product
state space $\mathcal I([\vec x])$ lexicographically
(Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}) groups its
$\bigl|\mathcal I([x_{p_1}])\bigr|\cdot n$ states into
$\bigl|\mathcal I([x_{p_1}])\bigr|$ blocks of size $n$, one block per
value of the first variable $x_{p_1}$, with the remaining $l-1$
variables enumerated within each block by the induction hypothesis;
nestability, $\mathcal I(Q)_{mn} = \bigl(\mathcal I(Q)_n\bigr)^{\otimes
m} \seq \mathcal I(Q)_m$ at $m = \bigl|\mathcal I([x_{p_1}])\bigr|$,
collapses this blocked tensor into first applying the (by induction)
iterated interpretation of $Q$ over the last $l-1$ variables within
each block, then applying $\mathcal I(Q)_m$ over the blocks indexed by
$x_{p_1}$ --- exactly the iterated interpretation
$\sem{Qx_{p_1}(Q\vec x'(\phi))}$ for $\vec x' = \vec x
\setminus\{x_{p_1}\}$. Symmetry of the family absorbs the reindexing
needed to reach any other ordering of $\vec x$, since permuting the
inputs of $\mathcal I(Q)_n$ (resp.\ $\mathcal I(Q)_m$) does not change
its value.
\end{proof}

\label{rem:quantifier-families-nestable}
The four running quantifier columns --- Max, Min, the $\Sigma$--$\Pi$

\begin{example}[Dice or die]
\label{ex:dice-scoping}
Let $\mathsf{dice} \colon [\,] \to \bigcirc\mathrm{Nat}$,
$6 \colon [\,] \to \mathrm{Nat}$, ${=} \colon \mathrm{Nat}^2 \to \tau$,
$\mathsf{even} \colon \mathrm{Nat} \to \tau$, and
$\AndC \colon \tau^2 \to \tau$ (van Krieken et al.'s scoping example),
and contrast
\[
  \bigl(x = 6 \AndC \mathsf{even}(x)\bigr)[x := \mathsf{dice}]
  \qquad\text{versus}\qquad
  (\mathsf{dice} = 6) \AndC \mathsf{even}(\mathsf{dice}).
\]
On the left, one throw is bound to $x$ and its \emph{value} is copied
into both subformulas; on the right, each occurrence of $\mathsf{dice}$
is its own throw. Unfolding Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}
on the left, using Definition~\ref{def:feed-forward} for
$\seqop$ and $\bind$,
\[
  \sem{\bigl(x=6\AndC\mathsf{even}(x)\bigr)[x:=\mathsf{dice}]}
  = \sem{\mathsf{dice}} \seqop \sem{x=6\AndC\mathsf{even}(x)}
  = \Do\{\, x \leftarrow \mathcal I(\mathsf{dice});\ \Ret\ x=6 \AndC \mathsf{even}(x) \,\},
\]
whereas on the right, expanding the compound formula's list-semantics
before the atomic formulas,
\[
  \sem{(\mathsf{dice}=6)\AndC\mathsf{even}(\mathsf{dice})}
  = \Do\{\, n_1 \leftarrow \mathcal I(\mathsf{dice});\ n_2 \leftarrow
  \mathcal I(\mathsf{dice});\ \Ret\ n_1=6\AndC\mathsf{even}(n_2) \,\}.
\]
For a fair die these give probability $\tfrac16$ versus
$\tfrac16\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac1{12}$: the substituted formula copies the
\emph{value}, the repeated term copies the \emph{computation}.
\uses{def:kleisli-interpretation, def:feed-forward}
[NeSyCat Theory v2 (new.tex 1--396)]
\end{example}

\section{Batching}
\label{sec:batching}

\begin{definition}[Batch monad]
\label{def:batch-monad}
Let $\Bidx = \{1,\ldots,B\}$ index a mini-batch of $B$ samples
evaluated together in one run ($B=N$ for the full sample). The
\textbf{batch monad} $\Bmon$ is the reader monad on $\Bidx$: for a set
$X$,
\[
  \Bmon X := X^{\Bidx} \cong (\Bidx \to X), \qquad
  \Ret_{\Bmon}(x) := \mathrm{const}\,x, \qquad
  (m \bind f)(b) := f(m(b))(b).
\]
[NeSy26, \S 5]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The batch monad's bind is diagonal]
\label{rem:batch-monad-diagonal}
The bind of Definition~\ref{def:batch-monad} is the \emph{diagonal}:
the continuation at index $b$ sees only index $b$'s value.
\uses{def:batch-monad}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Batch transformer]
\label{prop:batch-transformer}
For every strong monad $\mathcal M$ (in particular for every
$\mathcal M = \MS{S}$ of Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}, or
$\mathcal M = \Idmon$), the composite
\[
  \Bmon\mathcal M X := \bigl(\Bidx \to \mathcal M X\bigr)
\]
is again a monad --- the reader monad transformer at $\Bidx$ applied to
$\mathcal M$ --- and there are two canonical monad morphisms into it:
\[
  \mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M} \colon \mathcal M X \to \Bmon\mathcal M X,\
  \mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M} := \mathrm{const},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{lift}_{\Bmon} \colon \Bmon X \to \Bmon\mathcal M X,\
  \mathrm{lift}_{\Bmon}(m) := \Ret_{\mathcal M} \circ m.
\]
$\mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M}$ embeds a batch-constant effect;
$\mathrm{lift}_{\Bmon}$ embeds a batched but \emph{certain} value (an
observation).
\uses{def:batch-monad, thm:semiring-monad-laws}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Bmon\mathcal M$'s unit and bind are the reader-monad
ones of Definition~\ref{def:batch-monad} taken pointwise in
$\mathcal M X$; the monad laws for $\Bmon\mathcal M$ reduce, index by
index, to the monad laws of $\mathcal M$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws}). $\mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M}$
is a monad morphism since a constant family trivially commutes with
diagonal bind; $\mathrm{lift}_{\Bmon}$ is a monad morphism since
$\Ret_{\mathcal M}$ is natural and $\Ret_{\mathcal M}(m(b)) \bind_{\mathcal M} \Ret_{\mathcal M}\circ f = \Ret_{\mathcal M}(f(m(b)))$
by the unit laws of $\mathcal M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise evaluation]
\label{prop:pointwise-eval}
For each $i \in \Bidx$, evaluation
$\mathrm{ev}_i \colon \Bmon\mathcal M X \to \mathcal M X$,
$m \mapsto m(i)$, is a monad morphism. Assuming in addition that every
symbol and quantifier interpretation of
Definition~\ref{def:domain-interpretation} used in the batched reading
is \emph{batch-natural}, i.e.\ defined pointwise across the batch ---
$\mathcal I^{\Bmon\mathcal M}(f) = \mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M} \circ
\mathcal I^{\mathcal M}(f)$ applied index by index, for every symbol
$f$ --- $\mathrm{ev}_i$ commutes with the Kleisli interpretation of
feed-forward composites (grammar interpretation,
Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}): for a batch $s \colon
\Bidx \to \mathcal I(S)$,
\[
  \sem{\varphi}^{\Bmon\mathcal M}(s)(i) \;=\; \sem{\varphi}^{\mathcal M}(s_i)
  \qquad (i \in \Bidx).
\]
\uses{prop:batch-transformer, def:kleisli-interpretation}
[NeSy26, App.~A]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{ev}_i$ is a monad morphism because
$\mathrm{ev}_i(\Ret_{\Bmon\mathcal M}(a)) = \mathrm{const}(a)(i) = a =
\Ret_{\mathcal M}(a)$ and $\mathrm{ev}_i(m \bind f) = f(m(i))(i) =
m(i) \bind_{?}\!\ldots$ unfolds, under the batch-naturality hypothesis
on every symbol occurring in $f$, to $\mathrm{ev}_i(m) \bind
\mathrm{ev}_i \circ f$; induction on the grammar of
Definition~\ref{def:grammatical-signature} then transports this through
every clause of Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Necessity of batch-naturality]
\label{rem:batch-naturality-necessity}
Without the batch-naturality hypothesis of
Proposition~\ref{prop:pointwise-eval}, only the pure $\Ret/\bind$
skeleton of a feed-forward fragment is guaranteed to commute with
$\mathrm{ev}_i$ (that part follows from $\mathrm{ev}_i$ being a monad
morphism alone); a symbol interpretation chosen independently per batch
index rather than pointwise would break the displayed equation.
\uses{prop:pointwise-eval}
\end{remark}

\section{Bridges and normalization}
\label{sec:bridges}

\begin{definition}[Decode, encode, mass]
\label{def:dec-enc-mass}
For a finite set $X$, write a $\Tmon X$-value as a log-weight vector
$a \in \LogS^X$ (allowing $-\infty$ coordinates for zero mass). Define
\[
  Z_X(a) := \sum_{x \in X} e^{a_x} \qquad \text{(total mass)},
\]
\[
  \dec_X \colon \Tmon X \to \Dmon X, \quad \dec_X(a)_x := \frac{e^{a_x}}{Z_X(a)}
  \qquad \text{(softmax)},
\]
\[
  \enc_X \colon \Dmon X \to \Tmon X, \quad \enc_X(p)_x := \log p(x)
  \qquad \text{(pointwise log)}.
\]
\uses{def:dist-monad, lem:log-iso}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Pure maps]
\label{lem:pure-maps}
For pure $h \colon X \to Y$: $Z\bigl(\Tmon(h)(a)\bigr) = Z(a)$ and
$\dec_Y \circ \Tmon(h) = \Dmon(h) \circ \dec_X$.
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The fibers $h^{-1}(y)$ partition $X$; summing $e^{a_x}$
fiber by fiber rearranges the same total sum, giving both the mass
identity and, after dividing by $Z(a)$, the commuting square.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tensor]
\label{lem:tensor}
Write $a \otimes b \in \Tmon(X\times Y)$ for the tensorial-strength
pairing $(a\otimes b)_{(x,y)} := a_x+b_y$ of $a \in \Tmon X$, $b \in
\Tmon Y$ (the general instance of which the Boolean-lifted $\AndC$ of
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} is the special case $X=Y=\BoolS$
composed with $\Tmon(\wedge)$). Then
\[
  Z(a \otimes b) = Z(a)\,Z(b), \qquad
  \dec(a \otimes b) = \dec(a) \otimes \dec(b)
\]
(the right-hand $\otimes$ the analogous independent-joint pairing on
$\Dmon$).
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$e^{a_x+b_y} = e^{a_x}e^{b_y}$; sum over $x,y$ for the
mass identity, then divide by $Z(a)Z(b)$ to get the pointwise product
of softmaxes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Units]
\label{lem:units}
$\enc \circ \Ret_{\Dmon} = \Ret_{\Tmon}$, $\dec \circ \enc =
\mathrm{id}_{\Dmon}$, hence $\dec \circ \Ret_{\Tmon} = \Ret_{\Dmon}$,
and $Z(\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)) = 1$.
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\enc(\Ret_{\Dmon}(x))_{x'} = \log \delta_x(x')$, which
is $0$ at $x'=x$ and $\log 0 = -\infty$ elsewhere, matching
$\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)$ exactly (over $\LogS$); $\dec(\enc(p))_x =
e^{\log p(x)}/\sum_{x'} e^{\log p(x')} = p(x)/\sum_{x'}p(x') = p(x)$
since $p$ has mass $1$ (Definition~\ref{def:dist-monad}); the third
identity follows by composing the first two, and
$Z(\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)) = e^0 + \sum_{x'\ne x}e^{-\infty} = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exactness and the numerical $\epsilon$-floor]
\label{rem:units-epsilon-floor}
The identities of Lemma~\ref{lem:units} are exact only over the true
log semiring $\LogS = \mathbb R \cup \{-\infty\}$ of
Definition~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}; the numerical $\epsilon$-floor
implementation, which substitutes a large negative finite number for
$-\infty$, satisfies them only up to $O(\epsilon)$ error, because
$\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)$ genuinely needs a $-\infty$ (not merely small)
coordinate at every $x' \ne x$ for $\enc\circ\Ret_{\Dmon} =
\Ret_{\Tmon}$ to hold on the nose.
\uses{lem:units}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Strengths]
\label{lem:strengths}
Write the strength at slot $j$,
$\sigma^{(j)}_{\Tmon}(x_1,\ldots,a,\ldots,x_m) :=
\Ret_{\Tmon}(x_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes a \otimes \cdots \otimes
\Ret_{\Tmon}(x_m)$
(pairing the pure coordinates in via Lemma~\ref{lem:units}, tensoring
the effectful slot $j$ in via Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}), and
$\sigma^{(j)}_{\Dmon}$ likewise over $\Dmon$. Then
\[
  Z\bigl(\sigma^{(j)}_{\Tmon}(x_1,\ldots,a,\ldots,x_m)\bigr) = Z(a),
  \qquad
  \dec \circ \sigma^{(j)}_{\Tmon}
  = \bigl(\mathrm{id} \otimes \cdots \otimes \dec_j \otimes \cdots
     \otimes \mathrm{id}\bigr) \circ \sigma^{(j)}_{\Dmon} \circ (\mathrm{id},\ldots,\dec,\ldots,\mathrm{id})
\]
in the sense that decoding the strength agrees with decoding only the
effectful slot and inserting the plain coordinates as certain values.
\uses{lem:tensor, lem:units}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The plain coordinates enter as $\Ret_{\Tmon}(x_i)$,
mass $1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:units}; apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor} to the resulting $m$-fold tensor, all of whose
factors but the $j$-th have mass $1$, so both the total mass and the
decode-commutation reduce to the single effectful factor.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tilt]
\label{lem:tilt}
Let $a \in \Tmon X$ and $k \colon X \to \Tmon Y$, with $\bind$ the
$\Tmon$-bind of Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} (i.e.\
$(a \bind k)_y = \lse_x(a_x+k(x)_y)$). Assume $Z(k(x)) > 0$ for every
$x \in \supp(\dec(a))$ (equivalently, $k$'s mass is nowhere zero on
$a$'s support), needed for the tilt weight below to be positive there
and hence for $\mathrm{tilt}$ to be well-defined. Then
\[
  \dec(a \bind k) \;=\; \tilt_{Z \circ k}\bigl(\dec(a)\bigr) \bind
  \bigl(\dec \circ k\bigr),
  \qquad
  \tilt_w(p)(x) := \frac{w(x)\,p(x)}{\sum_{x'} w(x')\,p(x')}
  \quad (w \colon X \to \mathbb R_{>0}).
\]
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass, lem:units}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand both sides at $y \in Y$: the left side is
$e^{\lse_x(a_x+k(x)_y)}/Z(a\bind k) = \sum_x e^{a_x}e^{k(x)_y} /
\sum_{x,y'}e^{a_x+k(x)_{y'}}$; the right side is
$\sum_x \tilt_{Z\circ k}(\dec(a))(x)\,\dec(k(x))_y = \sum_x
\frac{Z(k(x))\dec(a)_x}{\sum_{x'}Z(k(x'))\dec(a)_{x'}} \cdot
\frac{e^{k(x)_y}}{Z(k(x))}$. The numerators agree termwise once
$\dec(a)_x = e^{a_x}/Z(a)$ is unfolded and the $Z(k(x))$ factors
cancel, and the two denominators both equal
$\sum_x e^{a_x}Z(k(x))/Z(a) = \sum_{x,y'}e^{a_x+k(x)_{y'}}/Z(a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Mass preserving]
\label{def:mass-preserving}
An arrow $k \colon X \to \Tmon Y$ is \textbf{mass preserving} if
$x \mapsto Z(k(x))$ is constant on $X$.
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Mass-preserving arrows are closed]
\label{lem:mass-preserving-closure}
The class of mass-preserving arrows (Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving})
contains $\Ret_{\Tmon} \circ h$ for every pure $h$, every lift
$\Tmon(h)$, the units of Lemma~\ref{lem:units}, and the strengths of
Lemma~\ref{lem:strengths}; it is closed under composition, under
$\otimes$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}), and under the structural wiring
(copying, discarding, permuting wires). A neural leaf is generally
\emph{not} in the class: its logits carry whatever mass they like.
\uses{def:mass-preserving, lem:pure-maps, lem:tensor, lem:units, lem:strengths}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$Z(\Ret_{\Tmon}(h(x))) = 1$ for all $x$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:units}), constant; $Z(\Tmon(h)(a)) = Z(a)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pure-maps}) exhibits $\Tmon(h)$ as mass-preserving only
when precomposed with a fixed $a$, and as an arrow $X \to \Tmon Y$ via
$x \mapsto \Tmon(h)(\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)) = \Ret_{\Tmon}(h(x))$ it reduces
to the previous case; strength factors have constant mass $Z(a)$
independent of the plain coordinates by Lemma~\ref{lem:strengths};
composing two mass-preserving arrows composes two constant functions
into a constant function, and $\otimes$ of two mass-preserving arrows
has mass the (constant) product by Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}; wiring
(copying, discarding, permuting) does not touch the weight vector at
all, hence trivially preserves any constant-mass property.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pull out]
\label{thm:pullout}
Let $\Phi = B_r \circ \cdots \circ B_1 \circ L$ be the point-free chain
of a feed-forward composite, where $L$ tensors together the leaves
(via Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}, landing in $\Tmon(Y_1\times\cdots\times
Y_n)$) and each $B_i$ is a lifted pure map $\Tmon(h)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pure-maps}), a unit insertion
(Lemma~\ref{lem:units}), a strength factor
(Lemma~\ref{lem:strengths}), or a bind $({-}\bind k_i)$. If every
bound continuation $k_i$ is mass preserving
(Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving}), then
\[
  \dec \circ \Phi \;=\;
  \bigl(B_r^{\Dmon} \circ \cdots \circ B_1^{\Dmon}\bigr) \circ
  (\dec \otimes \cdots \otimes \dec) \circ L,
\]
the same chain read in $\Dmon$, with one $\dec$ per leaf. If some
$k_i$ is not mass preserving, the two sides differ in general, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt} gives the exact discrepancy: one
$\tilt_{Z \circ k_i}$ per offending bind.
\uses{lem:pure-maps, lem:tensor, lem:units, lem:strengths, lem:tilt, def:mass-preserving, def:feed-forward}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $r$: each non-bind factor commutes with
$\dec$ by Lemmas~\ref{lem:pure-maps}, \ref{lem:tensor},
\ref{lem:units}, \ref{lem:strengths}; a bind factor commutes with
$\dec$ exactly when $\tilt_{Z\circ k_i} = \mathrm{id}$, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt} holds precisely when $Z\circ k_i$ is constant,
i.e.\ $k_i$ is mass preserving; the converse direction (some $k_i$ not
mass preserving forces a genuine discrepancy) is witnessed by
Example~\ref{ex:tilt-failure}.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[How wrong can it get]
\label{cnj:chain-bound}
If $m_i \le Z(k_i(x)) \le M_i$ for all $x$ and
every bind $k_i$ in the chain $\Phi$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout}, the
conjectured statement is that the two log-truth values satisfy
\[
  \bigl|\log t_{\Tmon} - \log t_{\Dmon}\bigr| \;\le\; \sum_i \log(M_i/m_i),
\]
with equality (the identity) exactly when every $M_i = m_i$. The
\emph{one-bind} case of this bound is provable directly from
Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt} (both sides average the same values under
densities that differ pointwise by at most a factor $M/m$, giving the
single-term bound $\bigl|\log t_{\Tmon} - \log t_{\Dmon}\bigr| \le
\log(M/m)$). The \emph{chain} (multi-bind) version stated above is
\emph{not} established by any proof in the source and is recorded here
as a standing research target of this library, not as a theorem; no
proof or proof sketch below may cite this conjecture as if it were
proved for $r > 1$ binds.
\uses{lem:tilt, thm:pullout}

\end{conjecture}

\begin{definition}[Normalizer]
\label{def:normalizer}
$\nrm_X := \enc_X \circ \dec_X \colon \Tmon X \to \Tmon X$, concretely
$\nrm(a) = a - \log Z(a)\cdot\mathbf 1$ (the log-softmax).
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Normalizer properties]
\label{lem:normalizer-props}
$\nrm$ (Definition~\ref{def:normalizer}) satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\dec \circ \nrm = \dec$ (predictions unchanged);
\item[(ii)] $Z(\nrm(a)) = 1$ (mass one);
\item[(iii)] $\nrm(a + c\cdot\mathbf 1) = \nrm(a)$ for every constant
  $c$ (kills the shift);
\item[(iv)] $\nrm \circ \nrm = \nrm$ (idempotent).
\end{enumerate}
\uses{def:normalizer}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\dec(\nrm(a))_x = e^{a_x-\log Z(a)}/Z(\nrm(a))$, and
(ii) $Z(\nrm(a)) = \sum_x e^{a_x}/Z(a) = 1$, so (i)'s numerator/1 is
$e^{a_x}/Z(a) = \dec(a)_x$; (iii) $Z(a+c\mathbf1) = e^cZ(a)$, so
$\nrm(a+c\mathbf1) = a+c\mathbf1-\log(e^cZ(a))\mathbf1 =
a-\log Z(a)\mathbf1 = \nrm(a)$; (iv) is (iii) at $c=-\log Z(a)$ applied
to $\nrm(a)$, using (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Normalized heads]
\label{cor:normalized-heads}
If every neural symbol interpretation is post-composed with $\nrm$
(Definition~\ref{def:normalizer}), then every leaf and every bound
continuation has mass one, hence is mass preserving
(Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving}), and Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout}
applies to \emph{every} program: $t_{\Tmon} = t_{\Dmon}$ for arbitrary
chains of neural symbols. By property (i) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:normalizer-props}, no prediction changes; the price is
one $\lse$ evaluation per head.
\uses{def:normalizer, lem:normalizer-props, thm:pullout, def:mass-preserving}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A mass-one weight vector $w$ has $Z(w)=1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:normalizer-props}(ii)), so $Z\circ(\text{symbol})$ is
the constant function $1$, i.e.\ mass preserving
(Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving}); apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout} with every $k_i$ of this form, so the
mass-preservation hypothesis holds unconditionally and the two sides
coincide, and (i) of Lemma~\ref{lem:normalizer-props} shows the
predicted distribution is unchanged by inserting $\nrm$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Tilt failure, exact numbers]
\label{ex:tilt-failure}
Let $X = Y = \{0,1\}$, $a = (0,0)$, $k(0) = (0,0)$,
$k(1) = (\ln 100,\, 0)$ (coordinates $(\cdot_0,\cdot_1)$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}/\ref{lem:lifted-log}). Then $Z(k(0)) = 2$,
$Z(k(1)) = 101$, so $k$ is far from mass preserving
(Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving}). Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt} tilts
$\dec(a) = (\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ by $w = Z\circ k = (2,101)$ into
$\tilt_w(\dec(a)) = \bigl(\tfrac{2}{103}, \tfrac{101}{103}\bigr)$, and
one checks directly that
\[
  \dec(a \bind k)(0) = \frac{101}{103} \approx 0.981,
\]
whereas the naive $\Dmon$-side computation that Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout}
would give \emph{if} $k$ were (wrongly assumed) mass preserving is
\[
  \dec(a)(0)\,\dec(k(0))(0) + \dec(a)(1)\,\dec(k(1))(0)
  = \tfrac14 + \tfrac{50}{101} \approx 0.745,
\]
visibly different: one bind with non-constant mass, one tilt, one
clearly wrong naive answer. This instantiates the converse direction
of Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout}.
\uses{lem:tilt, thm:pullout}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3]
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The MNIST chains commute]
\label{ex:mnist-commute}
In the two-digit MNIST-addition axiom's $\Tmon$-chain and
$\Dmon$-chain (Section~\ref{sec:syntax-semantics}'s running example),
the leaves are neural digit classifiers and every subsequent bind
binds either a strength, or a pure comparison $\Ret\circ({+})$, or a
pure comparison $\Ret\circ({=}s)$ against the observed sum: all mass
preserving by Lemma~\ref{lem:mass-preserving-closure}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout} therefore applies directly, giving
$t_{\Tmon} = t_{\Dmon}$ for the MNIST axiom. Concretely, the theorem
specializes on the addition step to the convolution identity
\[
  \dec\bigl(\mathrm{logconv}(a,b)\bigr) = \dec(a) * \dec(b)
\]
(discrete convolution of the two digit distributions), an instance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout} at the bind $({-}\bind \Ret\circ({+}))$,
which is mass preserving by Lemma~\ref{lem:mass-preserving-closure}
since $\Ret\circ h$ is mass preserving for every pure $h$.
\uses{thm:pullout, lem:mass-preserving-closure}
[NeSy26, App.~B draft/T3; \S 5]
\end{example}
