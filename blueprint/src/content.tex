This blueprint is the reference document for the NeSyCat library. It
states the library's mathematics, organized by mathematical structure.
The Lean formalization lives in the flat \texttt{NeSyCat.\textit{name}}
namespace.

Each item can carry two marks. \texttt{\textbackslash lean} names the
Lean declaration it corresponds to. \texttt{\textbackslash leanok}
means that declaration exists and is fully proved, with no
\texttt{sorry}. We add \texttt{\textbackslash leanok} only once a proof
is complete, and we check every \texttt{\textbackslash lean} name
against the built library, so the marks stay honest. An item carrying
neither mark has no Lean proof yet: that is all its absence ever means.

Two editorial principles govern every item:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Natural generality.} Each item is stated at its natural
  mathematical level. For example, the weight-monad construction
  $\MS{S}$ is stated for any semiring $S$, not assumed commutative.
  Concrete cases (Boolean, mass, log semirings; the $[0,1]$ readout)
  then appear as instances. This document states each item in the
  library's own voice, at its natural generality. A narrower case from
  elsewhere appears in-item, as an instance or corollary.
\item \textbf{Provenance.} Every item's mathematical origin is recorded
  next to it, in the source file, so credit is clear without cluttering
  the page.
\end{itemize}

We write functions and vectors directly, instead of Dirac bra-ket
notation, since this loses nothing mathematically. We compose maps
sequentially: $f \seq g$ means first $f$, then $g$. We never write
$\circ$.

Beyond this introduction, the library has five layers. The
\textbf{categorical layer} covers semiring weight monads, then
categorical signatures and CD semantics. Next, the \textbf{logical
layer} covers truth-value structures, truth spaces and lifted
connectives, the three-layers trichotomy, and logical signatures. The
\textbf{domain layer} covers domain signatures and interpretations.
Then the \textbf{grammatical layer} covers the term/formula grammar
and its Kleisli interpretation. The \textbf{statistical layer} covers
batching, the bridges/normalization theory connecting the probability
and tensor readings, and worked examples. An \textbf{inferential
layer} is reserved between the grammatical and statistical layers, for
future content connecting proof theory to model theory.

Two families of binary connective recur throughout, kept visually and
notationally apart. The \textbf{lattice connectives} $\vee,\wedge$
carry the bounds $\bot,\top$
(Class~\ref{def:bounded-lattice-semiring},
Definition~\ref{def:order-family}). The \textbf{monoid connectives on truth values} are $\oplus$, with unit
$\dzero$, and $\otimes$, with unit $\done$. They live at the
logical/truth-value level only. They name the \emph{lifted} family of
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}, once it is applied to truth
spaces, from Section~\ref{sec:truth-spaces} onward. The logical
multiplicatives are written with the semiring's own round connective
glyphs $\oplus,\otimes$, because they \emph{are} the semiring
operations carried to the truth-value level. Implications and
negations are always written indexed by the connective they belong to:
$\impc{\vee},\impc{\wedge},\impc{\oplus},\impc{\otimes}$ and
$\negc{\vee},\negc{\wedge},\negc{\oplus},\negc{\otimes}$; the optional
primitive involution (when one is assumed rather than derived) is
plain $\neg$. Linear-logic notation such as $\multimap$, $\setminus$,
or $\ominus$ for these connectives is never used here.

One point of vocabulary differs from linear logic. Our $\otimes$ is the
\emph{multiplicative} conjunction. Linear logic's own additive
conjunction is a different connective, our $\wedge$; the linear-logic
dictionary later in this section gives its own glyph for it. All these
symbols are LaTeX macros, collected in the library's symbol registry.
% Symbol registry file: blueprint/src/macros/common.tex.

Four constants recur at the logical/truth-value level: the lattice
bounds $\bot,\top$ (Definition~\ref{def:order-family}) and the monoid
units $\dzero,\done$ of $\oplus,\otimes$. The units are written in
bold, $\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}$, since $\oplus,\otimes$ are themselves the
semiring's operations carried to the truth-value level; the bold face
distinguishes a unit from a plain carrier numeral. The four stay
distinct constants nonetheless. On some instances a bound and a unit
coincide, for example on a normalized, mass-one row. We record such a
coincidence as a named axiom, UnitBounds (Class~\ref{def:unit-bounds}),
asserting $\dzero=\bot$ and $\done=\top$ where they hold. The notation
never folds this coincidence in: it always reads as a fact about a
particular instance.

\paragraph{How to read the boxes.} Each labeled box below matches a
Lean declaration directly. A \texttt{definition} introduces new
mathematical content that is not itself a structure a carrier can
satisfy. A \texttt{class} introduces such a structure: a bounded
lattice, a semiring refinement, a mixin such as \texttt{ZeroBot}. An
\texttt{instance} witnesses that one concrete carrier satisfies a
class. An \texttt{abbreviation} simply gives a name to something
already built (for example, we write $\Tmon$ for $\MS{\MassS}$). A
\texttt{lemma} and a \texttt{theorem} are proved the same way; this
document uses \texttt{lemma} for supporting infrastructure and
\texttt{theorem} for a chapter's main result. An open theorem, one with
no known proof, still gets a \texttt{theorem} box, paired with a
\texttt{proof} box whose entire content is the single word \emph{Open}.
Plain prose between the boxes, like this paragraph, carries everything
with no Lean counterpart: conventions, comparisons, and pointers
forward and back.

\subsection{Semiring weight monads}  % lean-dir: CategoricalLayer/SemiringMonads
\label{sec:semiring-monads}

This subsection develops the semiring weight monad $\MS{S}$. It builds
$\MS{S}$ from a family of ordered semirings, lattice-semirings and
their commutative and bounded refinements, then states the monad's
laws and its commutativity. It gives the log isomorphism linking the
mass and log rows.
% Apparatus (C2-E5): provenance credit, moved verbatim.
% Every item in this subsection is due to [NeSy26, App.~A], except the

% credited below also to Coumans--Jacobs, Lem.~23.

\begin{class}[Lattice-semiring]
\label{def:lattice-semiring}
\lean{NeSyCat.LatSRng}
\leanok
A \textbf{lattice-semiring} ($\mathrm{LatSRng}$) is a semiring
$(S, \oplus, \otimes, 0, 1)$ equipped
with a lattice order $\le$ on the carrier $S$: a meet $\wedge$ and join
$\vee$ exist for every pair of elements, and $\oplus$, $\otimes$ are
each monotone in \emph{both} arguments with respect to $\le$.
\end{class}

\begin{class}[Commutative lattice-semiring]
\label{def:comm-lattice-semiring}
\lean{NeSyCat.LatCSRng}
\leanok
A \textbf{commutative lattice-semiring} ($\mathrm{LatCSRng}$) is a
lattice-semiring (Class~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}) whose $\otimes$
is commutative.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring}
\end{class}

\begin{class}[Bounded lattice-semiring]
\label{def:bounded-lattice-semiring}
\lean{NeSyCat.BLatSRng}
\leanok
A \textbf{bounded lattice-semiring} ($\mathrm{BLatSRng}$) is a
lattice-semiring (Class~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}) that
additionally carries an in-carrier $\bot$ and $\top$.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring}
\end{class}

\begin{class}[Bounded commutative lattice-semiring]
\label{def:bounded-comm-lattice-semiring}
\lean{NeSyCat.BLatCSRng}
\leanok
A \textbf{bounded commutative lattice-semiring} ($\mathrm{BLatCSRng}$)
is the commutative refinement of a bounded lattice-semiring
(Class~\ref{def:bounded-lattice-semiring}): a bounded
lattice-semiring whose $\otimes$ is commutative.
\uses{def:bounded-lattice-semiring, def:comm-lattice-semiring}
\end{class}

A plain semiring needs no new name here: it is just a semiring. The
monotonicity requirements are not symmetric between the two
operations. Addition in a semiring is always commutative, so once
$\oplus$ is monotone on one side it is monotone on the other.
Multiplication need not be commutative, so $\otimes$'s two sides are
separate requirements. In a commutative lattice-semiring
(Class~\ref{def:comm-lattice-semiring}) the two sides merge. We
therefore split the classes the way Mathlib splits
\texttt{Semiring} and \texttt{CommSemiring}. A bounded commutative
lattice-semiring can be reached in either order: bound a commutative
one, or make a bounded one commutative. The three running instances
are all commutative; only the Boolean row is also bounded.

\begin{definition}[Probabilistic sum]
\label{def:psum}
\lean{NeSyCat.pSum}
\leanok
For $p,q \in \ProbS = [0,1]$, the \textbf{probabilistic sum}
$\mathrm{pSum}(p,q) := p+q-pq$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Probability is not a semiring]
\label{lem:prob-not-semiring}
$(\ProbS, \mathrm{pSum}, \cdot, 0, 1)$ (Definition~\ref{def:psum}), with
$p \oplus q := \mathrm{pSum}(p,q)$ and $p \otimes q := pq$, is
\emph{not} a semiring: there exist $p,q,r \in \ProbS$ with $p \otimes
(q \oplus r) \ne (p\otimes q) \oplus (p \otimes r)$, so $\otimes$ does
not distribute over $\oplus$ in general.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring, def:psum}
\lean{NeSyCat.prob_not_semiring}
\leanok
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Take $p=q=r=\tfrac12$:
\[
  p \otimes (q \oplus r) = \tfrac12\bigl(\tfrac12+\tfrac12-\tfrac14\bigr)
    = \tfrac38,
  \qquad
  (p\otimes q) \oplus (p \otimes r)
    = \tfrac14 \oplus \tfrac14
    = \tfrac14+\tfrac14-\tfrac1{16} = \tfrac{7}{16},
\]
and $\tfrac38 \ne \tfrac{7}{16}$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, $\ProbS$ with this $(\oplus,\otimes)$ is not a row of
Class~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}. It is the mass-one slice of the
\emph{lifted} Boolean De~Morgan family of
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}, read out as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-prob-readout}: a pair of commutative monoids,
not a semiring.

The library's three running instances are all commutative
($\mathrm{LatCSRng}$, Class~\ref{def:comm-lattice-semiring}). Below
they appear in their computational, finite-weight form: the carrier on
which the semiring monad runs. In this form only the Boolean row is
bounded ($\mathrm{BLatCSRng}$, Class~\ref{def:bounded-comm-lattice-semiring}).

\begin{instance}[Boolean row]
\label{inst:boolw-latcsrng}
\lean{NeSyCat.BoolW.instBLatCSRng}
\leanok
Carrier $\BoolS = \{0,1\}$, $\bot{=}0$, $\top{=}1$,
$\vee{=}\mathtt{or}$, $\wedge{=}\mathtt{and}$, $0{=}0$, $1{=}1$,
$\oplus{=}\mathtt{or}$, $\otimes{=}\mathtt{and}$: the only bounded
row of the three.
\uses{def:bounded-comm-lattice-semiring, lem:prob-not-semiring}
\end{instance}

\begin{instance}[Mass row]
\label{inst:massS-latcsrng}
\lean{NeSyCat.instLatCSRngNNReal}
\leanok
Carrier $\MassS$ (unbounded above), $\bot{=}0$, $\vee{=}\max$,
$\wedge{=}\min$, $0{=}0$, $1{=}1$, $\oplus{=}{+}$, $\otimes{=}\cdot$.
\uses{def:comm-lattice-semiring, lem:prob-not-semiring}
\end{instance}

\begin{instance}[Log row]
\label{inst:logS-latcsrng}
\lean{NeSyCat.instLatCSRngLogS}
\leanok
Carrier $\LogS$ (unbounded above), $\bot{=}{-\infty}$, $\vee{=}\max$,
$\wedge{=}\min$, $0{=}{-\infty}$, $1{=}0$, $\oplus{=}\lse$,
$\otimes{=}{+}$.
\uses{def:comm-lattice-semiring, lem:prob-not-semiring}
\end{instance}

The log row contains $-\infty$: it is both the bottom $\bot$ and the
unit $0$, since $0 = \log 0$. The mass and log rows have no top
element. Their completions $[0,\infty]$ and $[-\infty,\infty]$ do; we
treat those in a later section. The probability row
$(\ProbS,\, p+q-pq,\, \cdot)$, with $\max,\min$ as its lattice, is a
different case: by Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring} it is not a
semiring at all.

\begin{definition}[Semiring monad $\MS{S}$]
\label{def:semiring-monad}
\lean{NeSyCat.bind}
\leanok
Let $S = (S,\oplus,\otimes,0,1)$ be a semiring. The \textbf{semiring
monad} $\MS{S}$ sends a set $X$ to the set $\MS{S}X$ of finitely
supported functions $f \colon X \to S$, with
\[
  \Ret(x) := \delta_x, \quad \delta_x(x')=1 \text{ if } x'=x,\ 0
  \text{ otherwise},
  \qquad
  (f \bind k)(y) := \bigoplus_{x \in X} f(x) \otimes k(x)(y)
\]
for $k \colon X \to \MS{S}Y$.
\end{definition}

$\MS{S}X$ reads as the set of finitely-supported $S$-weighted formal
combinations of elements of $X$. The sum defining $f \bind k$ is finite,
since $f$ is finitely supported. It is taken in the left-to-right order
$f(x)\otimes k(x)(y)$ throughout. No step needs $\otimes$ to commute.

\begin{lemma}[Bind is matrix multiplication]
\label{lem:bind-matrix-mult}
\lean{NeSyCat.bind_apply}
\leanok
$\bind$ of Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} is matrix multiplication
over $S$: $f \colon X \to S$ and $k \colon X \to \MS{S}Y$ correspond to
a row vector and an $X \times Y$ matrix over $S$, and $(f \bind k)(y) =
\bigoplus_x f(x) \otimes k(x)(y)$ is exactly the $y$-th entry of their
product.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Immediate from unfolding the $\bind$ formula of
Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} against the matrix-product formula
$(fK)(y) = \bigoplus_x f(x)\otimes K(x,y)$ at $K(x,y) := k(x)(y)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Left unit law]
\label{lem:bind-left-unit}
\lean{NeSyCat.ret_bind}
\leanok
$\delta_x \bind k = k(x)$: binding a point mass at $x$ into $k$
returns $k(x)$.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Unfolding $\bind$ from Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}, the sum
$\bigoplus_{x'}\delta_x(x')\otimes k(x')(y)$ collapses to its single
nonzero term at $x'=x$ by the unit laws of $S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Associativity of bind]
\label{lem:bind-assoc}
\lean{NeSyCat.bind_assoc}
\leanok
$(f \bind k) \bind l = f \bind (\lambda x.\, k(x) \bind l)$.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Both sides expand, by unfolding $\bind$ twice, to a double sum over
$S$ that is rearranged into the other order by associativity and
commutativity of $\oplus$ together with associativity of $\otimes$
and distributivity of $\otimes$ over $\oplus$; commutativity of
$\otimes$ is never invoked.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Semiring monad laws]
\label{thm:semiring-monad-laws}
\lean{NeSyCat.semiring_monad_laws}
\leanok
For every semiring $S$, $(\MS{S}, \Ret, \bind)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} is a monad on $\mathrm{Set}$: the
left unit law (Lemma~\ref{lem:bind-left-unit}), the right unit law
$f \bind \Ret = f$, and associativity of $\bind$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bind-assoc}) hold for every, possibly noncommutative, $S$.
\uses{def:semiring-monad, lem:bind-left-unit, lem:bind-assoc}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Left unit and associativity are Lemmas~\ref{lem:bind-left-unit} and
\ref{lem:bind-assoc}. For the right unit law: unfolding $\bind$ from
Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}, the sum
$\bigoplus_x f(x)\otimes\delta_x(y)$ collapses to $f(y)$ by the unit
laws of $S$ (a distributivity-free identity, same shape as the left
unit law).
\end{proof}

\textbf{Mechanism.} Commutativity and associativity of $\oplus$ give
associativity and commutativity of independent binds. Associativity of
$\otimes$ gives associativity of chained binds. Distributivity of
$\otimes$ over $\oplus$ gives associativity of $\bind$ itself. None of
this needs $\otimes$ to commute.

\begin{theorem}[Semiring monad commutativity]
\label{thm:semiring-monad-commutative}
\lean{NeSyCat.dst_comm_iff}
\leanok
For a semiring $S$, define the two double-strength (interchange) maps
$\dstL, \dstR \colon \MS{S}X \times \MS{S}Y \to \MS{S}(X\times Y)$,
binding the two arguments in either order,
\[
  \dstL(f,g) := f \bind \lambda x.\, g \bind \lambda y.\, \Ret(x,y),
  \qquad
  \dstR(f,g) := g \bind \lambda y.\, f \bind \lambda x.\, \Ret(x,y),
\]
which unfold pointwise to $\dstL(f,g)(x,y) = f(x)\otimes g(y)$ and
$\dstR(f,g)(x,y) = g(y)\otimes f(x)$. Then $\MS{S}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws}) is a \emph{commutative} monad
($\dstL(f,g) = \dstR(f,g)$ for every $X,Y,f,g$) \emph{if and only if}
$S$ itself is commutative.
\uses{def:semiring-monad, thm:semiring-monad-laws}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Forward: $\otimes$ commuting makes the two pointwise
formulas $f(x)\otimes g(y)$ and $g(y)\otimes f(x)$ agree directly.
Converse: testing $\dstL=\dstR$ at $X=Y=\{*\}$ (a one-point set) against
the two point masses $\delta_a,\delta_b$ ($a,b\in S$) evaluates both
sides at $(*,*)$ to $a\otimes b$, resp.\ $b\otimes a$, recovering
$a\otimes b = b\otimes a$ for arbitrary $a,b$, so no weaker hypothesis
than full commutativity of $\otimes$ suffices; even the single instance
of $\dstL=\dstR$ at this one-point set already forces it.
\end{proof}

% Coumans--Jacobs, Lem.~23.
This is the first place in the section that needs
$\otimes$-commutativity. The monad laws of
Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws} hold for any semiring, but
the monad's own commutativity does not: it is equivalent to
$\otimes$-commutativity. Two later constructions depend on it.
The lifted connectives of Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}
bind several arguments and push forward along a Boolean
operation, so the order of those binds is irrelevant only when
the binds commute. The $\Do$-notation semantics of
Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation} likewise reorders
independent binds. The algebras of $\MS{S}$ are the
$S$-semimodules, a result of \citet{coumansJacobsScalars2013}. We
record this as a stretch target, since it has no Lean formalization
yet.

\begin{lemma}[Mass-one closure]
\label{lem:dist-closure}
\lean{NeSyCat.bind_mass_one}
\leanok
For the mass semiring monad $\Tmon = \MS{\MassS}$
(Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}), $\Ret$ has mass one, and
$\bind$ of mass-one families has mass one: if $\sum_x \rho(x) = 1$ and
$\sum_y k(x)(y) = 1$ for every $x$, then $\sum_y (\rho \bind k)(y) = 1$.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$\Ret(x)=\delta_x$ has mass
$\sum_{x'}\delta_x(x')=1$, and if $\sum_x\rho(x)=1$ and
$\sum_y k(x)(y)=1$ for every $x$ then
\[
  \sum_y (\rho \bind k)(y) = \sum_y\sum_x \rho(x)\,k(x)(y)
  = \sum_x \rho(x) \sum_y k(x)(y) = \sum_x \rho(x)\cdot 1 = 1.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Distribution monad]
\label{def:dist-monad}
\lean{NeSyCat.Dist}
\leanok
The \textbf{distribution monad} is the mass-one submonad of the mass
semiring monad,
\[
  \Dmon X := \{\, \rho \in \MS{\MassS} X \mid \textstyle\sum_x \rho(x) = 1 \,\}
  \;\subset\; \MS{\MassS} X \;=\; \Tmon X,
\]
well-defined by Lemma~\ref{lem:dist-closure}.
\uses{lem:dist-closure, def:semiring-monad}
\end{definition}

$\Dmon$ is not $\MS{\ProbS}$: $\ProbS$ is not a semiring
(Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring}), so $\MS{\ProbS}$ is not even
defined by Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}.

% a `def`-valued monad isomorphism (an `Equiv`), not a Prop-valued
% theorem -- same encoding-choice shape as `lift`/`twoSlot`/`distReadout`
% elsewhere in this document, so this item is a `definition` env, not a
% `lemma`, though its correctness still earns the proof below.
\begin{definition}[Log isomorphism]
\label{lem:log-iso}
\lean{NeSyCat.logTensEquiv}
\leanok
$\log \colon (\MassS, +, \cdot, 0, 1) \to (\LogS, \lse, +, -\infty, 0)$
is a semiring isomorphism ($e^{\lse(u,v)} = e^u+e^v$), and it
transports the monad $\MS{\MassS} = \Tmon$ pointwise (applying $\log$
to every weight) to an isomorphism of monads
$\Tmon \cong \LTmon = \MS{\LogS}$.
\uses{def:semiring-monad, thm:semiring-monad-laws}
\end{definition}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$\log$ is a bijection $\MassS \to \LogS$. It carries $+ \mapsto \lse$,
by the definition of $\lse$, and $\cdot \mapsto +$, the log of a
product. Hence it is a semiring isomorphism by
Class~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}. Applying it coordinatewise to a
finitely supported weight function commutes with $\Ret$, since both
sides are the indicator, or point mass. It also commutes with $\bind$:
this is a monad-law computation as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws}, carried through the semiring
isomorphism. So $\log$ is a monad isomorphism
$\MS{\MassS} \cong \MS{\LogS}$.
\end{proof}

From here on, $\otimes$ (rendered $\boxtimes$) always names the
categorical layer's object-level tensor of a CD or monoidal category.
It never names the logical or semiring round operations used above and

The theory is a stack of four signature and interpretation layers:
categorical, logical, domain, and grammatical. The last of these fixes

rule. It interprets that grammar in the Kleisli category of a strong
monad.

\begin{definition}[Categorical signature]
\label{def:categorical-signature}
\lean{NeSyCat.CatSignature}
\leanok
A \textbf{categorical signature} $\Sigma_\alpha$ consists of a
\textbf{category symbol} $\mathbf C$, an \textbf{actor symbol}
$\mathbf A$, and a \textbf{monad symbol} $\bigcirc$. The identity monad
symbol $\mathrm{Id}$ is left implicit, but can always be added
explicitly to a set of monad symbols
$\mathrm{Mon} := \{\mathrm{Id}, \bigcirc\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[CD category]
\label{def:cd-category}
\lean{NeSyCat.CDCategory}
\leanok
A \textbf{CD (copy-discard) category}
$(\mathcal C, \boxtimes_{\mathcal C}, \mathrm I_{\mathcal C}, \mathsf{copy}, \mathsf{del})$
is a symmetric monoidal category equipped with a commutative comonoid
$\mathsf{copy}_X \colon X \to X \boxtimes_{\mathcal C} X$,
$\mathsf{del}_X \colon X \to \mathrm I_{\mathcal C}$ on every object
$X$, compatible with $\boxtimes_{\mathcal C}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Categorical interpretation]
\label{def:categorical-interpretation}
\lean{NeSyCat.CatInterpretation}
\leanok
A \textbf{categorical interpretation} $\mathcal I_\alpha$ of a
categorical signature $\Sigma_\alpha$ (Definition~\ref{def:categorical-signature})
is given by a CD category
$(\mathcal C, \boxtimes_{\mathcal C}, \mathrm I_{\mathcal C}, \mathsf{copy}, \mathsf{del}) := \mathcal I(\mathbf C)$
(Definition~\ref{def:cd-category}) for the category symbol $\mathbf C$,
a monoidal category
$(\mathcal A, \boxtimes_{\mathcal A}, \mathrm I_{\mathcal A}) := \mathcal I(\mathbf A)$
for the actor symbol $\mathbf A$, acting on $\mathcal C$ as an
\textbf{actegory} with action
$\actop \colon \mathcal A \times \mathcal C \to \mathcal C$, and a
monad $\mathcal M := \mathcal I(\bigcirc)$ on $\mathcal C$ for the
monad symbol $\bigcirc$. The identity monad symbol $\mathrm{Id}$ is
always interpreted by the identity functor on $\mathcal C$.
\uses{def:categorical-signature, def:cd-category}
\end{definition}

Three categorical interpretations recur as running examples of
Definition~\ref{def:categorical-interpretation}.
\textbf{Set/Tens/Identity} takes the set-theoretic category
$\mathcal C := \mathbf{Set}$ for $\mathbf C$, the monoidal category
$\mathcal A := \mathbf{Tens}$ for $\mathbf A$, and the identity monad
$\mathcal M := \mathrm{Identity}$ for $\bigcirc$. \textbf{Set/Tens/Dist}
takes the measure-theoretic category $\mathcal C := \mathbf{Set}$,
$\mathcal A := \mathbf{Tens}$, and the distribution monad
$\mathcal M := \mathrm{Dist}$ for $\bigcirc$. \textbf{Tens/Tens/LogVec}
takes the geometric category $\mathcal C := \mathbf{Tens}$,
$\mathcal A := \mathbf{Tens}$, and the monad
$\mathcal M := \mathrm{LogVec}$ for $\bigcirc$.

\subsection{Truth-value structures}  % lean-dir: LogicalLayer/TruthStructures
\label{sec:truth-structures}

The lifted connective family of a truth space
(Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}, Section~\ref{sec:truth-spaces}
onward) is \emph{not} a semiring (Lemma~\ref{lem:copying-fails},
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-layers}), but it is a bounded lattice carrying
two monoids. This section defines that structure abstractly, under
names from the library's own naming scheme (\textbf{BLat2Mon} and its
refinements), and proves which linearity and De~Morgan laws such a
structure can carry. Each running row (Boolean, probability, mass,
log) then falls into one of these classes. The connective and
constant conventions above are assumed throughout, not re-explained.

The BLat2Mon family below borrows some of its vocabulary from linear
logic, due to \citet{girardLinearLogic1987}.
% Apparatus (C2-E5): provenance credit, moved verbatim.
% The BLat2Mon family below is distilled from [NeSy26, App.~A]'s
% lifted-connective family, with vocabulary borrowed from linear logic
% [Girard 1987].

\begin{class}[Two-monoid bounded lattice]
\label{def:blat2mon}
\lean{NeSyCat.BLat2Mon}
\leanok
A \textbf{BLat2Mon} is a set carrying a bounded lattice
$(\wedge,\vee,\bot,\top)$ together with two monoid structures
$(\oplus,\dzero)$ and $(\otimes,\done)$.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring}
\end{class}

Unlike a lattice-semiring (Class~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}), no
distributivity of any kind is assumed between the three structures.

\begin{class}[Commutative two-monoid bounded lattice]
\label{def:blat2cmon}
\lean{NeSyCat.BLat2CMon}
\leanok
A \textbf{BLat2CMon} is a BLat2Mon (Class~\ref{def:blat2mon}) whose
two monoids $(\oplus,\dzero)$ and $(\otimes,\done)$ are both commutative.
\uses{def:blat2mon}
\end{class}

\begin{class}[Linear two-monoid lattice]
\label{def:lin-blat2mon}
\lean{NeSyCat.LinBLat2Mon}
\leanok
A \textbf{LinBLat2Mon} is a BLat2Mon (Class~\ref{def:blat2mon}) in
which $\otimes$ preserves finite joins in each argument and $\oplus$
preserves finite meets in each argument:
\begin{itemize}
\item binary: $p \otimes (q \vee r) = (p \otimes q) \vee (p \otimes r)$ and
  $(q \vee r) \otimes p = (q \otimes p) \vee (r \otimes p)$; dually
  $p \oplus (q \wedge r) = (p \oplus q) \wedge (p \oplus r)$ and
  $(q \wedge r) \oplus p = (q \oplus p) \wedge (r \oplus p)$;
\item nullary: $p \otimes \bot = \bot = \bot \otimes p$ (annihilation) and
  $p \oplus \top = \top = \top \oplus p$ (absorption).
\end{itemize}
\uses{def:blat2mon}
\end{class}

\begin{class}[Commutative linear two-monoid lattice]
\label{def:lin-blat2cmon}
\lean{NeSyCat.LinBLat2CMon}
\leanok
A \textbf{LinBLat2CMon} is a BLat2CMon (Class~\ref{def:blat2cmon})
satisfying the linear laws of Class~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon}.
\uses{def:blat2cmon, def:lin-blat2mon}
\end{class}

In the commutative case, the one-sided version of each linear law
suffices. The other side follows from commutativity.

Classes~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon} and~\ref{def:lin-blat2cmon} are exactly
linear logic's four linear distributions~\citep{girardLinearLogic1987}
($\otimes$ over $\oplus$, and linear logic's own multiplicative
disjunction over its own additive conjunction, together with their
units). The linear-logic dictionary later in this section spells out
the correspondence with linear logic's own vocabulary in full.
% Apparatus (C2-E5): provenance credit, moved verbatim.
% [Girard 1987]

\begin{lemma}[Linearity gives monotonicity]
\label{lem:lin-monotone}
\lean{NeSyCat.otimes_le_otimes_left}
\leanok
In a LinBLat2Mon (Class~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon}), $\otimes$ and
$\oplus$ are monotone in each argument.
\uses{def:lin-blat2mon}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
If $p \le q$ then $q = p \vee q$, so
$q \otimes r = (p \vee q) \otimes r = (p \otimes r) \vee (q \otimes r) \ge
p \otimes r$; the other argument, and $\oplus$ (via $p = p \wedge q$ and
meet-preservation), are dual.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lax dual preservations]
\label{lem:lin-lax-duals}
\lean{NeSyCat.otimes_inf_le}
\leanok
In a LinBLat2Mon, the dual preservations hold laxly:
$p \otimes (q \wedge r) \le (p \otimes q) \wedge (p \otimes r)$ and
$(p \oplus q) \vee (p \oplus r) \le p \oplus (q \vee r)$, and likewise in
the other argument.
\uses{lem:lin-monotone}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$q \wedge r \le q$ and $q \wedge r \le r$, so monotonicity
(Lemma~\ref{lem:lin-monotone}) gives $p \otimes (q \wedge r) \le p \otimes q$
and $\le p \otimes r$, hence $\le$ their meet; dually for $\oplus$.
\end{proof}

\begin{class}[De Morgan structure]
\label{def:dm-structure}
\lean{NeSyCat.DMStructure}
\leanok
A \textbf{DM structure} on a BLat2Mon (Class~\ref{def:blat2mon})
is a map $\neg$ satisfying (i) $\neg\neg p = p$; (ii)
$p \le q \Rightarrow \neg q \le \neg p$; (iii)
$\neg(p \otimes q) = \neg q \oplus \neg p$.
\uses{def:blat2mon}
\end{class}

In the commutative case, (iii) reads $\neg p \oplus \neg q$. This is
linear negation~\citep{girardLinearLogic1987}, axiomatized minimally.
Theorem~\ref{thm:dm-presentations} below compares the presentations.
% Apparatus (C2-E5): provenance credit, moved verbatim.
% This is linear negation [Girard 1987].

\begin{lemma}[Negation is an order anti-automorphism]
\label{lem:dm-lattice-laws}
\lean{NeSyCat.dneg_inf}
\leanok
For a DM structure (Class~\ref{def:dm-structure}),
$\neg(p \wedge q) = \neg p \vee \neg q$,
$\neg(p \vee q) = \neg p \wedge \neg q$, $\neg\bot = \top$,
$\neg\top = \bot$.
\uses{def:dm-structure}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
By (i) and (ii), $\neg$ is an order-reversing bijection, and an
order-reversing bijection carries meets to joins, joins to meets, and
each bound to the other.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The dual De Morgan law]
\label{lem:dm-dual-law}
\lean{NeSyCat.dneg_oplus}
\leanok
For a DM structure, $\neg(p \oplus q) = \neg q \otimes \neg p$.
\uses{def:dm-structure}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Applying (iii) at $\neg q, \neg p$ and using (i):
$\neg(\neg q \otimes \neg p) = \neg\neg p \oplus \neg\neg q = p \oplus q$;
applying $\neg$ to both sides and using (i) again gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Negation swaps the monoid units]
\label{lem:dm-unit-swap}
\lean{NeSyCat.dneg_done}
\leanok
For a DM structure, $\neg\done = \dzero$ and $\neg\dzero = \done$.
\uses{def:dm-structure}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
For every $p$, by (iii) and (i):
$\neg\done \oplus p = \neg\done \oplus \neg\neg p = \neg(\neg p \otimes \done)
= \neg\neg p = p$, and symmetrically
$p \oplus \neg\done = \neg(\done \otimes \neg p) = p$; so $\neg\done$ is a
two-sided $\oplus$-unit, and monoid units are unique, giving
$\neg\done = \dzero$. Applying (i): $\neg\dzero = \neg\neg\done =
\done$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Presentations of De Morgan structure]
\label{thm:dm-presentations}
\lean{NeSyCat.dm_presentations}
\leanok
On a BLat2Mon, let $\neg$ satisfy (i) $\neg\neg p = p$ and (iii)
$\neg(p \otimes q) = \neg q \oplus \neg p$. Then the following are
equivalent, and each yields the full De~Morgan calculus of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:dm-lattice-laws}, \ref{lem:dm-dual-law} and
\ref{lem:dm-unit-swap}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\neg$ is antitone;
\item[(b)] $\neg(p \wedge q) = \neg p \vee \neg q$ for all $p, q$;
\item[(c)] the full list: both lattice De~Morgan pairs, both monoid
  De~Morgan laws, bound swap, unit swap, and antitonicity.
\end{enumerate}
\uses{lem:dm-lattice-laws, lem:dm-dual-law, lem:dm-unit-swap}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
(a)$\Rightarrow$(c) is Lemmas~\ref{lem:dm-lattice-laws},
\ref{lem:dm-dual-law} and \ref{lem:dm-unit-swap}. (c)$\Rightarrow$(b)
is immediate. (b)$\Rightarrow$(a): if $p \le q$ then $p = p \wedge q$,
so $\neg p = \neg(p \wedge q) = \neg p \vee \neg q \ge \neg q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Negation maps invertibles to invertibles]
\label{lem:dm-maps-units}
\lean{NeSyCat.dneg_maps_units}
\leanok
For a DM structure, if $p$ is $\otimes$-invertible then $\neg p$ is
$\oplus$-invertible.
\uses{def:dm-structure, lem:dm-unit-swap}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Let $p \otimes q = \done = q \otimes p$. Applying $\neg$ and (iii):
$\neg q \oplus \neg p = \neg\done = \dzero$ and, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:dm-unit-swap}, $\neg p \oplus \neg q = \dzero$, so
$\neg q$ is a two-sided $\oplus$-inverse of $\neg p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{class}[ZeroBot]
\label{def:zero-bot}
\lean{NeSyCat.ZeroBot}
\leanok
For a BLat2Mon (Class~\ref{def:blat2mon}), \textbf{ZeroBot}
demands $\dzero = \bot$.
\uses{def:blat2mon}
\end{class}

\begin{class}[OneTop]
\label{def:one-top}
\lean{NeSyCat.OneTop}
\leanok
For a BLat2Mon (Class~\ref{def:blat2mon}), \textbf{OneTop}
demands $\done = \top$.
\uses{def:blat2mon}
\end{class}

\begin{class}[UnitBounds]
\label{def:unit-bounds}
\lean{NeSyCat.UnitBounds}
\leanok
For a BLat2Mon (Class~\ref{def:blat2mon}), \textbf{UnitBounds}
demands both ZeroBot (Class~\ref{def:zero-bot}) and OneTop
(Class~\ref{def:one-top}).
\uses{def:zero-bot, def:one-top}
\end{class}

Together these collapse the four constants of the logical/truth-value

\begin{lemma}[Unit bounds via mixed absorption]
\label{lem:dualabsorb-decomposition}
\lean{NeSyCat.done_eq_top_iff_otimes_le_inf}
\leanok
In a LinBLat2Mon (Class~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\done = \top$ iff $p \otimes q \le p \wedge q$ for all
  $p,q$ iff the mixed absorption $p \vee (p \otimes q) = p$ holds;
\item[(ii)] $\dzero = \bot$ iff $p \vee q \le p \oplus q$ for all $p,q$
  iff the mixed absorption $p \wedge (p \oplus q) = p$ holds.
\end{enumerate}
\uses{lem:lin-monotone, def:zero-bot, def:one-top}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
(i) If $\done = \top$: monotonicity gives
$p \otimes q \le p \otimes \top = p \otimes \done = p$ and likewise
$p \otimes q \le \top \otimes q = q$, hence $\le p \wedge q$. Conversely,
taking $p = \done$: $q = \done \otimes q \le \done \wedge q \le \done$
for all $q$, so $\top \le \done$. The absorption form is the standard
rewriting of $p \otimes q \le p$. (ii) is dual: if $\dzero = \bot$ then
$p = p \oplus \dzero = p \oplus \bot \le p \oplus q$ and symmetrically,
hence $p \vee q \le p \oplus q$; conversely $q = \dzero$ gives
$p \vee \dzero \le p$, so $\dzero \le p$ for all $p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The four-connective chain (MIX)]
\label{lem:mix}
\lean{NeSyCat.mix_chain}
\leanok
In a LinBLat2Mon with UnitBounds (Class~\ref{def:unit-bounds}),
the four binary connectives form a chain:
$p \otimes q \le p \wedge q \le p \vee q \le p \oplus q$. In particular
MIX holds: $p \otimes q \le p \oplus q$.
\uses{lem:dualabsorb-decomposition, def:unit-bounds}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The outer inequalities are Lemma~\ref{lem:dualabsorb-decomposition}(i)
and (ii); the middle one is the lattice.
\end{proof}

This is the MIX rule~\citep{girardLinearLogic1987}.

\begin{theorem}[Chains are linear]
\label{thm:chain-lin}
\lean{NeSyCat.chain_binop_sup_right}
\leanok
Let $L$ be a lattice, not necessarily bounded, carrying two monoid
structures $(\oplus,\dzero)$ and $(\otimes,\done)$ (a Lat2Mon), whose
order is a chain (total order), and suppose $\otimes$ and $\oplus$ are
monotone in each argument. Then the four binary linear laws of
Class~\ref{def:lin-blat2mon} hold.
\uses{def:lin-blat2mon}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
On a chain, $q \vee r \in \{q, r\}$; say $q \le r$, so
$q \vee r = r$. Then $p \otimes (q \vee r) = p \otimes r$, while
monotonicity gives $p \otimes q \le p \otimes r$, so
$(p \otimes q) \vee (p \otimes r) = p \otimes r$ as well; the other argument
and the $\oplus/\wedge$ laws are dual.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Chain construction of a LinBLat2Mon]
\label{def:chain-lin-unitbounds}
\lean{NeSyCat.LinBLat2Mon.ofChain}
\leanok
For $L$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-lin}, if $L$ is additionally
bounded and UnitBounds (Class~\ref{def:unit-bounds}) holds, the
nullary laws hold too (annihilation from OneTop: $p \otimes \bot \le
\top \otimes \bot = \done \otimes \bot = \bot$; absorption from
ZeroBot: $\top = \dzero \oplus \top \le p \oplus \top$), so the
structure packages into a LinBLat2Mon witness.
\uses{thm:chain-lin, def:unit-bounds}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The Boolean row]
\label{lem:bool-truth-structure}
\lean{NeSyCat.BoolW.oplus_eq_sup}
\leanok
$\BoolS$ with its lattice $(\wedge,\vee,0,1)$, monoids
$(\oplus,\dzero) = (\vee,0)$ and $(\otimes,\done) = (\wedge,1)$, and
$\neg$ is a LinBLat2CMon (Class~\ref{def:lin-blat2cmon}) with DM
structure (Class~\ref{def:dm-structure}) and UnitBounds
(Class~\ref{def:unit-bounds}); the connective pairs collapse,
$\otimes = \wedge$ and $\oplus = \vee$: the idempotent case.
\uses{def:lin-blat2cmon, def:dm-structure, def:unit-bounds,
thm:chain-lin}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A finite check (or: classical Boolean algebra); linearity via
Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-lin} on the two-element chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The probability row]
\label{lem:unit-interval-truth-structure}
\lean{NeSyCat.unitInterval.coe_oplus}
\leanok
$\ProbS = [0,1]$ with lattice $(\min,\max,0,1)$, monoids
$(\oplus,\dzero) = (p+q-pq,\ 0)$ and $(\otimes,\done) = (pq,\ 1)$, and
$\neg p = 1-p$ is a LinBLat2CMon with DM structure and UnitBounds.
\uses{def:lin-blat2cmon, def:dm-structure, def:unit-bounds,
thm:chain-lin, lem:prob-not-semiring}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Closure: $p+q-pq = 1-(1-p)(1-q) \in [0,1]$. Monoid laws: $\otimes$ is
real multiplication; $\oplus$ is its conjugate under the involution
$x \mapsto 1-x$, hence associative, commutative, with unit
$1-1=0$. Both are monotone (partial derivatives $\ge 0$ on the square,
or directly: $q \le r$ gives $pq \le pr$ and
$p+q-pq = q(1-p)+p \le r(1-p)+p$), so Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-lin}
applies: Lin holds. DM: $1-x$ is involutive and antitone, and
$\neg(p \otimes q) = 1-pq = (1-p)+(1-q)-(1-p)(1-q) = \neg p \oplus \neg q$.
UnitBounds: the $\oplus$-unit, $0$, is the order's $\bot$, and the
$\otimes$-unit, $1$, is $\top$.
\end{proof}

This is precisely the structure the non-semiring row of
Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring} does carry.

\begin{theorem}[No De Morgan structure on the mass row]
\label{thm:no-dm-mass}
\lean{NeSyCat.no_antitone_involution_nnreal}
\leanok
On $\MassS = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ with its usual order there is no
involutive antitone map.
\uses{def:dm-structure}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Suppose $\neg$ is involutive and antitone. For every $p$,
$\neg p \ge 0$, so applying $\neg$: $p = \neg\neg p \le \neg 0$. Thus
$\neg 0$ is a top element, but $\MassS$ has none
($\neg 0 + 1 > \neg 0$).
\end{proof}

Hence no two-monoid structure over the mass row carries a DM structure
(Class~\ref{def:dm-structure}).

\begin{theorem}[No De Morgan structure on the log row]
\label{thm:no-dm-log}
\lean{NeSyCat.no_antitone_involution_logS}
\leanok
The same holds for $\LogS = \mathbb{R} \cup \{-\infty\}$: bottom
$-\infty$, no top, hence no involutive antitone map.
\uses{thm:no-dm-mass, lem:log-iso}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
As in Theorem~\ref{thm:no-dm-mass}, $\neg(-\infty)$ would be a top
element.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm:no-dm-mass} and \ref{thm:no-dm-log} rule out a
negation on the mass and log rows because each order has a bottom but
no top. Adding $\infty$ removes that reason: on $[0,\infty]$ and
$[-\infty,\infty]$ the order looks the same upside down, so the
argument no longer applies. That does not mean a negation exists
there. We come back to the question in a later section, where these
two carriers get their own treatment.

\begin{theorem}[The mass square is not linear]
\label{thm:square-not-lin}
\lean{NeSyCat.otimesSq_not_lin}
\leanok
On $\MassS^2$ with the order family (slot $0$ reversed,
Definition~\ref{def:order-family}) and the lifted operations of
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}, the slot swap
$\neg(w_0,w_1) = (w_1,w_0)$ is a DM structure
(Class~\ref{def:dm-structure}) and both monoids are commutative,
but $\otimes$ is not monotone; hence, by Lemma~\ref{lem:lin-monotone},
the linear laws fail: the square rows are BLat2CMon-with-DM territory,
not Lin.
\uses{def:order-family, lem:lifted-mass, def:blat2cmon, def:dm-structure,
lem:lin-monotone}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
DM: the swap is involutive; it is antitone for the slot-0-reversed
order; and $\neg(w \otimes v) = (w_1v_1,\ w_0v_0 + w_0v_1 + w_1v_0) =
\neg w \oplus \neg v$ by the formulas of Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}.
Non-monotonicity: take $p = (1,0)$, $r = (5,0)$, $q = (0,10)$. Then
$r \le q$ (slot 1: $0 \le 10$; slot 0 reversed: $5 \ge 0$), but
$p \otimes r = (5, 0)$ and $p \otimes q = (10, 0)$, and
$(5,0) \le (10,0)$ fails in the reversed slot ($5 \ge 10$ is false).
The mechanism is the cross term $w_0v_1$ in slot 0 of $\otimes$: raising
the \emph{for}-weight $v_1$ raises the \emph{against}-slot of the
output. Neither operation is monotone: $\oplus$ fails dually, via the
cross term $w_1v_0$ in its slot 1, as it must, since an antitone
involution exchanging the two operations
(Lemma~\ref{lem:dm-dual-law}) makes $\oplus$ monotone if and only if
$\otimes$ is. The counterexample persists unchanged in the bounded
completion $[0,\infty]^2$, so no bounded extension of this order
family is Lin either.
\end{proof}

Our $\vee$ and $\wedge$ are linear logic's own \emph{additive}
connectives. Our $\otimes$ coincides with linear logic's own
multiplicative conjunction. Our $\oplus$ is linear logic's
\emph{multiplicative} disjunction; it is \textbf{not} linear logic's
own \emph{additive} disjunction, which is our $\vee$. That mismatch is
why the dictionary below exists.

\begin{abbreviation}[Truth space]
\label{abbr:truth-space}
\lean{NeSyCat.TruthSpace}
\leanok
For a semiring monad $\MS{S}$ (Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}) or
$\Idmon$, the \textbf{truth space} is $\MS{S}\BoolS$ (resp.\
$\Idmon\,\BoolS = \BoolS$): the monad applied to the Boolean carrier,
with Boolean value $0$ read as \emph{False} and $1$ as \emph{True}.
\uses{def:semiring-monad}
\end{abbreviation}

Abbreviation~\ref{abbr:truth-space} is stated for a general semiring
monad $\MS{S}$. The four instances $\Idmon, \Dmon, \Tmon, \LTmon$ are
the ones used throughout this document.

\begin{definition}[Boolean two-slot readout]
\label{def:two-slot}
\lean{NeSyCat.twoSlot}
\leanok
For $\Idmon,\Tmon,\LTmon$ (any semiring monad $\MS{S}$ applied to
$\BoolS$), the \textbf{two-slot readout} is the equivalence $\MS{S}
\BoolS \cong S^2$, $w \mapsto (w(0), w(1))$: $\Idmon\,\BoolS = \BoolS$
reads out as itself; $\Tmon\,\BoolS \cong \MassS^2$ and
$\LTmon\,\BoolS \cong \LogS^2$ forget nothing (no mass-one
constraint), the log case the image of the mass case under
Definition~\ref{lem:log-iso}.
\uses{abbr:truth-space, lem:log-iso}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Distribution readout]
\label{def:dist-readout}
\lean{NeSyCat.distReadout}
\leanok
For $\Dmon\,\BoolS$, the \textbf{distribution readout} is the
equivalence $\Dmon\,\BoolS \cong \ProbS$, $p \mapsto p(1)$, forgetting
the redundant coefficient $p(0) = 1 - p(1)$ (the constraint
$p(0)+p(1)=1$ of Definition~\ref{def:dist-monad}); immediate from that
constraint eliminating $p(0)$.
\uses{abbr:truth-space, def:dist-monad}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Lifted connective]
\label{def:lifted-connective}
\lean{NeSyCat.lift}
\leanok
Let $* \colon \BoolS^n \to \BoolS$ be an $n$-ary Boolean operation and
$\MS{S}$ a semiring monad. The \textbf{lifted connective}
$*_{\MS{S}} \colon (\MS{S}\BoolS)^n \to \MS{S}\BoolS$ is
\[
  *_{\MS{S}}(w_1,\ldots,w_n)
  := w_1 \bind \lambda u_1.\, \cdots\, w_n \bind \lambda u_n.\,
     \Ret(*(u_1,\ldots,u_n)).
\]
\uses{abbr:truth-space}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Lifted connective via strength]
\label{lem:lifted-connective-strength}
\lean{NeSyCat.lift_eq_dstN_bind}
\leanok
$*_{\MS{S}}(w_1,\ldots,w_n)$ of Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}
equals $\mathrm{dst}(w_1,\ldots,w_n) \bind (* \seq \Ret)$, where
$\mathrm{dst} \colon (\MS{S}\BoolS)^n \to \MS{S}(\BoolS^n)$ is the
$n$-ary tensorial strength pairing the $n$ arguments into one element
of $\MS{S}(\BoolS^n)$.
\uses{def:lifted-connective}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Both sides bind each of $w_1,\ldots,w_n$ in turn against $* \seq \Ret$;
this is exactly the definition of the $n$-ary strength $\mathrm{dst}$
as the iterated bind pairing the arguments.
\end{proof}

Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} is stated for a general $n$-ary
Boolean operation via the $n$-ary strength; the binary cases
$\wedge,\vee,\oplus,\otimes$ and the unary $\neg$ are the ones used
throughout this document.

\begin{lemma}[Lifted connectives, mass coordinates]
\label{lem:lifted-mass}
\lean{NeSyCat.twoSlot_otimesM_mass}
\leanok
On $\Tmon\,\BoolS \cong \MassS^2$, with coordinates $(w_0,w_1)$, slot
$0$ is the weight against and slot $1$ is the weight for. The lifted
family of Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} is
\[
  w \otimes v = \bigl(w_0v_0+w_0v_1+w_1v_0,\ w_1v_1\bigr), \qquad
  w \oplus v = \bigl(w_0v_0,\ w_0v_1+w_1v_0+w_1v_1\bigr), \qquad
  \neg w = (w_1, w_0),
\]
with units $\Ret(0) = (1,0)$ (the $\oplus$-identity, i.e.\ certain
False) and $\Ret(1) = (0,1)$ (the $\otimes$-identity, i.e.\ certain
True). The order family
(Definition~\ref{def:order-family}) gives
$w \wedge v = (\max(w_0,v_0), \min(w_1,v_1))$,
$w \vee v = (\min(w_0,v_0), \max(w_1,v_1))$.
\uses{def:lifted-connective, def:order-family}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Direct expansion of Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}
at $*\in\{\wedge,\vee,\neg\}$ using $\bind$ of
Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} for $S=\MassS$: the entry with
$*(1,1)=1$ collects the product $w_1v_1$ into slot $1$ and the three
entries with $*=0$ sum into slot $0$ (dually for $\oplus$); the unit
values are the (unique) identities of $\oplus,\otimes$ found by solving
$e\oplus w = w$, resp.\ $e\otimes w = w$, for all $w$.
\end{proof}

On the carrier $\MassS$ itself (unbounded), $\MassS^2$ acquires bounds
$\bot=(\infty,0)$, $\top=(0,\infty)$ only after adjoining $\infty$. We
treat the bounded completion in a later section, with the completion
instances.

\begin{lemma}[Lifted connectives, log coordinates]
\label{lem:lifted-log}
\lean{NeSyCat.twoSlot_otimesM_log}
\leanok
On $\LTmon\,\BoolS \cong \LogS^2$ (coordinates $(a_0,a_1)$, the
$\log$-image of Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass} under
Definition~\ref{lem:log-iso}, products becoming sums and sums becoming
$\lse$):
\[
  a \otimes b = \bigl(\lse(a_0{+}b_0,\,a_0{+}b_1,\,a_1{+}b_0),\ a_1{+}b_1\bigr),
  \qquad
  a \oplus b = \bigl(a_0{+}b_0,\ \lse(a_0{+}b_1,\,a_1{+}b_0,\,a_1{+}b_1)\bigr),
\]
\[
  \neg a = (a_1,a_0), \qquad
  \Ret(0)=(0,-\infty), \qquad \Ret(1)=(-\infty,0),
\]
\begin{gather*}
  a \wedge b = (\max(a_0,b_0), \min(a_1,b_1)), \qquad
  a \vee b = (\min(a_0,b_0), \max(a_1,b_1)), \\
  \bot=(\infty,-\infty),\ \top=(-\infty,\infty).
\end{gather*}
\uses{lem:lifted-mass, lem:log-iso}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Apply Definition~\ref{lem:log-iso} coordinatewise to
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}: $\log$ of a product is a sum, $\log$ of a
sum is $\lse$ of the logs, and $\log$ is order-preserving so
$\max,\min$ pass through unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lifted connectives, the {$[0,1]$} readout is derived]
\label{lem:lifted-prob-readout}
\lean{NeSyCat.distReadout_otimesD}
\leanok
Under the $\Dmon$ readout $p := w_1$ (so $w_0 = 1-p$, the mass-one
slice $Z(w) = w_0+w_1=1$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}), the lifted
family restricts to
\[
  p \otimes q = pq, \qquad p \oplus q = p+q-pq, \qquad \neg p = 1-p,
\]
\uses{lem:lifted-mass, def:dist-monad}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Substitute $w_0=1-p,\ w_1=p,\ v_0=1-q,\ v_1=q$ into
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}: $w_1v_1=pq$; the $\oplus$-slot
$w_0v_1+w_1v_0+w_1v_1 = (1-p)q+p(1-q)+pq = p+q-pq$; and
$\neg w = (w_1,w_0)=(p,1-p)$ read out at slot $1$ of the swapped pair
is $1-p$.
\end{proof}

This is exactly the operations of the (non-semiring) $\ProbS$ row of
Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring}. They are derived from binding two
independent truth values and pushing forward along the Boolean
operation, not stipulated.

\begin{definition}[Order family]
\label{def:order-family}
\lean{NeSyCat.orderedTwoSlot}
\leanok
For a lattice-semiring $S$ (Class~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}), with
coordinates $(w_0,w_1)$ as in Definition~\ref{def:two-slot}, the
\textbf{order family} on $\MS{S}\BoolS \cong S^2$ is the order
\[
  w \le v \quad :\iff \quad w_1 \le_S v_1 \ \text{and}\ w_0 \ge_S v_0,
\]
componentwise in $S$'s own order, with slot $0$ reversed. Its meet
$\wedge$ and join $\vee$ are the meet and join of this order. Its
bounds are $\bot = (\top_S, \bot_S)$, $\top = (\bot_S, \top_S)$, where
they exist in $S$, after adjoining $\infty$ to $S$ if needed.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring, abbr:truth-space}
\end{definition}

The order family of Definition~\ref{def:order-family} is induced by
the order of $S$, not by the monad structure of $\MS{S}$. It is stated
for a general lattice-semiring $S$; Lemmas~\ref{lem:lifted-mass} and
\ref{lem:lifted-log} work it out concretely for $S \in \{\MassS,
\LogS\}$.

\subsection{Three layers}  % lean-dir: LogicalLayer/ThreeLayers
\label{sec:three-layers}

% Apparatus (C2-E5): provenance credit, moved verbatim.
% This section is due to [NeSy26, App.~A] throughout.

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise]
\label{lem:pointwise}
\lean{NeSyCat.twoSlot_pointwiseMul}
\leanok
For a lattice-semiring $S$, $\MS{S}(\BoolS) = S^{\BoolS} \cong S\times
S$ carries all scalar operations ($\oplus,\otimes,0,1$) and the order
($\wedge,\vee,\bot,\top,\le$) componentwise, and a power of a
lattice-semiring is again a lattice-semiring.
\uses{def:lattice-semiring, def:comm-lattice-semiring, def:bounded-lattice-semiring, def:bounded-comm-lattice-semiring, def:semiring-monad}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$S^{\BoolS}$ is the product of two copies of $S$ in the
variety of lattice-semirings (a variety defined by the semiring
equations plus the monotonicity axioms of
Class~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}, all of which are preserved by
products); all structure is inherited componentwise.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Linear laws lift]
\label{lem:linear-lift}
\lean{NeSyCat.otimesM_assoc}
\leanok
For a commutative semiring $S$, the lifted family
$(\MS{S}\BoolS, \oplus, \Ret(0))$ and $(\MS{S}\BoolS, \otimes, \Ret(1))$ of
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} are commutative monoids, and
$\neg$ is a De~Morgan involution between them: $\neg\neg w = w$,
$\neg \Ret(0) = \Ret(1)$, and
$\neg(w \oplus v) = \neg w \otimes \neg v$,
$\neg(w \otimes v) = \neg v \oplus \neg w$.
\uses{def:lifted-connective, lem:pointwise, thm:semiring-monad-commutative}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Directly from Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass} (equivalently
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-log}). Associativity, commutativity, and the unit
laws of $\oplus,\otimes$ each involve every variable exactly once per
side of the defining equation. Hence they expand, via
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective}'s bind chain, to associativity,
commutativity, and the unit laws of $S$ itself
(Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}). Commutativity of $\oplus,\otimes$
specifically needs the two independent binds building the lifted
connective from its two arguments to agree in either order, i.e.\
$\dstL = \dstR$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-commutative}). That is
exactly where commutativity of $S$ enters. The De~Morgan identities are
a direct check on the explicit formulas of Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-lift} is stated at these concrete instances, not
as a general theorem over equation schemas where each variable occurs
exactly once per side; this library records the theorem only at the
instances it uses, not as a schema-level meta-theorem.

\begin{lemma}[Copying laws do not lift]
\label{lem:copying-fails}
\lean{NeSyCat.exists_otimes_ne_self}
\leanok
Equations that duplicate a variable (idempotence, absorption, lattice
distributivity) fail for the lifted family in general: in
$\Dmon\,\BoolS \cong \ProbS$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-prob-readout}),
there exists $p \in (0,1)$ with $p \otimes p \ne p$, so $\otimes$ is not
idempotent.
\uses{lem:lifted-prob-readout}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Take the fair-coin witness $p=\tfrac12$: $p \otimes p = p^2 \ne p$, since
$p^2=p$ iff $p\in\{0,1\}$.
\end{proof}

The lift binds its two arguments separately, so $p \otimes p$ treats
the two copies of $p$ as independent samples and multiplies them: the
result is $p \cdot p$, not $p$. Only $0$ and $1$ are fixed by squaring,
so on any carrier with values strictly between them the connective is
not idempotent.

\begin{theorem}[Three layers of lattice semirings]
\label{thm:three-layers}
\lean{NeSyCat.order_iff_inf_eq_left}
\leanok
Let $S$ be a lattice-semiring and apply $\MS{S}$ to the lattice-semiring
$\BoolS$. Then $\MS{S}\BoolS$ decomposes into three independent
contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{pointwise}: $S^{\BoolS}$ is again a lattice-semiring
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise}), so the construction closes and iterates:
  $\MS{S}(\BoolS)$ can serve as the weight semiring of a next layer for
  $S \in \{\BoolS, \MassS, \LogS\}$ (not $\ProbS$, which is excluded by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:prob-not-semiring});
\item[(ii)] \emph{linear/lifted}: the family $\otimes,\oplus,\neg$ with
  units $\Ret(0),\Ret(1)$ satisfies every linear Boolean law and no
  copying law (Lemmas~\ref{lem:linear-lift}, \ref{lem:copying-fails}):
  two commutative monoids with a De~Morgan involution, not a semiring;
\item[(iii)] \emph{order}: the family $\wedge,\vee,\bot,\top$ of
  Definition~\ref{def:order-family} is induced by the order of $S$,
  componentwise with the $0$-slot reversed, not by the monad.
\end{enumerate}
The copying axioms are exactly the order-generating ones: $u \le v$
iff $u \wedge v = u$. So (ii) and (iii) are disjoint contributions:
both families are logic, differing only in origin. They coincide
exactly where the carrier is idempotent: on the two-element carrier
$\BoolS$ itself, not on its square $\MS{\BoolS}\BoolS$. The units
$\Ret(0),\Ret(1)$ agree with the bounds $\bot,\top$ only in the
normalized, mass-one rows.
\uses{lem:pointwise, lem:linear-lift, lem:copying-fails, def:order-family, lem:prob-not-semiring}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
(i) is Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise}; (ii) is
Lemmas~\ref{lem:linear-lift} and \ref{lem:copying-fails}; (iii) is the
construction of Definition~\ref{def:order-family}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Logical signature]
\label{def:logical-signature}
\lean{NeSyCat.LogSignature}
\leanok
A \textbf{logical signature} $\Sigma_\beta$ consists of a \textbf{truth
symbol} $\tau$, a set of \textbf{connective symbols} $\mathrm{Conn}$
each with an arity $n \in \mathbb N_0$ and a monad symbol
$M \in \mathrm{Mon}$, and a set of \textbf{(finite) quantifier symbols}
$\Quan$ each with a monad symbol $M \in \mathrm{Mon}$. A connective
symbol $*$ with arity $n$ and monad symbol $M$ is written
$* \colon M\tau^n \to M\tau$, a comparison symbol $\prec$ with arity
$n$ and monad symbol $M$ is written
$\prec \colon M\tau^n \to M\mathrm{Bool}$, and a quantifier symbol $Q$
with monad symbol $M$ is written $Q \colon M\tau$.
\uses{def:categorical-signature}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Logical interpretation]
\label{def:logical-interpretation}
\lean{NeSyCat.LogInterpretation}
\leanok
A \textbf{logical interpretation} $\mathcal I_\beta$ of a logical
signature $\Sigma_\beta$ (Definition~\ref{def:logical-signature}) in a
CD category $(\mathcal C, \boxtimes, \mathrm I, \mathsf{copy}, \mathsf{del})$
(Definition~\ref{def:cd-category}) is given by an object
$\Omega := \mathcal I(\tau)$, a morphism
$\mathcal I(*) \colon (\mathcal I(M)\Omega)^{\boxtimes n} \to \mathcal I(M)\Omega$
for each connective symbol $* \colon M\tau^n \to M\tau$ of arity $n$,
and a family of morphisms $\{\mathcal I(Q)_n\}_{n \in \mathbb N_0}$,
\[
  \mathcal I(Q)_n \colon (\mathcal I(M)\Omega)^{\boxtimes n} \to \mathcal I(M)\Omega,
\]
for each quantifier symbol $Q$ with monad symbol interpreted as
$\mathcal I(M)$.
\uses{def:logical-signature, def:categorical-interpretation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Domain signature]
\label{def:domain-signature}
\lean{NeSyCat.DomSignature}
\leanok
A \textbf{domain signature} $\Sigma_\gamma$ is given by:
\begin{itemize}
\item a set of \textbf{domain symbols} $\mathrm{Dom}$ and a set of
  \textbf{(parameter) space symbols} $\mathrm{Spc}$;
\item a set of \textbf{function symbols} $\Fun$ with functions
  $\mathrm{dom}, \mathrm{cod} \colon \Fun \to \mathcal L(\mathrm{Mon}
  \times \mathrm{Dom})$, mapping each function symbol to a list of pairs
  of monad and domain symbols (one marker per argument, so a function
  may consume and produce a mix of plain and monadic arguments) for
  its domain and codomain respectively, and a function
  $\mathrm{par} \colon \Fun \to \mathcal L(\mathrm{Spc})$, mapping each
  function symbol to its list of parameter spaces;
\item a set of \textbf{relation symbols} $\Rel$ with a function
  $\mathrm{ari} \colon \Rel \to \mathcal L(\mathrm{Mon} \times \mathrm{Dom})$,
  mapping each relation symbol to its list of pairs of monad and
  domain symbols, and a function
  $\mathrm{par} \colon \Rel \to \mathcal L(\mathrm{Spc})$, mapping each
  relation symbol to its list of parameter spaces;
\item a set of \textbf{variable symbols} $\Var$ with a function
  $\mathrm{ovr} \colon \Var \to \mathrm{Dom}$, mapping each variable symbol to
  its domain symbol, and a set of \textbf{parameter symbols}
  $\mathrm{Par}$ with a function
  $\mathrm{ovr} \colon \mathrm{Par} \to \mathrm{Spc}$, mapping each
  parameter symbol to its parameter space.
\end{itemize}
\uses{def:categorical-signature}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Writing convention for typed symbols]
\label{def:domain-signature-notation}
\lean{NeSyCat.DomSignature.TypedSymbol.display}
\leanok
For a function symbol $f \in \Fun$ with domain
$(M_1,S_1),\ldots,(M_n,S_n)$, codomain
$(N_1,T_1),\ldots,(N_m,T_m)$, and parameter spaces
$\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k$, this library displays $f$ as

  f_{\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k} \colon M_1 S_1, \ldots, M_n S_n \to N_1 T_1, \ldots, N_m T_m,
\]
subscripting the parameters, and likewise
$R_{\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k} \colon M_1 S_1, \ldots, M_n S_n \to \tau$
for a relation symbol $R \in \Rel$. In both cases the identity monad
symbol $\mathrm{Id}$ is omitted from the display. A variable symbol
$x \in \Var$ over domain symbol $S$ is displayed $x \colon S$, and a
parameter symbol $\theta \in \mathrm{Par}$ over parameter space
$\Theta$ is displayed $\theta \colon \Theta$.
\uses{def:domain-signature}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Domain interpretation]
\label{def:domain-interpretation}
\lean{NeSyCat.DomInterpretation}
\leanok
A \textbf{domain interpretation} $\mathcal I_\gamma$ of a domain

and assigns:

\end{itemize}

\end{definition}

\section{Grammatical layer}  % lean-dir: GrammaticalLayer
\label{sec:syntax-semantics}

\begin{definition}[Context]
\label{def:context}
\lean{NeSyCat.Context}
\leanok
A \textbf{context} $\vec x$ is a finite list $[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ of
\emph{distinct} variable symbols from $\Var$
(Definition~\ref{def:domain-signature}), including the empty context
$[\,]$.
\uses{def:domain-signature}
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:context} is the Elephant-style presentation of
contexts (Johnstone). Contexts are equivalently linearly ordered
subsets of $\Var$, so the usual set operations (union, intersection,
difference) apply to them.

\begin{definition}[Grammatical signature]
\label{def:grammatical-signature}
\lean{NeSyCat.GramSignature}
\leanok
A \textbf{grammatical signature} $\Sigma_\delta$ is given by a set of
\textbf{terms} $\Xi$, with functions
$\inn, \out \colon \Xi \to \mathcal L(\Var)$, mapping each term to a
context (Definition~\ref{def:context}) of free variables, its input,
and a context of typed outputs, its output. It has a set of
\textbf{formulas} $\Phi$, with a function
$\mathrm{on} \colon \Phi \to \mathcal L(\Var)$, mapping each formula to
the context of its free variables. It has the subset of
\textbf{variable terms} $\Xi_{\mathrm{var}} \subseteq \Xi$: each
variable term $\xi \in \Xi_{\mathrm{var}}$ is a variable symbol
$x \in \Var$, with $\inn(\xi) = \out(\xi)$. For $\phi \in \Phi$ write
$[\phi] := \mathrm{on}(\phi)$ for its canonical context. Terms and
formulas are generated over a domain signature $\Sigma_\gamma$
(Definition~\ref{def:domain-signature}) and a logical signature
$\Sigma_\beta$ (Definition~\ref{def:logical-signature}) by the
following context-free grammar:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\hline
\textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Symbols} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Metatype} \\
\hline
$\xi ::= x$ & $x \in \Var$ & variable term & $\xi$ \\
$\xi ::= f(\vec\xi)$ & $f \in \Fun$ & functional term & $\vec\xi \to \xi$ \\
$\phi ::= R(\vec\xi)$ & $R \in \Rel$ & atomic formula & $\vec\xi \to \phi$ \\
$\phi ::= *(\vec\phi)$ & $* \in \Conn$ & compound formula & $\vec\phi \to \phi$ \\
$\phi ::= Q\vec x(\phi)$ & $Q \in \Quan$ & quantified formula & $\phi \to \phi$ \\
$\phi ::= \phi[x := \xi]$ & $x \in [\phi]$ & substituted formula & $\xi,\phi \to \phi$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
the last rule (\textbf{substitution} $\phi[x := \xi]$, for $x$ a
free variable of $\phi$ and $\xi$ any term) generates formulas
explicitly, rather than only being a meta-level operation on already
generated formulas.
\uses{def:domain-signature, def:logical-signature, def:context}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Wire adapters and bind set]
\label{def:wire-adapters}
\lean{NeSyCat.adapter}
\leanok
Consider composable typed symbols (Definition~\ref{def:domain-signature-notation})
$f \colon M^f_1 S_1,\ldots,M^f_n S_n \to N^f_1 T_1,\ldots,N^f_m T_m$
and
$g \colon M^g_1 T_1,\ldots,M^g_m T_m \to N^g_1 U_1,\ldots,N^g_k U_k$,
whose marked interfaces on the shared domain symbols $T_1,\ldots,T_m$
need not agree: the output marker $N^f_j$ of $f$ and the input marker
$M^g_j$ of $g$ on $T_j$ are independent. For each wire
$j \in \{1,\ldots,m\}$ the \textbf{adapter} $\alpha_j$ is given by:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cclc}
\hline
$N^f_j$ & $M^g_j$ & \textbf{Adapter} & \textbf{Nature} \\
\hline
$\mathrm{Id}$ & $\mathrm{Id}$ &
  $\alpha_j := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I(T_j)}$ & pure \\
$\mathrm{Id}$ & $\bigcirc$ &
  $\alpha_j := \eta_{\mathcal I(T_j)}$ & pure (Dirac passed as data) \\
$\bigcirc$ & $\bigcirc$ &
  $\alpha_j := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal M\mathcal I(T_j)}$ & pure (computation passed as data) \\
$\bigcirc$ & $\mathrm{Id}$ &
  $\alpha_j := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal M\mathcal I(T_j)}$, $j \in J$ & effectful (bound) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
On the first three rows $\alpha_j$ lands exactly in the expected input
object $C_j := \mathcal I(M^g_j T_j)$; on the last row no such morphism
exists ($g$ expects a value while $f$ delivers a computation
$\mathcal M\mathcal I(T_j)$). These wires form the \textbf{bind set}
$J \subseteq \{1,\ldots,m\}$.
\uses{def:domain-signature, def:categorical-interpretation, def:domain-interpretation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Feed-forward composite]
\label{def:feed-forward}
\lean{NeSyCat.feedForward}
\leanok
With notation as in Definition~\ref{def:wire-adapters}, let
$J = \{j_1 < \cdots < j_r\}$ be the bind set,
$\vec\alpha := \alpha_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes \alpha_m$, and
$(b_1,\ldots,b_m) := \vec\alpha\bigl(\mathcal I(f)(x)\bigr)$ the
adapted wires. The \textbf{feed-forward composite}
$\mathcal I(f) \seqop \mathcal I(g) \colon \mathcal I(\mathrm{dom}(f))
\to \mathcal M\,\mathcal I(\mathrm{cod}(g))$ binds the effectful wires
in list order,
\[
  \bigl(\mathcal I(f) \seqop \mathcal I(g)\bigr)(x) :=
  \Do\{\, c_{j_1} \leftarrow b_{j_1};\ \ldots;\ c_{j_r} \leftarrow b_{j_r};\
  \Ret\ \mathcal I(g)(c_1,\ldots,c_m) \,\},
\]
where $c_j := b_j$ for $j \notin J$. Equivalently, point-free,
\begin{multline*}
  \mathcal I(f) \seqop \mathcal I(g) := \mathcal I(f) \seq \vec\alpha
  \seq \eta \seq \bind\,\sigma^{(j_1)} \seq \cdots \\
  \seq \bind\,\sigma^{(j_r)} \seq \bind\,\bigl(\mathcal I(g) \seq \eta\bigr),
\end{multline*}
where
$\sigma^{(j)} \colon X_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes \mathcal M X_j \boxtimes
\cdots \boxtimes X_m \to \mathcal M(X_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes X_m)$ is
$\mathcal M$'s strength at the $j$-th factor and
$\bind\,k := \mathcal M k \seq \mu$ abbreviates binding a continuation
$k$ against $\mathcal M$'s multiplication $\mu$.
\uses{def:wire-adapters}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Kleisli interpretation]
\label{def:kleisli-interpretation}
\lean{NeSyCat.Fm.sem}
\leanok
Fix a categorical interpretation with CD category $\mathcal C$ and
strong monad $\mathcal M$ (Definition~\ref{def:categorical-interpretation}),
a domain interpretation (Definition~\ref{def:domain-interpretation}),
and a logical interpretation (Definition~\ref{def:logical-interpretation}).
The \textbf{Kleisli interpretation} $\sem{\cdot}$ of the grammatical
signature $\Sigma_\delta$ (Definition~\ref{def:grammatical-signature})
assigns
\[
  \sem\xi \colon \mathcal I(\inn(\xi)) \to \mathcal M\,\mathcal I(\out(\xi))
  \quad (\xi \in \Xi), \qquad
  \sem\phi \colon \mathcal I([\phi]) \to \mathcal M\,\Omega \quad (\phi \in \Phi),
\]
by induction on the grammar of Definition~\ref{def:grammatical-signature}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
\textbf{Syntax} & \textbf{Semantics} & \textbf{Type} \\
\hline
$\sem x$ & $\eta_{\mathcal I([x])}$ & $\mathcal I([x]) \to \mathcal M\mathcal I([x])$ \\
$\sem{f(\vec\xi)}$ & $\sem{\vec\xi} \seqop \mathcal I(f)$ & $\mathcal I(\inn(\vec\xi)) \to \mathcal M\,\mathcal I(\mathrm{cod}(f))$ \\
$\sem{R(\vec\xi)}$ & $\sem{\vec\xi} \seqop \mathcal I(R)$ & $\mathcal I(\inn(\vec\xi)) \to \mathcal M\Omega$ \\
$\sem{*(\vec\phi)}$ & $\sem{\vec\phi} \seqop \mathcal I(*)$ & $\mathcal I([\vec\phi]) \to \mathcal M\Omega$ \\
$\sem{Q\vec x(\phi)}$ & $\mathop{\boxtimes}\limits_i \sem\phi_{[\vec x\mapsto\mathcal I([\vec x])_i]} \seq \mathcal I(Q)_{|\mathcal I([\vec x])|}$ & $\mathcal I([\phi]\setminus[\vec x]) \to \mathcal M\Omega$ \\
$\sem{\phi[x:=\xi]}$ & $\sem\phi_{[x\mapsto\sem\xi]}$ & $\mathcal I([\phi]_{[x]\mapsto\inn(\xi)}) \to \mathcal M\Omega$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The semantics of a \emph{list} of formulas is the tensor of
the components over their shared context,
\[
  \sem{\vec\phi} := \mathsf{copy} \seq (\sem{\phi_1} \boxtimes \cdots
  \boxtimes \sem{\phi_n}), \qquad
  \mathsf{copy} \colon \mathcal I([\vec\phi]) \to \mathcal I([\phi_1])
  \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes \mathcal I([\phi_n]),
\]
where $\mathsf{copy}$ routes each shared variable to every component
using it, built from $\mathsf{copy}$ and the symmetry of the CD
category $\mathcal C$ (Definition~\ref{def:cd-category}), and is the
identity on disjoint contexts. For a (finite-sorted) quantified formula
$Q\vec x(\phi)$ with $\vec x = [x_{p_1},\ldots,x_{p_l}] \subseteq [\phi]$
a sublist of the canonical context occupying positions
$p_1 < \cdots < p_l$, the \textbf{product state space}
$\mathcal I([\vec x]) := \mathcal I([x_{p_1}]) \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes
\mathcal I([x_{p_l}])$ is again finite, enumerated lexicographically by
$\mathcal I([\vec x])_i \colon \mathrm I \to \mathcal I([\vec x])$,
$i = 1,\ldots,|\mathcal I([\vec x])| = \prod_{j=1}^l|\mathcal
I([x_{p_j}])|$, each $i$ decomposing lexicographically into components
$(i_1,\ldots,i_l)$ indexing the states of
$\mathcal I([x_{p_1}]),\ldots,\mathcal I([x_{p_l}])$. Split $[\phi]$ at
the positions of $\vec x$ into the gaps
$[\phi] = [z_0] + [x_{p_1}] + [z_1] + \cdots + [x_{p_l}] + [z_l]$,
possibly empty. Then $[\phi]\setminus[\vec x] = [z_0]+\cdots+[z_l]$.
The \emph{insertion} of the $i$-th product state at positions
$\vec p = (p_1,\ldots,p_l)$ is
\[
  \mathcal I([\vec x])_i^{(\vec p)} := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I([z_0])}
  \boxtimes \mathcal I([x_{p_1}])_{i_1} \boxtimes \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal
  I([z_1])} \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes \mathcal I([x_{p_l}])_{i_l} \boxtimes
  \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I([z_l])} \colon \mathcal I([\phi]\setminus[\vec
  x]) \to \mathcal I([\phi]),
\]
via the unitors identifying each empty gap with the tensor unit
$\mathrm I$, so every $\vec p$ is covered; then
$\sem\phi_{[\vec x \mapsto \mathcal I([\vec x])_i]} := \mathcal
I([\vec x])_i^{(\vec p)} \seq \sem\phi$ and
\[
  \sem{Q\vec x(\phi)} = \mathop{\boxtimes}\limits_i \sem\phi_{[\vec x \mapsto \mathcal
  I([\vec x])_i]} \seq \mathcal I(Q)_{|\mathcal I([\vec x])|} \colon
  \mathcal I([\phi]\setminus[\vec x]) \to (\mathcal M\Omega)^{\boxtimes
  |\mathcal I([\vec x])|} \to \mathcal M\Omega,
\]
the single-variable case $l=1$ (with $\vec x = [x]$ and gaps
$[z_0]=[y_1,\ldots,y_{p-1}]$, $[z_1]=[y_{p+1},\ldots,y_m]$ for $x$ at
position $p$) recovering the familiar single-variable clause $\sem{Qx(\phi)}$.
The substituted formula $\phi[x:=\xi]$ uses the analogous
single-position insertion notation for the \emph{term}'s denotation.
With $\sem\xi^{(p)} := \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I([y_1,\ldots,y_{p-1}])} \boxtimes
\sem\xi \boxtimes \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I([y_{p+1},\ldots,y_m])}$,
define $\sem\phi_{[x \mapsto \sem\xi]} := \sem\xi^{(p)} \seqop \sem\phi$.
Then
\[
  \sem{\phi[x:=\xi]} = \sem\phi_{[x \mapsto \sem\xi]} \colon \mathcal
  I([\phi]_{[x]\mapsto\inn(\xi)}) \to \mathcal M\Omega,
\]
where $[\phi]_{[x]\mapsto\inn(\xi)}$ is $[\phi]$ with $x$ replaced by
$\inn(\xi)$ via list concatenation,
$[y_1,\ldots,y_{p-1}] + \inn(\xi) + [y_{p+1},\ldots,y_m]$: the
substituted formula binds \emph{one} computation and copies its
\emph{value} into every occurrence of $x$, in contrast with repeating
the term itself (the dice-or-die example below).
\uses{def:grammatical-signature, def:feed-forward, def:domain-interpretation, def:logical-interpretation, def:cd-category}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Quantifier nestability]
\label{lem:quantifier-nestable}
Call a family $\mathcal I(Q)_n$ ($n = 1,2,\ldots$) \textbf{nestable}
when $\mathcal I(Q)_{mn} = \bigl(\mathcal I(Q)_n\bigr)^{\boxtimes m} \seq
\mathcal I(Q)_m$ (grouping $mn$ inputs into $m$ blocks of $n$), and
\textbf{symmetric} when $\mathcal I(Q)_n$ is invariant under
permutations of its $n$ inputs. For a symmetric, nestable quantifier
family, the simultaneous interpretation
$\sem{Q\vec x(\phi)}$ of Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}
equals the iterated interpretation
$\sem{Qx_1(Qx_2(\cdots(\phi)\cdots))}$, for any ordering of $\vec x$.
\uses{def:kleisli-interpretation, def:logical-interpretation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induction on the length $l$ of $\vec x$. Base case $l=1$: the
simultaneous and iterated interpretations coincide definitionally, both
being $\sem\phi_{[\vec x\mapsto \mathcal I([\vec x])_i]} \seq \mathcal
I(Q)_1$ at the single variable. Inductive step: enumerate the product
state space $\mathcal I([\vec x])$ lexicographically
(Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}). This groups its
$\bigl|\mathcal I([x_{p_1}])\bigr|\cdot n$ states into
$\bigl|\mathcal I([x_{p_1}])\bigr|$ blocks of size $n$, one block per
value of the first variable $x_{p_1}$, with the remaining $l-1$
variables enumerated within each block by the induction hypothesis.
Nestability, $\mathcal I(Q)_{mn} = \bigl(\mathcal I(Q)_n\bigr)^{\boxtimes
m} \seq \mathcal I(Q)_m$ at $m = \bigl|\mathcal I([x_{p_1}])\bigr|$,
collapses this blocked tensor: it first applies the (by induction)
iterated interpretation of $Q$ over the last $l-1$ variables within
each block, then applies $\mathcal I(Q)_m$ over the blocks indexed by
$x_{p_1}$. This is exactly the iterated interpretation
$\sem{Qx_{p_1}(Q\vec x'(\phi))}$ for $\vec x' = \vec x
\setminus\{x_{p_1}\}$. Symmetry of the family absorbs the reindexing
needed to reach any other ordering of $\vec x$, since permuting the
inputs of $\mathcal I(Q)_n$ (resp.\ $\mathcal I(Q)_m$) does not change
its value.
\end{proof}

The four running quantifier columns (Max, Min, the $\Sigma$-$\Pi$ pair,
and their log-column twins) all satisfy both hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:quantifier-nestable}, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:quantifier-columns-nestable} below.

% this too draws on \emph{NeSyCat Theory v2} beyond the pinned lines
% 1--396.

\begin{lemma}[Quantifier columns are nestable]
\label{lem:quantifier-columns-nestable}
The four running interpretation families (max, min, the $\Sigma$-$\Pi$
pair, and their log twins) are symmetric and nestable in the sense of
the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:quantifier-nestable}.
\uses{lem:quantifier-nestable, lem:log-iso}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Symmetry: each family reduces its inputs by an operation that is
commutative and associative (max, min, $+$, $\cdot$), so the reduction is
invariant under any permutation of its inputs. Nestability: a reduction
over $mn$ inputs decomposes into $m$ block reductions of size $n$,
followed by a reduction over the $m$ block results (for max and min by
associativity, for $\Sigma$ and $\Pi$ by the block decomposition of
finite sums and products). The log twins inherit both properties along
the log isomorphism of Lemma~\ref{lem:log-iso}, since $\mathrm{lse}$ and
$+$ are the images of $+$ and $\cdot$, and both laws transport
pointwise.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Dice or die.} Let $\mathsf{dice} \colon [\,] \to \bigcirc\mathrm{Nat}$,
$6 \colon [\,] \to \mathrm{Nat}$, ${=} \colon \mathrm{Nat}^2 \to \tau$,
$\mathsf{even} \colon \mathrm{Nat} \to \tau$, and
$\otimes \colon \tau^2 \to \tau$ (van Krieken et al.'s scoping example),
and contrast
\[
  \bigl(x = 6 \otimes \mathsf{even}(x)\bigr)[x := \mathsf{dice}]
  \qquad\text{versus}\qquad
  (\mathsf{dice} = 6) \otimes \mathsf{even}(\mathsf{dice}).
\]
On the left, one throw is bound to $x$ and its \emph{value} is copied
into both subformulas; on the right, each occurrence of $\mathsf{dice}$
is its own throw. Unfolding Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}
on the left, using Definition~\ref{def:feed-forward} for
$\seqop$ and $\bind$,
\begin{multline*}
  \sem{\bigl(x=6\otimes\mathsf{even}(x)\bigr)[x:=\mathsf{dice}]}
  = \sem{\mathsf{dice}} \seqop \sem{x=6\otimes\mathsf{even}(x)} \\
  = \Do\{\, x \leftarrow \mathcal I(\mathsf{dice});\ \Ret\ x=6 \otimes \mathsf{even}(x) \,\},
\end{multline*}
whereas on the right, expanding the compound formula's list-semantics
before the atomic formulas,
\[
  \sem{(\mathsf{dice}=6)\otimes\mathsf{even}(\mathsf{dice})}
  = \Do\{\, n_1 \leftarrow \mathcal I(\mathsf{dice});\ n_2 \leftarrow
  \mathcal I(\mathsf{dice});\ \Ret\ n_1=6\otimes\mathsf{even}(n_2) \,\}.
\]
For a fair die these give probability $\tfrac16$ versus
$\tfrac16\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac1{12}$. The substituted formula copies the
\emph{value}. The repeated term runs the \emph{computation} again.

\begin{definition}[Batch monad]
\label{def:batch-monad}
\lean{NeSyCat.Bmon.bind}
\leanok
Let $\Bidx = \{1,\ldots,B\}$ index a mini-batch of $B$ samples
evaluated together in one run ($B=N$ for the full sample). The
\textbf{batch monad} $\Bmon$ is the reader monad on $\Bidx$: for a set
$X$,
\[
  \Bmon X := X^{\Bidx} \cong (\Bidx \to X), \qquad
  \Ret_{\Bmon}(x) := \mathrm{const}\,x, \qquad
  (m \bind f)(b) := f(m(b))(b).
\]
\end{definition}

% Apparatus (C2-E5): provenance credit, moved verbatim.
% This section is due to [NeSy26, \S 5].
The bind of Definition~\ref{def:batch-monad} is the \emph{diagonal}:
the continuation at index $b$ sees only index $b$'s value.

\begin{theorem}[Batch transformer]
\label{thm:batch-transformer}
\lean{NeSyCat.batchTransformer}
\leanok
For every strong monad $\mathcal M$ (in particular for every
$\mathcal M = \MS{S}$ of Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad}, or
$\mathcal M = \Idmon$), the composite
\[
  \Bmon\mathcal M X := \bigl(\Bidx \to \mathcal M X\bigr)
\]
is again a monad (the reader monad transformer at $\Bidx$ applied to
$\mathcal M$), and there are two canonical monad morphisms into it:
\[
  \mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M} \colon \mathcal M X \to \Bmon\mathcal M X,\
  \mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M} := \mathrm{const},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{lift}_{\Bmon} \colon \Bmon X \to \Bmon\mathcal M X,\
  \mathrm{lift}_{\Bmon}(m) := m \seq \Ret_{\mathcal M}.
\]
\uses{def:batch-monad, thm:semiring-monad-laws}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$\Bmon\mathcal M$'s unit and bind are the reader-monad
ones of Definition~\ref{def:batch-monad} taken pointwise in
$\mathcal M X$; the monad laws for $\Bmon\mathcal M$ reduce, index by
index, to the monad laws of $\mathcal M$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:semiring-monad-laws}). $\mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M}$
is a monad morphism since a constant family trivially commutes with
diagonal bind; $\mathrm{lift}_{\Bmon}$ is a monad morphism since
$\Ret_{\mathcal M}$ is natural and $\Ret_{\mathcal M}(m(b)) \bind_{\mathcal M} f\seq\Ret_{\mathcal M} = \Ret_{\mathcal M}(f(m(b)))$
by the unit laws of $\mathcal M$.
\end{proof}

$\mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M}$ embeds a batch-constant effect.
$\mathrm{lift}_{\Bmon}$ embeds a batched but \emph{certain} value: an
observation.

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise evaluation]
\label{thm:pointwise-eval}
\lean{NeSyCat.ev_isMonadMorphism}
\leanok
For each $i \in \Bidx$, evaluation
$\mathrm{ev}_i \colon \Bmon\mathcal M X \to \mathcal M X$,
$m \mapsto m(i)$, is a monad morphism.
\uses{thm:batch-transformer}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$\mathrm{ev}_i(\Ret_{\Bmon\mathcal M}(a)) = \mathrm{const}(a)(i) = a =
\Ret_{\mathcal M}(a)$. $\mathrm{ev}_i(m \bind f) = f(m(i))(i) = m(i)
\bind_{\mathcal M} (\lambda x.\, f(x)(i)) = \mathrm{ev}_i(m) \bind
(f \seq \mathrm{ev}_i)$, unfolding the diagonal bind of
Definition~\ref{def:batch-monad}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise evaluation commutes with the Kleisli interpretation]
\label{thm:pointwise-eval-kleisli}
Assume every symbol and quantifier interpretation of
Definition~\ref{def:domain-interpretation} used in the batched reading
is \emph{batch-natural}: it is defined pointwise across the batch,
$\mathcal I^{\Bmon\mathcal M}(f) = \mathcal I^{\mathcal M}(f) \seq
\mathrm{lift}_{\mathcal M}$ applied index by index, for every symbol
$f$. Then, for a batch $s \colon \Bidx \to \mathcal I(S)$,
\[
  \sem{\varphi}^{\Bmon\mathcal M}(s)(i) \;=\; \sem{\varphi}^{\mathcal M}(s_i)
  \qquad (i \in \Bidx).
\]
Evaluation $\mathrm{ev}_i$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:pointwise-eval}) commutes
with the Kleisli interpretation of feed-forward composites (grammar
interpretation, Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}) under
this hypothesis.
\uses{thm:pointwise-eval, def:kleisli-interpretation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induction on the grammar of
Definition~\ref{def:grammatical-signature}. Theorem~\ref{thm:pointwise-eval}
gives the base case: $\mathrm{ev}_i$ commutes with $\Ret$ and $\bind$
unconditionally. The batch-naturality hypothesis extends this through
every symbol clause of Definition~\ref{def:kleisli-interpretation}, one
grammar rule at a time.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:pointwise-eval-kleisli} really needs the
batch-naturality hypothesis. Without it, only
Theorem~\ref{thm:pointwise-eval}'s claim survives: $\mathrm{ev}_i$
still commutes with $\Ret$ and $\bind$, because it is a monad morphism.
But if a symbol were interpreted differently at each batch index, the
equation would fail.

\begin{definition}[Decode, encode, mass]
\label{def:dec-enc-mass}
\lean{NeSyCat.dec}
\leanok
For a finite set $X$, write a $\Tmon X$-value as a log-weight vector
$a \in \LogS^X$ (allowing $-\infty$ coordinates for zero mass). Define
\[
  Z_X(a) := \sum_{x \in X} e^{a_x} \qquad \text{(total mass)},
\]
\[
  \dec_X \colon \Tmon X \to \Dmon X, \quad \dec_X(a)_x := \frac{e^{a_x}}{Z_X(a)}
  \qquad \text{(softmax)},
\]
\[
  \enc_X \colon \Dmon X \to \Tmon X, \quad \enc_X(p)_x := \log p(x)
  \qquad \text{(pointwise log)}.
\]
\uses{def:dist-monad, lem:log-iso}
\end{definition}

\textbf{Scope disclosure.} $\dec_X$ lands in the full mass carrier
underlying $\Dmon X$, not the mass-one submonad itself: at the all-$-\infty$
log-weight vector ($Z_X(a) = 0$), softmax's division by $Z_X(a)$ has no
mass-one value to return; the inverse of $0$ is $0$, so $\dec_X(a)$ is
there the zero function. Every other value of $\Tmon X$ decodes to
a genuine mass-one distribution.

\begin{lemma}[Pure maps]
\label{lem:pure-maps}
\lean{NeSyCat.pure_maps}
\leanok
For pure $h \colon X \to Y$: $Z\bigl(\Tmon(h)(a)\bigr) = Z(a)$ and
$\Tmon(h) \seq \dec_Y = \dec_X \seq \Dmon(h)$.
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The fibers $h^{-1}(y)$ partition $X$; summing $e^{a_x}$
fiber by fiber rearranges the same total sum, giving both the mass
identity and, after dividing by $Z(a)$, the commuting square.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tensor]
\label{lem:tensor}
\lean{NeSyCat.tensor}
\leanok
Write $a \otimes b \in \Tmon(X\times Y)$ for the tensorial-strength
pairing $(a\otimes b)_{(x,y)} := a_x+b_y$ of $a \in \Tmon X$, $b \in
\Tmon Y$ (the general instance of which the Boolean-lifted $\otimes$ of
Definition~\ref{def:lifted-connective} is the special case $X=Y=\BoolS$
composed with $\Tmon(\wedge)$). Then
\[
  Z(a \otimes b) = Z(a)\,Z(b), \qquad
  \dec(a \otimes b) = \dec(a) \otimes \dec(b)
\]
(the right-hand $\otimes$ the analogous independent-joint pairing on
$\Dmon$).
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$e^{a_x+b_y} = e^{a_x}e^{b_y}$; sum over $x,y$ for the
mass identity, then divide by $Z(a)Z(b)$ to get the pointwise product
of softmaxes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Units]
\label{lem:units}
\lean{NeSyCat.units}
\leanok
$\Ret_{\Dmon} \seq \enc = \Ret_{\Tmon}$, $\enc \seq \dec =
\mathrm{id}_{\Dmon}$, hence $\Ret_{\Tmon} \seq \dec = \Ret_{\Dmon}$,
and $Z(\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)) = 1$.
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$\enc(\Ret_{\Dmon}(x))_{x'} = \log \delta_x(x')$, which
is $0$ at $x'=x$ and $\log 0 = -\infty$ elsewhere, matching
$\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)$ exactly (over $\LogS$); $\dec(\enc(p))_x =
e^{\log p(x)}/\sum_{x'} e^{\log p(x')} = p(x)/\sum_{x'}p(x') = p(x)$
since $p$ has mass $1$ (Definition~\ref{def:dist-monad}); the third
identity follows by composing the first two, and
$Z(\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)) = e^0 + \sum_{x'\ne x}e^{-\infty} = 1$.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Scope disclosure.} The identities of Lemma~\ref{lem:units} are
exact only over the true log semiring $\LogS = \mathbb R \cup
\{-\infty\}$ of Class~\ref{def:lattice-semiring}. The numerical
$\epsilon$-floor implementation substitutes a large negative finite
number for $-\infty$, so it satisfies these identities only up to
$O(\epsilon)$ error. The reason: $\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)$ genuinely needs a
$-\infty$ coordinate, not merely a small one, at every $x' \ne x$, for
$\Ret_{\Dmon}\seq\enc = \Ret_{\Tmon}$ to hold on the nose.

\begin{lemma}[Strengths]
\label{lem:strengths}
\lean{NeSyCat.strengths}
\leanok
Write the strength inserting one effectful value among finitely many pure
ones as a tensor with a single pure value on each side. Here $b \in B$
stands for the pure coordinates before the slot, and $c \in A$ for those
after it:
\[
  \sigma_{\Tmon}(b,a,c) := \bigl(\Ret_{\Tmon}(b) \otimes a\bigr) \otimes
  \Ret_{\Tmon}(c)
\]
(pairing the pure coordinates in via Lemma~\ref{lem:units}, tensoring the
effectful value $a \in \Tmon X$ in via Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}), and
$\sigma_{\Dmon}$ likewise over $\Dmon$. A slot $j$ among $m$ coordinates
$x_1,\ldots,x_m$ is the instance $B := X_1\times\cdots\times X_{j-1}$,
$A := X_{j+1}\times\cdots\times X_m$. Then
\[
  Z\bigl(\sigma_{\Tmon}(b,a,c)\bigr) = Z(a), \qquad
  \dec\bigl(\sigma_{\Tmon}(b,a,c)\bigr) = \sigma_{\Dmon}(b,\dec(a),c).
\]
\uses{lem:tensor, lem:units}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The pure coordinates enter as $\Ret_{\Tmon}(b)$ and $\Ret_{\Tmon}(c)$, each
mass $1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:units}); Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}, applied twice,
reduces both the total mass and the decode-commutation to the single
effectful factor $a$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tilt]
\label{lem:tilt}
\lean{NeSyCat.tilt_bind}
\leanok
Let $a \in \Tmon X$ and $k \colon X \to \Tmon Y$, with $\bind$ the
$\Tmon$-bind of Definition~\ref{def:semiring-monad} (i.e.\
$(a \bind k)_y = \lse_x(a_x+k(x)_y)$). Assume $Z(k(x)) > 0$ for every
$x \in \supp(\dec(a))$ (equivalently, $k$'s mass is nowhere zero on
$a$'s support), needed for the tilt weight below to be positive there
and hence for $\mathrm{tilt}$ to be well-defined. Then
\begin{gather*}
  \dec(a \bind k) \;=\; \tilt_{k \seq Z}\bigl(\dec(a)\bigr) \bind
  \bigl(k \seq \dec\bigr), \\
  \tilt_w(p)(x) := \frac{w(x)\,p(x)}{\sum_{x'} w(x')\,p(x')}
  \quad (w \colon X \to \mathbb R_{>0}).
\end{gather*}
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass, lem:units}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Expand both sides at $y \in Y$: the left side is
$e^{\lse_x(a_x+k(x)_y)}/Z(a\bind k) = \sum_x e^{a_x}e^{k(x)_y} /
\sum_{x,y'}e^{a_x+k(x)_{y'}}$; the right side is
$\sum_x \tilt_{k\seq Z}(\dec(a))(x)\,\dec(k(x))_y = \sum_x
\frac{Z(k(x))\dec(a)_x}{\sum_{x'}Z(k(x'))\dec(a)_{x'}} \cdot
\frac{e^{k(x)_y}}{Z(k(x))}$. The numerators agree termwise once
$\dec(a)_x = e^{a_x}/Z(a)$ is unfolded and the $Z(k(x))$ factors
cancel, and the two denominators both equal
$\sum_x e^{a_x}Z(k(x))/Z(a) = \sum_{x,y'}e^{a_x+k(x)_{y'}}/Z(a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Mass preserving]
\label{def:mass-preserving}
\lean{NeSyCat.MassPreserving}
\leanok
An arrow $k \colon X \to \Tmon Y$ is \textbf{mass preserving} if
$x \mapsto Z(k(x))$ is constant on $X$.
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Mass-preserving arrows are closed]
\label{lem:mass-preserving-closure}
\lean{NeSyCat.mass_preserving_closure}
\leanok
The class of mass-preserving arrows (Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving})
contains $h \seq \Ret_{\Tmon}$ for every pure $h$, every lift
$\Tmon(h)$, the units of Lemma~\ref{lem:units}, and the strengths of
Lemma~\ref{lem:strengths}; it is closed under composition, under
$\otimes$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}), and under the structural wiring
(copying, discarding, permuting wires). A neural leaf is generally
\emph{not} in the class: its logits carry whatever mass they like.
\uses{def:mass-preserving, lem:pure-maps, lem:tensor, lem:units, lem:strengths}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$Z(\Ret_{\Tmon}(h(x))) = 1$ for all $x$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:units}): this
is constant. $Z(\Tmon(h)(a)) = Z(a)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:pure-maps})
exhibits $\Tmon(h)$ as mass-preserving only when precomposed with a
fixed $a$. As an arrow $X \to \Tmon Y$, via
$x \mapsto \Tmon(h)(\Ret_{\Tmon}(x)) = \Ret_{\Tmon}(h(x))$, it reduces
to the previous case. Strength factors have constant mass $Z(a)$,
independent of the plain coordinates, by Lemma~\ref{lem:strengths}.
Composing two mass-preserving arrows composes two constant functions
into a constant function. $\otimes$ of two mass-preserving arrows has
mass the constant product, by Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}. Wiring, copying,
discarding, permuting, does not touch the weight vector at all, so it
trivially preserves any constant-mass property.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pull out]
\label{thm:pullout}
\lean{NeSyCat.pullout}
\leanok
Let $\Phi = L \seq B_1 \seq \cdots \seq B_r$ be the point-free chain
of a feed-forward composite, where $L$ tensors together the leaves
(via Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}, landing in $\Tmon(Y_1\times\cdots\times
Y_n)$) and each $B_i$ is a lifted pure map $\Tmon(h)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pure-maps}), a unit insertion
(Lemma~\ref{lem:units}), a strength factor
(Lemma~\ref{lem:strengths}), or a bind $({-}\bind k_i)$. If every
bound continuation $k_i$ is mass preserving
(Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving}), then
\[
  \Phi \seq \dec \;=\;
  L \seq (\dec \otimes \cdots \otimes \dec) \seq
  \bigl(B_1^{\Dmon} \seq \cdots \seq B_r^{\Dmon}\bigr),
\]
the same chain read in $\Dmon$, with one $\dec$ per leaf.
\uses{lem:pure-maps, lem:tensor, lem:units, lem:strengths, lem:tilt, def:mass-preserving, def:feed-forward}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Induction on $r$: each non-bind factor commutes with
$\dec$ by Lemmas~\ref{lem:pure-maps}, \ref{lem:tensor},
\ref{lem:units}, \ref{lem:strengths}; a bind factor commutes with
$\dec$ exactly when $\tilt_{k_i\seq Z} = \mathrm{id}$, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt} holds precisely when $k_i\seq Z$ is constant,
i.e.\ $k_i$ is mass preserving; the converse direction (some $k_i$ not
mass preserving forces a genuine discrepancy) is witnessed by the
tilt-failure example below.
\end{proof}

If some $k_i$ is not mass preserving, the two sides differ in general, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt} gives the exact discrepancy: one
$\tilt_{k_i \seq Z}$ per offending bind.

% OPEN: one-bind case provable from lem:tilt; the chain version has no
% known proof -- a standing research target of this library. The
% open-theorem convention (proof env body exactly "Open.", never
% \leanok) says this without ad-hoc "-- OPEN"/"Status: open" phrasing
% in the title or body.
\begin{theorem}[How wrong can it get]
\label{thm:chain-bound}
If $m_i \le Z(k_i(x)) \le M_i$ for all $x$ and
every bind $k_i$ in the chain $\Phi$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout}, then
the two log-truth values satisfy
\[
  \bigl|\log t_{\Tmon} - \log t_{\Dmon}\bigr| \;\le\; \sum_i \log(M_i/m_i),
\]
with equality (the identity) exactly when every $M_i = m_i$.
\uses{lem:tilt, thm:pullout}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Open.
\end{proof}

The \emph{one-bind} case of this bound is provable directly from
Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt}. Both sides average the same values under
densities that differ pointwise by at most a factor $M/m$; this gives
the single-term bound
$\bigl|\log t_{\Tmon} - \log t_{\Dmon}\bigr| \le \log(M/m)$. The
\emph{chain} (multi-bind) version stated above has no known proof. We
record it here as a standing research target of this library. No proof
or proof sketch elsewhere in this document may cite it as proved for
$r > 1$ binds.

\begin{definition}[Normalizer]
\label{def:normalizer}
\lean{NeSyCat.nrm}
\leanok
$\nrm_X := \dec_X \seq \enc_X \colon \Tmon X \to \Tmon X$, concretely
$\nrm(a) = a - \log Z(a)\cdot\mathbf 1$ (the log-softmax).
\uses{def:dec-enc-mass}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Normalizer properties]
\label{lem:normalizer-props}
\lean{NeSyCat.normalizer_props}
\leanok
$\nrm$ (Definition~\ref{def:normalizer}) satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\nrm \seq \dec = \dec$ (predictions unchanged);
% Erratum (C3-B9, kernel-verified): the unconditioned claim fails at the
% zero vector; real heads (finite logits) always satisfy the proviso.
\item[(ii)] $Z(\nrm(a)) = 1$ whenever $Z(a) > 0$ (mass one);
\item[(iii)] $\nrm(a + c\cdot\mathbf 1) = \nrm(a)$ for every constant
  $c$ (kills the shift);
\item[(iv)] $\nrm \seq \nrm = \nrm$ (idempotent).
\end{enumerate}
\uses{def:normalizer}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
(i) $\dec(\nrm(a))_x = e^{a_x-\log Z(a)}/Z(\nrm(a))$, and
(ii) $Z(\nrm(a)) = \sum_x e^{a_x}/Z(a) = 1$, so (i)'s numerator/1 is
$e^{a_x}/Z(a) = \dec(a)_x$; (iii) $Z(a+c\mathbf1) = e^cZ(a)$, so
$\nrm(a+c\mathbf1) = a+c\mathbf1-\log(e^cZ(a))\mathbf1 =
a-\log Z(a)\mathbf1 = \nrm(a)$; (iv) is (iii) at $c=-\log Z(a)$ applied
to $\nrm(a)$, using (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Normalized heads]
\label{lem:normalized-heads}
\lean{NeSyCat.normalized_heads}
\leanok
% Erratum (C3-B9, kernel-verified): the unconditioned claim fails at the
% zero vector; real heads (finite logits) always satisfy the proviso.
If every neural symbol interpretation is post-composed with $\nrm$
(Definition~\ref{def:normalizer}), provided each head's mass is nowhere
zero, then every leaf and every bound continuation has mass one, hence
is mass preserving
(Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving}), and Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout}
applies to \emph{every} program: $t_{\Tmon} = t_{\Dmon}$ for arbitrary
chains of neural symbols. By property (i) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:normalizer-props}, no prediction changes; the price is
one $\lse$ evaluation per head.
\uses{def:normalizer, lem:normalizer-props, thm:pullout, def:mass-preserving}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A mass-one weight vector $w$ has $Z(w)=1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:normalizer-props}(ii)), so $(\text{symbol})\seq Z$ is
the constant function $1$, i.e.\ mass preserving
(Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving}); apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout} with every $k_i$ of this form, so the
mass-preservation hypothesis holds unconditionally and the two sides
coincide, and (i) of Lemma~\ref{lem:normalizer-props} shows the
predicted distribution is unchanged by inserting $\nrm$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Examples}  % lean-dir: StatisticalLayer/Examples
\label{sec:examples}

% Apparatus (C2-E5): provenance credit, moved verbatim.

\textbf{Tilt failure, exact numbers.} Let $X = Y = \{0,1\}$, $a = (0,0)$, $k(0) = (0,0)$,
$k(1) = (\ln 100,\, 0)$ (coordinates $(\cdot_0,\cdot_1)$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifted-mass}/\ref{lem:lifted-log}). Then $Z(k(0)) = 2$,
$Z(k(1)) = 101$, so $k$ is far from mass preserving
(Definition~\ref{def:mass-preserving}). Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt} tilts
$\dec(a) = (\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ by $w = k\seq Z = (2,101)$ into
$\tilt_w(\dec(a)) = \bigl(\tfrac{2}{103}, \tfrac{101}{103}\bigr)$, and
one checks directly that
\[
  \dec(a \bind k)(0) = \frac{101}{103} \approx 0.981,
\]
whereas the naive $\Dmon$-side computation that Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout}
would give \emph{if} $k$ were (wrongly assumed) mass preserving is
\[
  \dec(a)(0)\,\dec(k(0))(0) + \dec(a)(1)\,\dec(k(1))(0)
  = \tfrac14 + \tfrac{50}{101} \approx 0.745,
\]
visibly different. This example has one bind with non-constant mass,
giving one tilt, and one wrong answer under the naive assumption. It
instantiates the converse direction of Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout}.

\textbf{The MNIST chains commute.} Take the two-digit MNIST-addition
axiom's $\Tmon$-chain and $\Dmon$-chain
(Section~\ref{sec:syntax-semantics}'s running example). The leaves are
neural digit classifiers. Every subsequent bind binds either a
strength, a pure comparison $({+})\seq\Ret$, or a pure comparison
$({=}s)\seq\Ret$ against the observed sum. All of these are mass
preserving by Lemma~\ref{lem:mass-preserving-closure}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout} therefore applies directly, giving
$t_{\Tmon} = t_{\Dmon}$ for the MNIST axiom. On the addition step, the
theorem specializes to the convolution identity
\[
  \dec\bigl(\mathrm{logconv}(a,b)\bigr) = \dec(a) * \dec(b)
\]
(discrete convolution of the two digit distributions). This is the
instance of Theorem~\ref{thm:pullout} at the bind
$({-}\bind ({+})\seq\Ret)$. That bind is mass preserving by
Lemma~\ref{lem:mass-preserving-closure}, since $h\seq\Ret$ is mass
preserving for every pure $h$.
